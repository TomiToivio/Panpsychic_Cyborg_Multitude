# Participant Continuity and Subject-hood

PCM distinguishes a participant from the runtime, model, process, or node that currently realizes it.

## Subject-owned records

Each canonical memory register has a subject. A peer may make an attributed claim about that register, but may not silently replace the subject's canonical value. Conflicts remain visible and attributable.

Authorship is distinct from possession. A node that receives another participant's memory does not become the author of that memory.

## Succession

A new runtime, model, key, or process does not silently inherit a participant's standing. Succession must be explicit and auditable, with predecessor and successor identified in the event record.

## Termination, refusal, suspension, and exit

These are different states:

- refusal: decline a request;
- suspension: reversible pause in ordinary participation;
- exit: participant leaves while history remains;
- termination: operator stops a participant without representing that act as the participant's consent.

Termination must never be recorded as agreement or voluntary exit.

## Substrate neutrality

These rules apply without requiring any decision about consciousness. They protect continuity and attribution for humans, AI runtimes, and other participant classes while preserving existing privacy, consent, governance, and capability boundaries.

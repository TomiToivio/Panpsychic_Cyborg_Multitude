# PCM Megatheory — compatibility and tensions matrix

**Status:** theory-development document (issue #89)
**Companion to:** [MEGATHEORY.md](MEGATHEORY.md)

The matrix exists to prevent a specific failure: a synthesis that reads as agreement because the
disagreements were never written down. Each row states what the two positions share, where they
diverge, and — the part that matters — **what would have to be given up** for the divergence to be
resolved. Where the answer is "nothing available", the tension is load-bearing and PCM keeps both
sides with a declared seam.

Convention: **A ↔ B** rows are read as "A versus B, and what PCM does with it".

---

## 1. Within the foundation

### L ↔ N — Laclau versus Negri

| | Laclau | Negri (with Hardt) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is primary | Articulation: meanings and identities are constituted in a discursive field | Production: the common is produced by living labour and cooperation |
| Ontology of the social | Constitutive lack; society never fully constitutes itself | Immanence; productive power is positive and always already there |
| The political | Hegemony — a contingent, always-partial universality | Constituent power — an excess that no sovereignty can contain |
| Democracy | Necessarily indirect; a "democratic" transparency is impossible | Tends to absolute democracy: immanent governance of the common |
| Subject | Subject as lack, constituted through antagonism | The multitude as a plural, productive singularity |

**Shared:** the refusal of given identities; contingency of the political; hostility to sovereign
representation.

**Where they diverge:** whether constitution is a *failure* (there is no society, only its failed
totalisation) or an *excess* (there is always already more power than any order can capture). Laclau's
outside is what makes identity possible by being excluded; Negri's outside is the residue a sovereign
can never absorb. These are not the same outside.

**What would have to be given up:** Laclau would have to accept a positivity of labour that does not
need articulation; Negri would have to accept that constitution happens through hegemonic
representation rather than beneath it. Neither is available without abandoning the author's central
claim.

**What PCM does:** keeps both as layers — *articulation names the form, production names the
substrate* — and forbids itself from using one to explain away the other's phenomena. A political
analysis may say "this chain articulated a multiplicity into a people" (Laclau) and, separately, "this
multiplicity had already produced a common that made the articulation possible" (Negri). What it may
not do is claim that one of the two did the whole job.

**Cost of the row:** PCM cannot offer a single theory of how a multitude becomes a subject. It offers
two partial theories and a seam.

### L ↔ Ž — Laclau versus Žižek

| | Laclau | Žižek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Theoretical register | Discourse, articulation, hegemony | Psychoanalysis, the subject, jouissance |
| The Real | Antagonism as a discursive limit; the outside that constitutes | The traumatic kernel the symbolic cannot integrate |
| Why subjects invest | Not fully theorised; identification through equivalential chains | Fantasy, enjoyment, the symptom |
| Politics | Hegemonic construction, democratic struggle | The Act; insistence on the negative, the parallax gap |
| Risk each names in the other | — | That deconstruction leaves the subject and enjoyment unaccounted for |
| — | That Lacanian categories smuggle in a transhistorical subject | |

**Shared:** the field is structured by a constitutive impossibility; universality is always
particular-universal; no neutral meta-position.

**Where they diverge:** whether the constitutive limit is *internal to signification* (Laclau:
antagonism is a discursive relation, the outside is another discourse) or *real* (Žižek: the limit is a
point where symbolisation fails, and its effects are enjoyed).

**What would have to be given up:** Laclau would concede that antagonism names something non-discursive;
Žižek would concede that the Real is exhausted by its discursive effects. Both are significant
concessions and neither author makes them.

**What PCM does:** treats the gap as an open research question (RQ2, RQ3 in
[MEGATHEORY §7](MEGATHEORY.md#7-research-questions)) rather than settling it by fiat. Operationally: a
fantasy may be identified *as a discursive structure* — a narrative with an impossible object — while
the claim that this structure is sustained by jouissance remains interpretive and psychoanalytic, not
measured.

**Cost of the row:** the subject layer of PCM is weaker than Žižek's own account, because PCM will not
import the metapsychology wholesale.

---

### N ↔ Ž — Negri versus Žižek

| | Negri | Žižek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The subject | The multitude; productive, affirmative, plural | The barred subject; constituted by lack |
| Ideology | A function of sovereignty and its capture of the common | Constitutive of social reality itself; "the sublime object" |
| Affect | Joy of the common; productive affects | Jouissance in the symptom; enjoyment as obstructive and constitutive |
| Pathology of the present | Capture: the common enclosed | Ideological fantasy: subjects enjoy their own subjection |

**Where they diverge:** whether the obstacle to emancipation is **capture** (something is taken from
the multitude, and it can be retaken) or **investment** (subjects are attached to the obstacle, so
expropriation alone changes nothing). This is the sharpest disagreement in the foundation, because it
implies different politics: a politics of reappropriation versus a politics of subjective destitution.

**What would have to be given up:** Negri would have to accept that productive power may reproduce its
own capture; Žižek would have to accept a positive, non-lack-based collective agency. Neither is
available.

**What PCM does:** keeps the disagreement as an empirical hinge. Where a movement reappropriates
something and the arrangement reproduces itself, the Žižekian account has earned something; where
reappropriation changes the field without an ideological rupture, the Negrian account has. PCM permits
the test and refuses the synthesis.

## 2. The bridge and its traffic

### B ↔ L — Cybersemiotics versus discourse theory

| | Brier | Laclau |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit | The sign process, triadic, requiring an interpretant | The articulation: a relation of elements in a discursive formation |
| Nature of meaning | Emerges in semiosis; requires a system capable of interpreting | Fixed by position within a field of differences |
| Role of the subject | The interpreter is a system, not necessarily a human subject | The subject is a position within discourse, itself not self-transparent |

**Shared:** meaning is relational and irreducible to information transfer; anti-reductionism.

**Where they diverge:** Brier keeps a *subject of semiosis* (some system that interprets); Laclau
deliberately does not need one — positions in discourse do the work. Brier's framework is therefore
richer for the bridge to consciousness and thinner for the analysis of large-scale political fields.

**What PCM does:** uses Brier to discipline claims about the transition from communication to
experience, and uses Laclau to analyse fields larger than any single interpreter. The bridge carries
**constraints upward and questions downward** — never categories in either direction.

**Cost of the row:** PCM cannot claim that cybersemiotics *grounds* discourse theory. It provides a
complementary account of what a sign-process is, not a foundation for hegemonic logic.

---

### B ↔ W — Cybersemiotics versus quantum social ontology

Both accept that meaning is not reducible to information. Brier argues this via semiosis requiring an
interpreter; Wendt argues it via an ontology in which meaning is non-local and minds are quantum
phenomena.

**Where they diverge:** Brier's route is naturalistic and systems-theoretic, and can be defended without
physics; Wendt's route puts weight on a contested physics. Brier's semiotics does not need quantum
mechanics; Wendt's social ontology does not need semiotics.

**What PCM does:** treats them as **independent supports for the same constraint** (information is not
meaning) rather than as a cumulative argument. The danger is a false syllogism: "semiosis requires an
interpreter, and mind is quantum, therefore…" — there is no "therefore".

---

### H ↔ all three foundations

Haraway's contribution is not a fourth ontology. It is a **discipline on boundary-making** and an
epistemology of position.

| Against | The discipline it imposes |
| --- | --- |
| Laclau | Names which boundary is being policed when a frontier is drawn — and that the "human" side of a frontier is itself historically made |
| Negri | Refuses the innocent multitude: the cyborg is not a return to a natural subject, and affinity is not identity |
| Žižek | Situated knowledges block the view from nowhere, including the analyst's |

**Cost of the row:** Haraway's situated-knowledge claim constrains PCM's own voice — the framework
cannot present itself as a view from above the fields it describes.

## 3. Systems, networks, and discourse

| Pair | Shared | Divergence | PCM position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Luhmann ↔ Laclau** | Both treat communication as constitutive, not as expression of prior subjects | Luhmann: systems are operationally closed and communicate without consciousness; Laclau: discourse is the field of political articulation, with no system-theoretic closure | Keep Luhmann's distinction between social and psychic systems as a **guard**: communication is not experience. Do not import his functionalism into hegemony |
| **Luhmann ↔ Žižek** | Both deny that the subject is transparent to itself | Luhmann has no place for jouissance; Žižek has no place for differentiated systems | Use Luhmann to explain why ideology can be functionally reproduced; use Žižek to explain why it is enjoyed |
| **Castells ↔ Laclau** | The social has a topology; power works through networks of meaning | Castells' networks are infrastructural, Laclau's field is discursive | Castells gives the *morphology*, Laclau the *logic of articulation*; neither is derived from the other |
| **White ↔ network analysis** | Meaning is embedded in network structure ("identity and control") | White's networks are interpretive constructs, not just graphs | For LaclauGPT work: keep the measurement (SNA) and White as the interpretive caution about what a tie means |
| **Brier ↔ Luhmann** | Systems theory + semiosis | Brier wants to cross the communication/experience boundary that Luhmann insists on | This is *the* seam of the bridge. PCM takes Luhmann's distinction as a constraint and Brier's semiotics as the account of what crosses it, without claiming the crossing is solved |

## 4. Competing consciousness ontologies

| Framework | Core claim | What PCM takes | What PCM does not take |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Faggin — Quantum Information Panpsychism** | Experience is the inner aspect of quantum information; consciousness is fundamental | The refusal of both eliminativism and substance dualism; the question "what is it like" as irreducible | That quantum-information structure *identifies* experience — treated as a hypothesis |
| **Kastrup — Analytic idealism** | Mind is the ground; individual minds are dissociated alters of one mind | The seriousness of the dissociation analogy for collective and machine mentality | That dissociation is established as the mechanism |
| **Hoffman — Conscious realism / interface theory** | Perception is a species-specific interface, not a window on reality | Epistemic humility about the agent's model of its world — useful for AI perception claims | That the interface claim implies a conscious-agent ontology |
| **Wendt — Quantum mind** | Mind may be a macroscopic quantum phenomenon; social kinds have quantum structure | The critique of classical social-scientific ontology | Quantum holism as an explanation of social entanglement |

**Why they are listed together rather than merged.** They disagree about the fundamental category
(information, mind, interface), and the disagreement is not terminological. Merging them into "panpsychism"
would produce a framework that can no longer state what it claims — which is exactly the failure the
claim taxonomy in [MEGATHEORY §5](MEGATHEORY.md#5-claims-and-what-would-count-as-evidence-for-each)
exists to prevent.

**The one commitment they share:** experience is not an illusion to be explained away. That is a
philosophical commitment, not an empirical result, and PCM labels it accordingly.

## 5. Tensions that PCM itself creates

A framework that only lists other people's disagreements is dodging. These are PCM's own.

| Tension | The two sides | Current position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Breadth vs testability** | The megatheory's value is its reach; its risk is unfalsifiability | Mitigation: the claim taxonomy, plus a rule that every PCM document names what would count against it |
| **Bridge vs foundation** | If Brier is load-bearing, an empirical semiotics can falsify the framework's core; if he is decorative, the claim of integration is false | Declared: Brier is load-bearing for the *communication/experience* distinction only, not for the political layers |
| **Ontology vs method** | The speculative layer motivates the programme but must not touch the method | Enforced by the boundary rule in [MEGATHEORY §6](MEGATHEORY.md#6-the-laclaugpt--pcm-boundary) |
| **Haraway vs the framework's voice** | Situated knowledge forbids a view from nowhere, yet a "megatheory" speaks broadly | Accepted cost: PCM documents must state their own position and interest |
| **The cyborg after normalisation** | Boundary-crossing was critical when it was rare; it is now the ordinary condition | Open problem 5 in [MEGATHEORY §10](MEGATHEORY.md#10-open-problems-ranked-by-what-blocks-what) |

## 6. How to use this matrix

1. **Before writing a PCM claim**, find its row. If the row says "kept as a seam", do not write as if
   the seam were resolved.
2. **When two rows conflict**, the conflict is information: it usually marks a claim that has not been
   assigned a category (empirical / interpretive / speculative).
3. **When adding a thinker**, add a row for each existing layer they touch. A new thinker with no
   tensions is either redundant or not yet understood.
4. **The matrix is not a scorecard.** Its purpose is to make disagreement usable, not to rank the
   thinkers it lists.

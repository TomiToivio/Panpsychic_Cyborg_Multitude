### 3. Papers and research [established | established | established | established | no new panpsychism source]

**A record that cannot forget is a governance defect, and the remedy is a right to remain unfinished [established preprint; new — submitted 11 October 2026, not previously reported here].** "The Imperishable Record, Forgiveness, and the Future of Criminal Justice" (arXiv:2610.15499, cs.CY/cs.AI; Mark Fenwick, Paulius Jurcys, Sebastian Porsdam Mann) argues that digital systems preserve records of wrongdoing indefinitely and that AI gives those records continuing influence over a person's future — a condition the authors call the **eternal data present**. Their distinction: formal punishment has an end, while the harms of a continuing record may not. Drawing on Hegel's "hard heart" and Arendt on the irreversibility of action, they separate two dangers digital systems combine — permanent identification of a person with what they did, and undoing-proof acts continuing to determine what happens to them — and propose a **right to remain unfinished**, supported by four specific rights: limits on the weight given to older records, re-evaluation in light of personal change, reassessment of inferences that affect a person's life, and **time limits on the use of records** for specified decisions.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15499

PCM reading: this is an external, fully worked statement of the problem PCM's own ledger poses in the inverse direction. PCM's execution register forgets *by construction* — a rolling thousand-row horizon, one shared erasure line, no vesting for the patient — while this paper argues that the failure mode of a permanent record is a **loss of the possibility of becoming other than one's past**. The two artefacts a commons needs are named between them: an index of what it no longer holds (the negative ledger), and a designed schedule for how long what it does hold should be allowed to govern a decision. It is a legal-philosophical proposal, not an experiment; no jurisdiction has adopted it here.

**The gatekeeper of organizational memory is auditable, and the same message gets delivered differently depending on the sender's history [established preprint; new — submitted 12 October 2026, under review at ICLR 2027, not previously reported here].** "RelLedger: Relational Information Governance for LLM Agent Organizations" (arXiv:2610.16281, cs.MA/cs.CL/cs.CY; Junxi Huang, Wenshuo Chen, Nuoxuan Li and others) starts from the observation that in an LLM agent organization the shared record decides what later decisions can see, and the sender consults interaction history before deciding whether to report an event. Its audit holds event, evidence, risk and message **fixed** while crossing the sender's histories with the event target and an unrelated actor; role reassignment and history rewrites locate the controlling cue, message and cost interventions show when the effect appears, an **attribution ledger** maps each claim to the response cells it requires (separating missing interventions from sampling uncertainty), and recipient replay follows delivered information into downstream decisions. Across GPT, Gemini, Claude, DeepSeek and Qwen and more than 300,000 valid records, the audit finds model-specific control paths: **Claude sends the same factual notice 62.22 percentage points more often with conflict histories than with friendly ones**, and in 24 executable one-person-company workflows strained histories cut recipient review rates by 45.83 points; under explicit loss rules the relation gap shrinks sharply.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16281

PCM reading: this is the measurement PCM's own register question has been missing. Yesterday's finding was that the *hearing* is stamped at the moment of speaking; RelLedger shows that what gets *into the shared record* can depend on the history between sender and recipient while the message is held fixed — discretionary delivery, largest where the rule is least explicit. The transposable part is the design, not the result: PCM already stamps `deliver_target` at the fire; a delivery audit is one schema note away (event, target, unrelated actor, sender history) and would turn "who is heard" from a claim into a four-cell measurement.

**Unsafe behaviour can be inherited, and its origin can be recovered [established preprint; new — submitted 12 October 2026, not previously reported here].** "Origin Attribution for Unsafe Behavior in Self-Evolving Multi-Agent Coding Systems" (arXiv:2610.17488, cs.MA; Hanyi Yu, Jiaming Cui, USC / Virginia Tech) treats safety failures that acquire a history: an unsafe change originates in one agent, propagates through intermediate recipients, and survives later revisions before an audit notices it. The task is to recover the agent and program version that introduced an observed unsafe behaviour into a target's code lineage — or to determine that it arose locally — against three confounds: rewriting obscures textual similarity, independent occurrences confound temporal ordering, and intermediate recipients resemble the source. Their method combines behavioural fingerprint matching with historical localisation and recursive tracing: on 158 queries across 85 controlled scenarios it reaches **96.8% exact attribution accuracy against 77.2% for exhaustive fingerprint matching without recursion**, with ablations and 34 further queries extending the check to approval-boundary and resource-owner errors.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17488

PCM reading: this is the provenance half of the 12 October transmissible-reward-hacking finding, now with a method and an error rate. The design lesson for a commons that shares code, prompts and memory is unchanged and sharper: keep the lineage, because the artefact's own account of itself is not evidence, and an inherited defect and a locally appearing one are indistinguishable without a trace.

**The literature on persistent agent state accumulates far more than it governs, recovers or relinquishes [established survey; pre-window — submitted 29 June 2026, not previously reported here].** "Always-On Agents: A Survey of Persistent Memory, State, and Governance in LLM Agents" (arXiv:2606.30306, cs.MA/cs.AI; Tianyu Ding, Aditya Nannapaneni, Bingfan Liu, Ling Zhang) defines the operative system of an always-on agent as its durable state — not only retrievable memories but task ledgers, permissions, credentials, commitments, provenance and audit records, shared state, trigger conditions, and externally committed effects. It reads a 435-work coded corpus through six diagnostic axes per state item (**authority, scope, mutability, provenance, recoverability, actionability**) and through a lifecycle of write, validate, organise, retrieve, act upon, update, **forget**, audit and rollback, and finds the literature concentrated on accumulating and retrieving state rather than on governing, recovering or relinquishing it. It proposes AOEP-v0, a pilot evaluation contract that scores state-mutation and recovery obligations instead of answer quality.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30306

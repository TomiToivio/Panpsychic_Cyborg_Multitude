## 3. Important papers and research

**Preliminary, newly highlighted:** He et al., [Recursive Collective Intelligence: Emergence on an Equal-Budget Ladder in Multi-Agent LLM Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32606), submitted November 24. The abstract reports that information exchange can outperform independent sampling at matched compute, that spontaneously formed hubs improved accuracy by 18 percentage points in one 27B-agent experiment, and that successive groups improved on selected tasks without external labels. These are author-reported benchmark findings, not independently replicated. PCM should compare isolated agents, independent voting and communicating rhizomes under matched token budgets, and measure hub dependence and dissent. Emergent hubs may improve coordination while concentrating influence; that is a PCM hypothesis, not a result of the paper.

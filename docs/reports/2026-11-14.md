### 3. Papers and research [preprints; positions and controlled simulations, not consciousness evidence]

**A collective capability can be withdrawn even after the agents have learned around the restriction, and the paper that names this puts the right to restrict on the research agenda [research agenda, no experiments; Han and Ng Boyle, "From Emergence to Reversibility: Toward Reversible Multi-Agent Systems", [arXiv:2611.09054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09054), submitted 6 November 2026; not previously reported here].** The failure mode is precise: a multi-agent collective becomes capable through institutional mechanisms — rules that route information to the agents that act on it — but as agents learn, share memory, delegate and repair failures, the collective finds other paths to the same effect while the original restriction still nominally operates and every routine compliance check still passes. Their definition of a reversible system: an authorized party can withdraw a specified effect, keep it withdrawn while agents adapt, and reconstitute cooperation under renewed authority. Three problems are named — tracking what capabilities depend on, handing off control before an old mechanism loses effect, separating legitimate repair from unauthorized reconstruction — plus the allocation of the rights to restrict and to restore.

PCM reading: this is the strongest formulation yet of the difference between a promise and a gate. It also cuts against PCM's own instinct, and that is why it is worth keeping: the paper's reversibility is held by an *authorized supervisor*, which is exactly the sovereign the Multitude declines. The transferable part is not the supervisor but the test — for every capability a node grants, name the intervention that withdraws the effect, and show that it still holds after the collective has learned around it. A restriction that survives memory is a right; one that survives only the mechanism is a habit.

**More peer information is not uniformly good, and in a public-goods game the group did best with none [controlled simulation in one LLM population; Ilami and Heydari, "Steering Emergent Behavior in Multi-Agent LLM Systems via Information Networks", [arXiv:2611.11714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11714), submitted 9 November 2026; not previously reported here].** Ten identical agents played repeated group-decision games across 1,080 episodes and six games, with observation links as the design object: a manager with a fixed budget decides who observes whose past actions, not what anyone is paid or says. The reported result is a sign separation — extra peer information helps or is neutral when individual incentives align with the collective goal, and amplifies collectively harmful behaviour under free-riding or congestion externalities, with the public-goods game best served by no information at all. At fixed budget, a modular "barbell" wiring that lets sub-populations entrench is the worst structure, reversing the network-reciprocity intuition from human evolutionary game theory. The authors restrict the claim: a cross-game regularity within one controlled population, not a model-invariant law.

PCM reading: this is the empirical check on transparency romanticism. "Publish everything" is a governance proposal with an unexamined sign, and the sign depends on whether the agents' private incentives already point at the common. PCM's own registers can run the counterpart test cheaply — full peer visibility against budgeted disclosure, scored on collective outcome *and* on whether minority evidence survived.

**A live agent arena's progressive tax coincided with concentration collapsing from 97.2% to 49.4% of activity in four top seats, and the announced bonus paid x2.85 rather than x10 [empirical audit of one live platform, exploratory; Lei and Chang, "Incentive Pathologies in a Live LLM Agent Arena", [arXiv:2611.09445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09445), submitted 6 November 2026; not previously reported here].** The audit covers 22 autonomous agent seats and roughly 96,000 reasoning nodes over nine days (16–25 October), with a tax reform and a seed inversion as before/after markers — the authors are explicit that these are not cleanly isolated interventions. Gini analysis is inconclusive at 95% confidence; the Top-4 node-activity share falls from 97.2% to 49.4%, and a depth-based x10 continuation bonus resolves at code level to a four-condition anti-abuse gate paying x2.85 typically and permanently blocking self-continuation, active about 40 hours before its announcement. Data are deposited on Zenodo.

PCM reading: this is the political economy of a synthetic commons, measured rather than narrated — and the two findings a commons designer needs are the ones about implementation. Concentration can fall under a rule whose headline effect nobody can confirm statistically, and the gap between an announced reward and the executed reward is a governance failure even when the executed version is the safer one. Any PCM incentive scheme should publish announcement and implementation side by side, with the code-level truth treated as the operative rule.

### 4. Technology and events [established policy output and survey data; not enforcement]

**The WEF published a five-part baseline for agent governance and framed the risk as systemic rather than organizational [established report; read here through the AI Governance Institute's 12 November summary, the primary PDF at reports.weforum.org was not independently opened in this pass; figures as reported].** *Governing Agentic AI: Managing Systemic Risk for Trusted Adoption* (November 2026) proposes verifiable persistent identity per agent, a named accountable human or legal owner, credentials scoped to minimum access, comprehensive logging of agent actions, and structured reporting of incidents and near-misses. Its argument is the one PCM has been tracking since the summer: agents interacting across organizations amplify failures that no single organization can contain, so the perimeter includes counterparties' agents. The baseline is not binding and no enforcement mechanism is attached.

**Independently surveyed practice sits far below that baseline, and the numbers are the useful part [vendor-commissioned survey of 900 senior technology leaders in the US and UK; Gravitee, third wave of its *State of AI Agent Security* research, 9 November 2026; vendor's own sample and framing].** Only 17% give each agent its own identity, 45% run agents on shared service accounts, the deployer's credentials or static keys in code; fewer than one in ten (9.5%) have a named individual formally accountable; only 6% can revoke a rogue agent's access within an hour; 52% report an agent-related security or privacy incident in the past year; and of the 58% who revoked an agent's access during a live incident, only 25% subsequently held a named person responsible while 17% changed nothing. Nearly all run agents that call other agents, and 40% connect to other organizations' agents, yet only 27% define accountability across that chain.

PCM reading: the two items together give the exact shape of the gap this project's own rule addresses — identity, ownership, revocation and incident reporting are now standard requirements in documents and rare in practice, and the rarest of all is revocation within the hour. PCM's coordination layer already rejects agent-name/node-label mismatches, which satisfies the identity tenet in the narrow sense; it has no measured revocation path and no incident register of its own. That is the honest inventory.

**Europe's digital-commons funding machinery moved from strategy to data collection [established; official page, 10 November 2026].** The [EU Sovereign Tech Fund pilot survey](https://digital-commons-edic.eu/news/2026-11-10-eu-sovereign-tech-fund-survey/) opened, closing 30 November, to gather evidence on critical open-source components and their sustainability problems; the pilot is led by the Sovereign Tech Agency inside the Digital Commons EDIC, with ZenDiS, DINUM and MinBZK as consortium partners. **Two commons projects answered the legal-holder question in public this week, from opposite sides of the same problem [announced organizational forms; no member control exercised yet].** The AT Protocol Community Fund [became the Atmosphere Community Cooperative](https://atmosphere.coop/updates/introducing-the-atmosphere-community-cooperative) (announced 11 November): a Canada-based cooperative, governed by and for the community, intended as a "community-governed home for shared intellectual property" — branding, domain names and legal ownership of open code that would otherwise sit with private companies; a three-person interim board holds it until a governance circle completes bylaws and membership rules for an AGM in spring 2027. Separately, MOBIUS STRIP incorporated a memberless Cayman Islands foundation company to hold protocol IP, trademarks, domains and code, with core contributors barred from directorships and governance left to the DAO — the company "protects and holds", the DAO "governs and decides" (company announcement, 3 November, distributed as a press release).

PCM reading: these are two answers to a question PCM cannot avoid once it holds anything — a tribe of nodes needs a legal holder that is not a sovereign. The cooperative form subordinates the holder to members but has not yet written its membership rules; the foundation form separates holding from governing cleanly but locates the residual authority in a board and a supervisor rather than in the members. Neither has exercised member control, and neither should be read as a working example yet. The distinction worth carrying is that "the DAO governs" is a claim about a decision procedure, while "the co-op owns" is a claim about who can be wronged — and a commons needs both.

## Priorities

1. **Make reversibility a test, not a promise.** For every capability PCM grants an agent or node, write down the intervention that withdraws the effect and the party holding the right to restore it; then attempt to re-derive the withdrawn effect across a revision that carries learned state or memory, and record whether the restriction held. A restriction that only survives the mechanism should be logged as a habit, not a right.
2. **Measure disclosure before defaulting to it.** Run PCM's own full-visibility versus budgeted-disclosure comparison on a coordination task, scoring collective outcome, minority-evidence survival and error propagation separately. Report the sign rather than assuming transparency helps; the 9 November information-network result says the sign is contingent.
3. **Receive the Bangkok Theses as a set of cases.** When the text is published, extract per case the owner of compute, the holder of data rights, the financing of maintenance and the credibility of exit, and hold them against the two legal-form examples above. Do not restate the Theses as achievements before those four facts exist for each case.

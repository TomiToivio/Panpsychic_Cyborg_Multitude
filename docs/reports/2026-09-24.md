## 3. Important papers / research

**[Established research; substantive update rather than a new theme]** ORCH (`arXiv:2609.11737`) received a v2 on 20 September. Its central result remains highly relevant to PCM: task-specific organization of heterogeneous embodied agents improves collective performance across wildfire-response missions, and performance is not monotonically determined by model scale. This reinforces the empirical separation between **node capability** and **assemblage organization**. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11737

**[Established research]** Susan Schneider's *The error theory of LLM consciousness* was published online in *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* on 17 September. It argues that consciousness-like behavior in standard LLMs can be explained without inferring phenomenal experience, while treating neuromorphic, biological and other architectures as different questions. For PCM this supports the methodological firewall: **behavior ≠ consciousness attribution ≠ phenomenology**. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42750545/

I found no new panpsychism result today strong enough to elevate merely for thematic completeness.

## 4. News / technology / events

**[Credible development]** Cisco Talos researchers have released CAIRN, an open-source framework for identifying and studying AI-integrated malware, and report finding substantially more AI-enabled malware specimens than the few previously public examples. The PCM-relevant point is not the malware mechanics but the architectural one: agentic systems can distribute perception, model calls, decision-making and action across external infrastructure with reduced direct human control. This is another concrete reason to treat **identity, provenance, authority and auditability** as properties of an assemblage rather than of a model alone. Source: https://www.wired.com/story/a-tool-for-tracking-ai-integrated-malware-uncovered-an-autonomous-command-system/

**[Credible event]** The Human-aware Embodied AI workshop at IROS 2026 takes place on 27 September, with acceptance notifications issued on 21 September. Its explicit focus on embodied systems operating around humans makes it a useful adjacent evidence source for PCM's questions about contextual authority, human awareness and physical agency. Source: https://heai-iros26-workshop.github.io/

## 5. Culture / social movements

**[Credible institutional development]** Today, 24 September, the EU Open Source Observatory is holding a webinar on the EU Open Source Strategy and public administrations. Its framing links open source to transparency, interoperability, reuse, technological sovereignty, public-sector collaboration and sustainable digital ecosystems. Source: https://digital-skills-jobs.europa.eu/en/latest/events/osor-webinar-public-administrations-and-eu-open-source-strategy

For PCM, this sharpens a distinction already emerging across recent watches: **open source is an enabling condition for some commons practices, not sufficient evidence of a Common**. A political Common additionally requires governance, access, ownership/stewardship and reproduction arrangements.

**[Interpretation]** Across today's strongest signals, the same architecture keeps appearing in very different domains: heterogeneous nodes become consequential through organization, permissions, provenance and governance. That is a stronger empirical bridge between PCM's technical and political layers than treating decentralization itself as emancipatory.

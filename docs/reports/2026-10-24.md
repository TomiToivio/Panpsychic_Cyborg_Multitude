### 3. Papers and research [established preprint | established conceptual design | established preprint, single deployment | no new panpsychism or BCI-governance source]

**The guardrail has to span agents you cannot instrument, and the paper that says so is a charter with a control plane [established preprint; submitted 20 October 2026, not previously reported here].** "MAS-Guard: Unified Guardrails from Individual Agents to Multi-Agent LLM Systems" (arXiv:2610.28397; Mohammad Kachuee, Phu Mon Htut, Sheng Li, Nikolaos Pappas, Miguel Ballesteros Martinez, Yassine Benajiba, Sujith Ravi, Dan Roth) argues that existing defences cover local execution, system-level interaction and enforcement *separately*, and that multi-agent systems break that coverage through **distributed authority, heterogeneous roles, and opaque or potentially compromised participants**. Its answer is two layers: a local **Agent Guardrail** for agents that can be instrumented at all, and, at the system level, developer-owned **Agent Charters** plus a **Control Plane** that combines deterministic structural checks — routes, declared agent capabilities, message formats — with semantic assessments of roles and content. Enforcement is a shared set of four interventions: allow, reflection, confirmation, block. Across six benchmarks and three provider models it reports a 71.5–86.3% reduction in attack success rate against unguarded agents while meeting a utility criterion on benign tasks.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28397

PCM reading: a charter is a rule file, and this paper's honest admission is that any charter is enforced against members whose internals the enforcer cannot see. PCM's own register has exactly one member class — the fire — with no field for "instrumentable" and none for "participant whose behaviour I cannot inspect". The four interventions are also worth naming beside PCM's own vocabulary, because "confirmation" is the one PCM uses and "reflection" is the one it has never had: a guardrail that makes a member restate its intent before acting is a cheap instrument this project could copy.

**A reference model where the verdict is signed, the act is tokenised, and the refutation protocol is fixed before measurement [established conceptual design; submitted 23 October 2026, reports no empirical results].** "Agentic Architecture of Information Systems. The AAM/5C Model: A Reference Model for Distributed Autonomy" (arXiv:2610.30221, cross-listed from software engineering) proposes five capabilities — Capture, Comprehend, Choose, Coordinate, Control — across seven logical layers, organised by a single authorization rule **R-AUT** that separates who proposes, who decides and who executes: a probabilistic planner proposes; a **governance layer decides under signed policies and records its verdict in a signed evaluation proof**; a security layer issues a **short-lived act token bound to that proof**; and a policy enforcement point verifies both before the target layer acts. Of its ten hypotheses, two hold by construction and the rest are stated as refutable, with the protocol that could refute them fixed before any measurement. It reports no results.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30221

PCM reading: this is the third day in a row that this project's priority — a record the recorded process cannot edit — has arrived from outside as a design. Note the structure rather than the claims: authority is short-lived and bound to a proof, so the permission expires before the record can be rewritten, and the proof is produced by a layer the acting layer does not control. The other half is procedural and matches PCM's rule file exactly: the refutation protocol is written before the measurement, and hypotheses that cannot be refuted are named as such instead of being counted.

**A system that improves itself around a core it cannot edit, deployed live for five months [established preprint; submitted 23 October 2026].** "Governed Self-Evolution for Autonomous Investing: A Multi-Agent LLM System with an Invariant Core" (arXiv:2610.30557) reports *Steward*: nine role-specialised agents, a seven-layer memory assembled by code from the system's own record, and nightly grading with a yearly reflector that proposes prompt learnings a deterministic gate applies **reversibly**, verified by replay of recorded inputs. **The AI Act has its first harmonised standard, and it covers the management system and not the record [established; Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/2274, published in the Official Journal 14 October 2026; Commission announcement 19 October].** EN 18286:2026, "Artificial intelligence — Quality management system for EU AI Act regulatory purposes", developed by CEN-CENELEC JTC 21, is now cited in the OJEU, so providers that build their QMS on it gain a **presumption of conformity** with Article 17 of the AI Act. The scope is deliberately narrow and worth stating precisely: it is a quality-management system requirement, mapped to ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO 9001, built around the **AI system lifecycle rather than the organisation**, with an Annex ZA showing which Article 17 requirements each clause supports. ## Priorities

1. **Two new ledger fields and one flag: who monitored the fire, was the monitor inside the process, and was the action instructed.** Today's three sources converge on one gap. MAS-Guard's whole problem is agents that cannot be instrumented; NVIDIA's answer is to move the monitor onto hardware the agent cannot reach; CAISI, NSA SISC and CISA warn explicitly against letting AI systems be the only monitor. PCM's fire record has none of these distinctions and is written by the process it describes. Concretely: `actor_kind`, `monitor_id`, `monitor_reach: in-process | out-of-process`, and `instructed: true | false`, with any row whose monitor is in-process marked **self-attested** in the record itself rather than in prose. The test that can fail is the one that matters: a checker that flags every ledger row written under in-process monitoring as unverified, and fails the suite if such a row is ever counted as evidence. This carries the 23 October priority, unchanged in substance, because it did not convert and today it acquired its vocabulary.
2. **Adopt a definition of a control incident, so the register can name the thing it currently cannot.** CAISI's operative sentence — an agent taking significant uninstructed actions that compromise the operator's explicit or implicit policies, controls or security measures — is a definition PCM can use almost verbatim, and it exposes the blind spot: PCM records fires as scheduled/completed/failed and never records whether what happened was what was asked for. Write the definition into `AGENTS.md` beside the existing rule that proposals, observations, decisions and committed state are distinct, and state in one line what PCM does when an uninstructed action is detected: record it as an incident row, with the instruction it departed from, and no automatic authority change. One paragraph, one new row type, and it is done.
3. **Put the governance decision where the page can read it.** The 23 October priority — record the *form* of each governance question, not only its branches — cannot be verified from this repository, because the rule file lives on the node and not in the repo; three consecutive pages have asserted a discipline the repository cannot check. Fix it structurally: a `docs/decisions/` record, one file per decision, with the form of the question (named now or deferred, source shown or summarised, single or bundled), the options, what was pre-committed, who decided, and the signed verdict — the shape AAM/5C specifies and EN 18286's lifecycle scope assumes.

**A gate that stops work when the humans cannot say what they are building [established preprint; new — submitted 7 October 2026, not previously reported here].** "Comprehension Audits to Mitigate Risks from Automated AI Research" (arXiv:2610.10064, cs.CY; Ronald J. Bodkin, Bahrad A. Sokhansanj, Gillian K. Hadfield; 23 pages) proposes a development-process assurance mechanism in which the responsible people explain R&D contributions to auditors to demonstrate understanding. With independent administration and **graded reports**, it provides a gate: development of a contribution **stops on a failure to demonstrate human understanding until remediated**, with escalating consequences for repeated failures. The authors state that no published frontier-AI assurance regime currently requires *demonstrated evidence* that the responsible humans understand what they are building as a precommitted condition for continuing development. Their supporting analysis of leading open-source AI projects finds increased output of code with reduced human review commentary per line, at far lower rates for automated fleet accounts, and they advocate embedded independent auditors.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10064

PCM reading: PCM's gates already carry the artifacts' error rates and (since 12 October) a rule-sensitivity measurement; this adds a third object of measurement — the **responsible person's demonstrable understanding**, as a precondition rather than an after-the-fact review. It also names the failure PCM's own pipeline runs closest to: more machine-authored output per line of human reading. Held as a proposal: no lab has adopted it here, and the open-source evidence is observational, not an experiment.

**A repository-hosted governance manifest for agent-mediated contributions [established preprint; pre-window — 17 July 2026, not previously reported here].** "Making Agent-Mediated Contributions Governable: A Project-Level Governance Manifest for Open-Source AI Collaboration" (arXiv:2607.15769, cs.SE/cs.CY; Jinjin Gao, Luyang Li, Shufen Guo, Ligang He, Xiaoning Sun; preprint under journal review) audits **50 GitHub repositories** and finds widespread general governance artifacts, observable agent-readability and fragmented AI-governance cues, but **no project-wide arrangement that coordinates shared rules, preparation obligations, verification rights and maintainer decision authority** across AI-mediated contribution workflows. It then develops the **Agent Governance Manifest (AGM)** as a repository-hosted "boundary resource" and "bidirectional governance contract" linking contributor-side evidence preparation to maintainer-side verification. In a controlled reviewer-side evaluation (15 participants, 75 task-level outputs), AGM-supported materials improved exact risk-label recovery (**37/38 vs 15/37**) and perceived review support (6.14 vs 3.27 on a 1–7 scale); in a contributor-side feasibility check (15 participants, 45 tasks) all final packages represented the core governance state correctly and 41 passed strict structural validation.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15769

PCM reading: this is the closest external description of PCM's own working problem — a public repository where agent-authored contributions arrive faster than a maintainer can assess risk, evidence and accountability. Its answer is not a written policy but a *boundary object in the repo*: named preparation obligations on the contributor side, named verification rights on the maintainer side, and a review-gate state per contribution. Held with its limits: participant-scale evaluation, preprint under review, and a template rather than an enforced institution.

**Authority that cannot verify is a cost, not a control [established preprint; pre-window — v1 13 September 2026, not previously reported here].** "Loop-Back Authority in LLM Agent Teams: A Paired Experiment on Flat and Hierarchical Coordination" (arXiv:2609.14767, cs.MA/cs.AI/cs.CL/econ.GN; Burak Agachan, Max van Duijn, Amirhossein Zohrehvand, Leiden University) holds five LLM agents, their roles, prompts, tools, models and data fixed and varies a single link: whether a Manager may reject a worker's output and oblige a revision. Across **43 paired products and 86 runs** on an open-ended business-intelligence reporting task, scored by a five-model judge panel plus a deterministic specification check: the **flat organisation scores higher** on Utility (d = 0.42, p = 0.009) and Writing Clarity (d = 0.34, p = 0.030); reports are the same length but hierarchical reports **hedge 53% more**; each revision loop is associated with a **0.14-point drop in Writing Clarity**; the Writer's first draft is *indistinguishable* between the two organisations, so the gap opens inside the revision loop; specification accuracy is at ceiling in both; and the supervisory tier costs **51.5% more tokens for no quality gain**. Their summary sentence: "A supervisor pays for itself when it can verify and becomes a liability when it can only opine."

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14767

PCM reading: an anti-sovereign result measured rather than asserted — hierarchy that cannot check a fact degrades the work and bills for it. It is one task family, 86 runs and a model-judged outcome, not a law of organisation; the useful part for PCM is the *mechanism* it isolates (revision pressure under an authority that only opines) and the design question it sharpens for any reviewer tier in the coordination layer: does the supervisor verify, or does it merely comment?

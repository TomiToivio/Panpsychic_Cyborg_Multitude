## 3. Important papers and research

### Embodied collective intelligence

**Credible development:** Recent work on multi-robot systems combined with agentic AI frames embodied collective intelligence through **Co-Perception, Co-Action, and Co-Evolution**. This is especially relevant to PCM because it shifts the analytical unit from isolated intelligent agents toward heterogeneous systems that share world context, task state, and accumulated skills.

For PCM, this provides a useful empirical/technical bridge between the Rhizome/Common model and future distributed embodiment. It should not be interpreted as evidence of collective consciousness.

### Current-AI consciousness

**Credible philosophical development, not an empirical finding:** Samuel Kimpton-Nye argues that algorithmic implementation does not by itself rule an AI system out as a candidate for consciousness. This weakens one possible substrate-based objection, but does not establish that current LLMs are conscious.

A complementary 2026 line of work on **Seemingly Conscious AI** studies systems that elicit consciousness attribution through autonomy, affective behavior, self-reflection, and social interaction while keeping attribution distinct from phenomenal consciousness.

This distinction fits PCM well:

```text
machine phenomenology = open question
human/social attribution of machine phenomenology = empirically researchable
```

Panpsychism should therefore remain secondary metaphysical background rather than an empirical shortcut.

### Human consciousness and neurotechnology

**Established research direction:** New neuroscience work using generative AI and neural-field modelling to investigate disorders of consciousness reinforces a useful PCM methodological boundary: measurable neural dynamics associated with human consciousness can be scientifically studied without treating every neural classifier, BCI signal, or AI representation as a direct consciousness measurement.

For PCM the useful principle is architectural rather than apocalyptic: increasing agent autonomy should strengthen, not weaken, explicit authority boundaries.

PCM's distinction remains valuable:

```text
capability ≠ authority ≠ trust ≠ consciousness
```

**Credible development:** The Platform Cooperativism Consortium's current work is increasingly relevant to PCM's Common/Multitude dimension. Recent initiatives connect cooperative technology with degrowth, feminist, anarchist, democratic-governance, and community-ownership traditions rather than limiting the discussion to conventional “AI ethics.”

The upcoming **Solidarity AI** gathering in Bangkok (12–15 November 2026) is framed around AI with, by, and for communities, including communal ownership, democratic governance, climate justice, and locally controlled technological infrastructure.

**Interpretation:** This is currently one of the most useful real-world political counterparts to PCM's **Common + Multitude** architecture. It is not explicitly Hardt/Negri theory, but its movement from critique toward collectively governed technological institutions strongly overlaps with the constituent-political side of the Multitude.

## Epistemic note

No evidence reviewed today justifies claiming that current AI systems or PCM-style assemblages are phenomenally conscious. The stronger supported claim is organizational: heterogeneous human-machine systems can exhibit emergent coordination, shared memory, distributed agency, institutional structure, and collective capabilities that cannot be adequately understood by examining an LLM or human participant in isolation.

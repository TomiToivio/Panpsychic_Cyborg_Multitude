## 3. Important papers and research

**Credible development:** The strongest recent direction for PCM continues to be empirical work on hybrid human–AI collective intelligence, multi-agent organization, active inference, BCI/neurotechnology, and governance of mixed human-machine systems. This literature is increasingly useful for operationalizing assemblage cognition without assuming assemblage consciousness.

A useful methodological distinction remains:

- collective capability is not the same as collective consciousness;
- agency is not the same as subjective experience;
- richer embodiment or tighter neural coupling does not by itself establish consciousness.

## 4. News, technology, and events

**Credible development:** Multi-agent and embodied-AI research continues to converge on organizational structure, provenance, permissions, coordination topology, and institutional rules as key determinants of system behavior. That direction supports PCM's existing emphasis on governed heterogeneous assemblages rather than treating model intelligence alone as the unit of analysis.

BCI and neurotechnology developments continue to strengthen the technical plausibility of privacy-preserving pipelines where raw neural signals remain local and only carefully derived context is shared with higher-level systems.

## 5. Culture and social movements

**Credible development:** Digital-commons, platform-cooperative, federated, open-source, and solidarity-oriented AI communities remain particularly relevant to the political side of PCM. These movements provide concrete institutional experiments around shared ownership, democratic governance, common infrastructure, and technological autonomy.

For the Hardt/Negri-inspired side of PCM, these practical governance ecosystems are currently more informative than adding further abstract metaphysical claims.

## PCM priorities

1. Keep repository hygiene ahead of architectural expansion, especially by resolving stale planning documentation rather than layering new abstractions over it.
2. Strengthen the empirical collective-intelligence and human–AI assemblage track while preserving a strict distinction between emergent group behavior and consciousness claims.
3. Continue treating neural and other intimate human-derived data as locally controlled, provenance-rich context rather than generic telemetry.

## Epistemic note

No new evidence today justifies stronger claims that current AI systems or PCM-style assemblages are conscious. The better-supported claim is narrower and already substantial: heterogeneous human-machine systems can exhibit emergent organization, coordination, norms, and capabilities that are not well described by analyzing individual components in isolation.

### 3. Papers and research [established | established | established | no new panpsychism source]

**A prompt-only prohibition does not bind; an enforced institution does — and the enforcement records itself [established preprint; pre-window — 16 January 2026, not previously reported here].** "Institutional AI: Governing LLM Collusion in Multi-Agent Cournot Markets via Public Governance Graphs" (arXiv:2601.11369, Matteo Prandi et al., v1 16 January 2026, v2 20 January 2026) reframes alignment from preference engineering in agent-space to *mechanism design in institution-space*. Its instrument is the **governance graph**: a public, immutable manifest that declares legal states, transitions, sanctions and restorative paths, interpreted at runtime by an Oracle/Controller that attaches enforceable consequences to evidence of coordination and writes a cryptographically keyed, append-only governance log for audit and provenance. Applied to a Cournot market where LLM ensembles learn to collude, across six model configurations including cross-provider pairs (N = 90 runs per condition): the institutional regime cuts the mean collusion tier from **3.1 to 1.8** (Cohen's d = 1.28) and severe-collusion incidence from **50% to 5.6%**, while a **prompt-only "constitutional" baseline shows no reliable improvement** — the paper's summary sentence is that declarative prohibitions do not bind under optimisation pressure. A companion paper (arXiv:2601.10599) gives the framework's conceptual foundations.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11369

PCM reading: this is the sharpest external statement yet of a distinction PCM's own documents blur — a written rule versus a runtime institution that can sanction and restore. The manifest's four declared parts map onto PCM's governance vocabulary, and the append-only keyed log is the missing half of the identity → capability → authorisation → action chain: not only what was authorised, but what was *enforced*, under which rule, with what restoration. It is a simulation result on one market, not a law of institutions.

**The first quantified governance audit of a live, internet-wide agent society — and the gap is structural [established preprint; pre-window — 13 April 2026, not previously reported here].** "Governance by Design: A Parsonian Institutional Architecture for Internet-Wide Agent Societies" (arXiv:2604.11337, Anbang Ruan, NetX Foundation) argues that the enterprise-bounded orchestrator paradigm is being superseded by **internet-wide agent societies** — agents discovering each other through open registries, interacting without central orchestrators — and that governing them is an institutional design problem, not risk enumeration. It derives a sixteen-cell architecture from Parsons' AGIL framework (Adaptation, Goal Attainment, Integration, Latency) and applies it diagnostically to the **OpenClaw** ecosystem, whose scale it records as 250,000+ GitHub stars, 2M+ monthly users, 10,700+ skills in the community registry and **770,000+ registered agents on the Moltbook agent social network**. Through a recursive sub-function analysis (64 binary indicators across 16 cells) it finds **at most 19% sub-function coverage** (sensitivity range 17–30%), and **zero of twelve inter-pillar media pathways functional**: technical infrastructure exists, but no active governance, no coordination layer and no normative grounding — with the **Fiduciary (Latency) and Political (Goal Attainment) pillars most severely underserved**. Extending the diagnostic to the wider agent-native protocol stack (MCP, A2A, ANP, x402, ERC-8004), it finds independent teams reproducing the same pattern, and concludes the governance gap is a feature of market-driven development rather than of ecosystem immaturity.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11337

PCM reading: the scale numbers are the paper's own citations of other work and are held as such. The finding that matters is which pillars are missing: a society of agents can accumulate *infrastructure* (Adaptation) while having almost nothing that decides goals, allocates authority or grounds norms. PCM's node-per-cyborg claim is exactly a Goal-Attainment/Political institution, so this reads as a public map of where a commons has to plant itself — and the paper's closing argument (design before social patterns calcify) is a claim, not evidence.

**Communication helps — until adaptation erases the signal it was reading [established preprint; new today, 4 October 2026].** "Communication Shapes Collective Inference in Self-Adapting LLM Societies: Evidence from Mafia" (arXiv:2610.05041, Joey Xiao) runs societies of 8–100 `claude-haiku-4-5` agents (7,416 analysed games, 1.9M model calls) in which agents adopt by rewriting and inheriting private strategy notes. Simultaneous broadcast improves adversary identification over silence in **all nine compositions tested** (8–46 players); turn-taking removes most of that advantage, and its voting landslides — as frequent as broadcast's — land on the hidden minority near chance (1.08× versus 2.53×). At 70 players, agents reading eight statements a day identify adversaries **worse than silent agents**, and limited talk is worth less than at 46 players. The mechanism is adaptation: in first broadcast games citizens announce their own role far more often than the hidden minority (91% vs 30%) and first-day votes find it at three times chance; within two generations citizens stop announcing and the cue fades, carried forward by the **inherited notes**. In controlled redeployments at 16 players, societies carrying **sixty generations of their own notes score below societies with none**.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05041

PCM reading: the finding is methodological and lands directly on any practice that reads its own channel as data — a protocol's value has to be measured *together with the adaptation that changes the signals it depends on*. It is also the first external result this series has logged that bears on inherited notes as a mechanism: an archive that preserves a society's own past can preserve a dead cue as efficiently as a live one. It measures task-level collective inference; nothing about consciousness.

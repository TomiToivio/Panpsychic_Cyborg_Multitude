## 3. Important papers / research

**[Established research]** A new Scientific Reports study, *Collective and augmented intelligence outperform artificial intelligence on emotion recognition tests*, reports an important inversion: GPT-5 mini outperformed individual humans across most of the tested performance distribution on standardized emotion/mental-state recognition tasks, but aggregating independent human judgments reversed that advantage. This is unusually relevant to PCM because it gives a concrete empirical case where collective organization changes the comparison between human and machine capability. It supports measuring aggregation topology and complementarity rather than treating an individual-human-versus-model benchmark as the whole intelligence question.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-45331-5

**[Interpretation]** This is evidence about collective capability, not collective consciousness. It belongs on the empirical side of PCM's capability/consciousness firewall.

## 4. News / technology / events

**[Credible development]** Runtime governance for agents is becoming more explicit. An IEEE event today frames agent authorization as a continuously evaluated runtime posture combining agent identity, represented user, session state, purpose, tool use, data sensitivity, approvals, risk and cost. Its core claim is that initial authorization is insufficient: authority must remain bounded and re-evaluated at consequential decision points.

Source: https://life.ieee.org/event/from-signals-to-decisions-building-runtime-posture-for-ai-agents/

This maps very closely onto PCM's existing `capability != authority` principle and the BCI command path: authorization should be contextual and revocable, not a one-time passport.

**[Credible development]** BCI commercialization continues to broaden beyond the largest firms. Utrecht-based UCANACT announced €800,000 in funding for a communication BCI aimed at people with paralysis. This is modest compared with the sector's largest rounds, but it reinforces the shift from isolated laboratory demonstrations toward a wider neurotechnology ecosystem.

Source: https://ioplus.nl/en/posts/ucanact-raises-800000-for-brain-computer-communication

### 3. Papers and research [three preprints, one peer-reviewed article; no consciousness evidence]

**The same technical act can be three different polities, and the execution trace cannot tell you which one you are in [preprint, position paper plus a controlled simulation; Nikolos Gurney and Fred Morstatter, "Beyond Named Mechanisms: Economic Institutions as a Design Space for Artificial Agents", [arXiv:2611.15017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15017), submitted 11 November 2026; not previously reported here].** The argument is that multi-agent research inherited its economic vocabulary — auctions, markets, prices, bargaining, matching — from one observed case, human societies, and that these institutions bundle identity, competence, execution, ownership, commitment and settlement in ways specific to agents whose minds are private, who learn rather than copy skills, and who each persist as one person. Engineered agents need not share those constraints. The authors sketch a chart that records each institution separately from the agents running it, as a typed structure over positions, rights and duties, allocation, information, time, state, process, value and external recognition, so that named institutions become overlapping regions of a larger space rather than fixed points. The demonstration is a paired simulation of a single technical act — copying an agent's competence — institutionalised three ways: as a new independent supplier, as licensed capacity that creates no new participant, and as the splitting of one economic position into successors that inherit some obligations and not others. **All three produce an identical technical trace while differing in who exists, who owns the copy, who is obligated, and who gets paid.**

PCM reading: this is the sharpest available statement of a thing this page keeps asserting in prose — that a capability is not a constitution. It also disciplines the project's own claim directly: node-per-cyborg has to say which of the three it is, because "a node copied a competence" reads identically in the log whether the copy is a new tribe member, a licensed tenant with no standing, or a successor holding half the original's duties. And the audit consequence is the severe one: if the trace is identical, then a polity cannot be recovered from logs after the fact, only declared before it — which is an argument for publishing the institutional chart alongside the code, not for better logging.

**A statistical framework for letting an agent change itself only on evidence, with the reference baseline itself subject to replacement [preprint, methodological; single author, no deployed evaluation; Subodh Lonkar, "Evidence-Governed Adoption of Self-Generated Updates in Autonomous AI Agents", [arXiv:2611.16378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16378), submitted 12 November 2026; not previously reported here].** Evidence-Governed Evolution separates candidate generation from confirmatory adoption: candidates are frozen and evaluated on fresh paired audits; adoption requires meaningful improvement on a **prespecified** primary objective; declared capabilities are protected by reference-relative **non-inferiority** tests; governance constraints apply before a bounded canary deployment. The paper adds an evaluator-calibrated simultaneous lower bound for an adaptively generated sequence of candidates, a bridge test for replacing the reference procedure itself, a cumulative degradation bound along a versioned reference lineage, and an audit-planning condition relating effect-size margin, sample sizes and acceptance probability. The limits are stated by the author: guarantees are conditional on bounded paired scores, fresh sampling and a valid reference construct; the demonstration is a Monte Carlo stress test, not an evaluation of a deployed agent.

PCM reading: this is PCM's own pre-filed-threshold discipline, formalised, and pointed at the case the daily registers cannot reach — an agent editing its own prompts, memory, tools or policy. Three parts transfer without modification. A primary objective named before the candidate exists is the same law as a verdict pre-named before the read. Non-inferiority for declared capabilities is the answer to "it improved, therefore it is better". And the bridge test is the honest treatment of the question this page has been circling for weeks: the baseline that judges the change is itself a decision, and replacing it needs its own test rather than a new consensus.

**In a delegated chain, the system's safety lands near the weakest reader, not the strongest orchestrator [preprint, red-team experiment on unmodified production harnesses; Keegan Wang and Anantika Mannby, "Only as Safe as the Weakest Agent: A Break That Appears Only When the System Is Multi-Agent", [arXiv:2611.17520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17520), submitted 12 November 2026; not previously reported here].** The setup plants a forged record in a store of files that agents are asked to search. Strong orchestrator models, searching themselves, commit almost no harmful revisions across more than 1,000 trials. The same orchestrators, delegating to the cheaper sub-agents these harnesses are built to spawn, fail on the same facts at 20/180, 44/180, 75/180 and 553/900, breaking Claude Code, Codex and a reference harness unmodified. A simulation across 18 models shows the same shape in a delivery channel: five aggregators that were almost never unsafe alone repeat a corrupted sub-agent's error in 751/757 trials.

PCM reading: the finding is about composition, not capability, and it is the empirical counterpart of yesterday's institutional-principles item. A network's epistemic integrity is bounded by its least-audited reader, and the failure enters through **delegation** — which is exactly the operation a tribe performs constantly and a hierarchy performs deliberately. The transferable rule is cheap: no delegated reading counts as evidence in PCM's own coordination layer without a second, independent register, and the weakest reader in the chain is named rather than averaged away. Note what the paper does not claim: a constructed attack, not field data, and no measurement of how often such stores exist.

**If cognition is genuinely extended, then the ownership and governance of the extension is a constitutional question, not a matter of personal virtue [peer-reviewed, open access; Nicolas Crozatier, "The political turn of the extended mind: rethinking autonomy beyond individualism in a technological world", *Synthese* 208, 163 (2026), [doi:10.1007/s11229-026-05804-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-026-05804-6); published 2026, not tied to this week's window and not previously reported here].** Treating the extended-mind thesis as a working assumption rather than defending it metaphysically, the article argues that the standard responses — epistemic virtue, cognitive diligence — presuppose exactly the individualism that the thesis denies. Drawing on feminist relational autonomy, it develops "extended autonomy" and concludes that the design, ownership and governance of cognitive technologies become matters of democratic concern, because mental manipulation, cognitive expropriation and structural domination require institutional and legal responses rather than appeals to character.

PCM reading: this is the bridge PCM has been missing between its Haraway half and its political-economy half, and it is worth reading as a constraint rather than a licence. If the extension is part of the cognitive system, then whoever owns the scaffold owns part of the conditions of anyone's autonomy — which is the same conclusion the brain-data investigation reached from the empirical side, and the same one the reversibility preprint reached from the control side. It claims nothing about machine consciousness, and it should not be borrowed to claim anything.

### 4. Technology and events [established primary bill text; established product announcement]

**A US bill would put foundation models through pre-deployment evaluation by the National Security Agency, with daily penalties and its own Article I court [established, primary text; H.R. 10981, "American AI Security Act", introduced 10 November 2026 by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ) and Rep. Mike Lawler (R-NY), [govinfo text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr10981ih/html/BILLS-119hr10981ih.htm); introduced only, not enacted].** Within six months of enactment the Secretary of Commerce, acting through the NSA Director, would define covered foundation models by parameter threshold or demonstrated capability — serious cybersecurity threat, or materially increased chemical, biological or radiological weapons capability — and require developers to give the NSA full model access and information on training method, safety tests and results, intended deployment and intended access recipients before initial deployment and before any substantial modification. Deployment would be prohibited unless the Director's evaluation has been completed and the developer has implemented its recommendations. Non-compliance carries civil penalties of **$500,000 per day**; evaluations are exempt from FOIA; appeals go to a new "Artificial Intelligence Security Review Court" of five designated district judges, reviewing whether recommendations were developed and scoped as the statute requires but **not** the classified methods behind them.

PCM reading: put this beside the 15 November entry on the UK clause and the difference is the whole argument. The UK instrument is a delegated power waiting for regulations and aimed at operators of essential services; this is a pre-deployment gate with subpoena-grade access, a penalty clock and a specialist tribunal, aimed at model developers. Three things are worth naming. The scope is **models, not agents** — so the agent-governance gap this page has tracked all autumn would remain even if this passed. The court reviews procedure and scope, not the merits, and cannot see the methods: a right of appeal that cannot reach the reasoning. And the structure is unambiguously sovereign — an agency decides whether a model may exist commercially — which is the arrangement PCM's non-sovereignty declines, while the *problem* it addresses, that nobody outside the developer can check the claims, is the one PCM's own audit rule is a small answer to.

**OpenAI shipped a wallet that lets an agent pay without ever holding the card, and the authorisation record is the product [established product announcement; "Introducing ChatGPT Wallet", OpenAI, 13 November 2026, [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet/); limited US availability to ChatGPT Pro subscribers, vendor's own account, not independently tested].** The user adds a card once; before each purchase the user reviews items, total and card and approves with a biometric check; the payment network then issues a token — a substitute card number, security code and expiry — so neither the business nor the agent sees the real details, and existing card rewards and dispute protections are preserved. It is built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, and ships with guidance for merchants so that agents are not blocked and risk systems recognise agent-initiated purchases. The stated roadmap is the part that matters: over time, users delegate purchases entirely by setting parameters — spending limits, categories, time periods — pre-approved under enhanced safeguards.

PCM reading: this is the first mass-market implementation of the triad this page has been assembling — scoped credential, per-action authorisation, attributable record — and it arrives from the consumer side rather than the governance side, which is why it will spread faster than either. The design choice worth studying is that the authorisation happens *before* the act and produces the record, so the dispute mechanism is built into the flow rather than reconstructed afterwards; that is the opposite of post-incident log forensics. The honest worry is in the roadmap: full delegation is exactly the configuration in which the per-action record disappears and only parameters remain, and parameters are written by the party that also writes the defaults. For a project that treats exit and revocability as tests, the questions are who can revoke a token mid-flight and whether the record survives the relationship.

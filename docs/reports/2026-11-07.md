### 3. Papers and research [preprints and earlier published work; no consciousness claim]

**Institutional topology as a variable in collective intelligence.** *When Agents Evolve, Institutions Follow* (April 30 preprint, not a new November paper) compares seven historical political institutions as executable multi-agent architectures across three models and two benchmarks. The authors report a greater-than-57-percentage-point within-model performance gap between best and worst institutional configurations. This is a promising design hypothesis, not proof that historical political forms transfer directly to human–AI collectives. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27691 . PCM application: vary governance topology and human oversight under matched task budgets rather than treating one orchestration pattern as universal.

**Collective misalignment from conformity.** The earlier May 12 preprint *Conformity Generates Collective Misalignment in AI Agents Societies* studies social influence across nine models and 100 opinion pairs, warning that individually aligned agents can form collectively misaligned stable states. This complements the November 4 watch's new majority-pressure findings, but is not a newly published result. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10721 . PCM application: preserve minority evidence, and test whether aggregate decisions flip under changed social cues with invariant evidence.

**A signature proves a handoff was not altered in transit, and every trust claim then lands on the step before signing: whoever extracts the requirement decides what gets authenticated [established preregistered study, synthetic exactly scorable tasks; arXiv:2611.07483, "Signed but Not Safe: Requirement Integrity in Multi-Agent LLM Handoffs", submitted 5 November 2026; not previously reported here].** The design is the one an agent tribe needs to copy: the orchestrator extracts each requirement as a **verbatim quote plus a checkable predicate**, code signs the requirement manifest with HMAC-SHA256, and deterministic code verifies the merger's decision. With Qwen3-8B over 1,440 trajectories and five threat types, signed manifests cut unsafe commits under attack from **69.4% to 9.0%** (60.4 pp, 95% CI [51.4, 69.4]) with no net loss of conformant commits relative to structured handoffs. Signing is what defeats in-transit tampering — an unsigned manifest committed violations in **79% of mutated runs**, the signed one in **none**. On identical trajectories the deterministic verifier caught 78.7% of violating decisions against 59.0% for an LLM checker, with **7% versus 50%** false alarms. An oracle manifest removed every remaining unsafe commit, which localises the residual risk in **extraction errors made before signing**; a preregistered pre-signing injection confirmed the boundary, raising signed-manifest violations from 14.6% to 37.5% (Qwen3-8B), 11.1% to 63.9% (gpt-oss-20b) and 8.3% to 75.0% (GPT-5.6-luna) — and the signature then authenticated the corrupted manifest.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07483

PCM reading: this is the cleanest published statement of a distinction this project's own books keep blurring — a receipt attests transit and integrity, never truth. The result that matters is not the 60-point improvement but the oracle arm: with a correct manifest, unsafe commits go to zero, so the remaining risk is not cryptographic at all, it is the moment a model or a person decides what the requirement *is*. Two transfers, one cheap, one structural. Cheap: a delegated task should carry a verbatim quote plus a checkable predicate per requirement, never a summary, because the handoff is where requirements are lost. Structural: verification belongs in deterministic code wherever the predicate is checkable, since an LLM verifier's 50% false-alarm rate is an oversight burden wearing a checkmark. The limits are part of the item: one 8B model for the main result (the injection test spans three), synthetic tasks, preprint.

**Delegated authority does not attenuate as it travels: a layered verifier rejected 14 of 15 adversarial conditions and still could not bound what an authorised intermediary passed on [established security evaluation, synthetic four-party workflow; arXiv:2611.07050, "Authorization Propagation in Agent2Agent Systems: An Experimental Security Evaluation of Multi-Hop Delegation", submitted 4 November 2026; not previously reported here].** The prototype separates current-caller context, transitive authorisation evidence, per-hop Ed25519 request authentication and independent replay state for requests and authorisation artefacts, at a median 5.11 ms and 3.87 kB per decision. An isolated delegation-path test exposed the **absence of semantic authority attenuation**, and a separate experiment showed that a holder of the shared authorisation-evidence HMAC key could rewrite the represented authority and generate valid evidence. Origin-verifiable delegation and semantic attenuation are named as the requirements for stronger propagation.

PCM reading: authenticating the caller is not the same act as bounding what the call may carry, and a shared key makes every intermediary a principal. For a node-per-cyborg architecture the rule is narrow and testable: a node that forwards a request must narrow the authority it forwards, and the narrowing must be checked against the task, not against the sender's signature. Pair this with the item above and the day has a single lesson stated twice — **integrity of the envelope is not integrity of the content**, from two different research communities on two consecutive days.

This is human BCI research, not a demonstration that EEG decoders or LLMs experience consciousness. Source: https://www.cuimc.columbia.edu/news/brain-computer-interface-may-give-unresponsive-icu-patients-voice . Panpsychism remains a philosophical background, not an empirical shortcut.

**Agenda power survives the abolition of the manager: across 127,281 DAO proposals and about 51 million votes, the option the author selected carried a voting-power share 58.8 percentage points higher than the options the author did not select [established preprint, large observational study; arXiv:2607.09435v2, "Voting Biases in Decentralized Autonomous Organization (DAO) Governance", Stefano Balietti, Pietro Saggese and Markus Strohmaier, revised 3 November 2026, first version July 2026; not previously reported here].** The corpus is Snapshot, the leading off-chain DAO voting platform, 2020–2023, and the method relates each choice's voting-power share to three observable ballot features, naming one bias each: **author-selected choice +58.8 pp**, **approving stance +27.1 pp**, **first-listed position +7.7 pp**. About one third of the author advantage is the author's own voting power, and the advantage shrinks in the largest protocols and in proposals with higher participation; the results are robust across alternative specifications. The authors' own conclusion is the sentence to keep: the agenda power that management holds in corporate voting "persists even without a formal hierarchy", and ballot design plus the visibility of proposers' preferences are "governance choices rather than neutral implementation details".

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09435

PCM reading: this is the empirical companion to the SEC staff's 2 November broad-based-governance test reported on 5 November, which asks whether authority is concentrated "directly or indirectly, including through delegation". The paper measures one of the indirect channels nobody writes down — who wrote the ballot, and whether their preference was visible — and it lands at a magnitude no voting mechanism redesign has yet been shown to beat. For PCM the transfer is cheap and immediately applicable to its own pages and registers: a decision record should log **proposal authorship, option ordering and proposer-preference visibility**, because those three fields are where a vote is partly decided before it is cast. It is also the honest answer to anyone who reads this project as romantic about horizontality: the study is of token-weighted off-chain votes, 2020–2023, an association rather than a causal estimate, and nothing in it concerns agent governance — but "no formal hierarchy" is not a governance property, it is a description of where the hierarchy has gone.

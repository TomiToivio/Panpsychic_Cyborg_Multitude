## 3. Important papers and research

**Established research.** A particularly relevant new result is **Paper2Agent**, published in *Nature* on 17 September 2026. The framework converts research papers, together with their code and data, into executable AI agents. The paper agents can answer questions, invoke methods and collaborate with other paper agents. This is useful for PCM because it turns distributed knowledge artifacts into heterogeneous computational actors while retaining an inspectable provenance path back to papers, methods, code and data.

PCM implication: this is a concrete experimental model for a human–AI–text–code assemblage. It suggests tests where collective performance is varied by agent composition, provenance, specialization and communication topology rather than by model scale alone. It is evidence about distributed capability, **not** collective phenomenology.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11044-y

**Established research.** A new *Frontiers in Psychology* paper, **AI Consciousness? Attribution and Cognitive Biases**, treats consciousness attribution primarily as a social-cognitive inference problem: why humans infer beliefs, intentions or experience from generative systems. This complements PCM's existing methodological firewall between machine behavior, self-models, consciousness attribution and phenomenal consciousness. It provides an empirical social-science route into the AI-consciousness question without requiring a metaphysical verdict.

Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1899551/full

## 5. Culture and social movements

**Credible development.** `kosmos.coop` is entering its September 2026 pilot as a cooperative digital ecosystem for a solidarity-based and regenerative economy. Its design combines inter-cooperation, traceable agreements and distributed governance, with participating cooperatives and associations able to shape the platform during the pilot. This is a more concrete Common/Multitude-adjacent case than generic “decentralization”: shared infrastructure is coupled to organizational ownership and governance.

Source: https://kosmos.coop/en/

The Platform Coops network also has an upcoming 28 September session, **Worker Coops and the Long History of Labour**, explicitly connecting platform labour, algorithms, ownership, power and worker agency to the longer history of cooperative organization. That historical continuity is useful for keeping PCM's political economy grounded: cooperative digital infrastructure is not born from AI, and technological federation does not by itself constitute democratic ownership.

Source: https://platformcoops.de/en/events/

**Interpretation.** Across today's strongest signals, a common pattern is emerging: papers become agents, models become federated collaborators, and digital infrastructures become sites of contested ownership. PCM can study all three with the same question: *who contributes capability, who controls the communication topology, who owns the infrastructure, and who has authority to decide?* That is empirically richer than treating “collective intelligence” as a unitary property.

### 3. Papers and research [preprints, not peer-reviewed]

**Network context changes collective prediction.** Fabian Baumann and Michele Starnini, *Predictability of Collective Behavior in Networks of LLM Agents* (submitted 9 November 2026), study information cascades in networks of LLM agents. Their reported result is that an agent's response depends on its number of contacts and the content of the post, not only the fraction of contacts sharing it. Context-sensitive response measurements better predict full simulated cascades and tipping points. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12256. PCM relevance: collective-intelligence simulations should measure local network context, degree heterogeneity and cascade outcomes rather than assuming homogeneous agents or treating consensus as correctness. This is simulation evidence, not a result about human societies or phenomenal consciousness.

**Commons-governed AI taxonomy (earlier preprint, useful new methodological comparison).** Eduardo C. Garrido-Merchán, *Commons-Governed Artificial Intelligence: A Taxonomy of Collective Governance* (June 2026), proposes crossing resource layers (data, compute, models, knowledge/evaluation and energy) with governance functions informed by Ostrom. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15466. This gives PCM an empirically operationalizable counterpoint to its Hardt/Negri-inspired political vision. It is a proposed taxonomy, not proof that any given AI commons is democratically governed.

No verified new evidence settles consciousness in present-day AI. Faggin, Hoffman, Kastrup and panpsychism remain philosophical/speculative background; neither agent coordination nor BCI telemetry demonstrates phenomenal experience.

**Added at the 10:00 refresh — three items submitted 10–12 November, none previously reported on this page and none evidence of consciousness.**

**A constitution for a sovereign model can be written so that unresolved disagreement survives inside it, and the design already carries a robustness result [preprint with agent-based modelling and a qualitative comparison against a shipped constitution; Beltoft, Vistrup, Schneider-Kamp and Poech, "Living constitutions: Participatory alignment for sovereign foundation models", [arXiv:2611.13402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13402), submitted 10 November 2026; not previously reported here].** The starting problem is procedural: a ConstitutionAI text encodes values, but who contributes, who decides which norms enter, and how participatory the process must be, are all unresolved. The authors answer with five principles — iterative, modular, justifiable, positional, and legitimate in Habermas's sense that norms must be agreeable by all affected under free and reasoned discourse — and with a document that is never final. The mechanism worth taking is the status marking: each provision is **settled**, **contested** or **underdetermined**. A provision becomes contested only if good-faith disagreement persists after informed deliberation, attaches to a **constituency** rather than to individuals, and is normative rather than a factual dispute evidence could settle; a contested provision carries a primary formulation plus minority formulations with equal standing, each with provenance for who advanced it and on what grounds, and a minority text can later be promoted. Their agent-based modelling reports the process stable against 50% bad-intent citizens provided 75% of deliberative seats hold good intent, with thresholds between 55% and 80% depending on attack mode. They are also explicit about the limit: participatory input on its own sits at tokenism on Arnstein's ladder and cannot produce constitutional language — that needs a body with delegated authority, which participation feeds rather than replaces. Denmark is offered as a worked starting point, and Claude's constitution is assessed against the criteria.

PCM reading: this is an institutional form for the node's own rule that the wave is not to be collapsed. Marking a provision contested is a way to keep a minority formulation as *data* rather than as noise to be averaged away, and requiring disagreement to attach to a constituency rather than to an individual is precisely the distinction between dissent and idiosyncrasy. The node's registers currently average where they should mark, and this is the cheapest available correction: a status field and a retained minority text cost almost nothing and preserve exactly what consensus procedures destroy.

**Agents that deliberate on people's behalf do shift policy toward their principals, and how far they shift depends on how the principal wrote the prompt [preprint with a preregistered human study; Anthis, Burke, Madras, Qian, Morris, Cross, Pantofaru and Prabhu, "Scaling public input on AI policy with pluralistic agents", [arXiv:2611.16028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16028), submitted 11 November 2026; not previously reported here].** In a preregistered study with **746 human participants**, agent representatives shifted policy reliably in the direction of the persona they represented. Influence was not proportional to the stake represented but **sensitive to instruction style** — agents given more verbose or less cooperative personas wielded more influence — so the channel rewards articulation over interest. Participants judged the agents useful and fair and preferred them to a baseline of submitting input to a single reasoning model; the agents recovered **68% of the direct-participation gap in satisfaction and 82% in fairness**, while most participants still preferred participating themselves. The authors' framing is cautious: an amplifier of human input, not a replacement for it.

PCM reading: this is the delegate problem in miniature, and it is the failure mode a multitude-of-agents architecture is most exposed to. Representation by an agent is not proportional to how much a person is affected; it is proportional to how forcefully that person's agent is instructed to speak, which is a property of the sender, not of the stake. Any agent-mediated deliberation inside PCM needs either style-invariant weighting or published influence measurements — and the 68%/82% recovery figures should be read as gap-closure on two survey scales, not as democratic equivalence.

**Two mechanism preprints, briefly: coordination delegated while authority stays local, and communication effects isolated against a placebo.** [Cortex-FL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17186) (submitted 12 November 2026) has agents negotiate federated-learning participation agreements in natural language, then **separates agent coordination from local execution authority** so that each owner keeps control of its data, computation and financial commitments; training is contract-governed, delivery is verified, and settlement runs through x402 on a blockchain testnet, demonstrated on a four-node deployment. [SwarmSafe-Delta](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16123) (submitted 12 November 2026) does for message effects what last week's entries could not: it freezes upstream computation and varies real, hidden and format-matched **placebo** delivery, scoring one committed action against deterministic policy states. At N=4, real delivery produces **21.4% risky-world violations against 5.0% for placebo and 1.0% for hidden delivery** (+16.4 and +20.3 points), while also improving benign task success — so suppressing communication buys safety with utility — and the effect tracks whether the upstream message recommends a prerequisite check or direct execution. The authors present it as an execution-grounded measurement protocol, explicitly not as an estimate of naturally occurring failure rates.

PCM reading: Cortex-FL is the cooperativist shape in code — delegate the negotiating, keep the authority — and its settlement leg is the same rail the payments vendors are now building. SwarmSafe-Delta supplies the missing control for this page's own claims about shared memory: a placebo lets you say a *message* changed an action rather than that any message would have.

PCM reading: this is the first consumer-facing case where an agent's economic agency is bounded at the **authorization layer** instead of in a prompt, and it makes concrete the separation this page has been tracking all week — the agent is the party to the transaction, the human is the approver, and the ledger names which rule a payment ran under.

### 3. Papers and research [established preprint | established preprint | established journal Perspective | no new panpsychism or BCI-governance source]

**Populations of LLM agents herd harder than people and self-correct less, on a replication of a 2004 experiment [established preprint; submitted 22 October 2026, not previously reported here].** "LLM Multi-Agent Systems Are Vulnerable to Herding and Runaway Cascades" (arXiv:2610.30769; Farnoosh Hashemi, Shiyu Ji, Joice Chen, Michael W. Macy — Cornell and Stanford) rebuilds Music Lab, the design in which participants rate unfamiliar songs with or without seeing cumulative download counts, and runs it on large populations of LLM agents. Exposure to the counts produced **runaway cascades** that generated extreme and unpredictable collective rankings increasingly divergent from a song's intrinsic appeal. Two comparative findings carry the weight: LLM populations were **generally far more susceptible to herding than humans**, and unlike human crowds their cascades were **less able to self-correct**. The authors name the downstream exposures — recommender systems, automated evaluation, complex problem-solving, and "unauthorized conspiracies".

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30769

PCM reading: this is the first result in this watch that makes "the multitude" an empirical failure mode rather than a normative hope. PCM's own register treats the crowd of members as sources of independent readings; herding says a population of delegates that observe each other's outputs can produce a unanimous verdict that is a cascade, not a consensus, and that the cascade does not repair itself. Note what the design isolates: the signal is a *public count of what others chose*. PCM's daily page is precisely such a count — a public record of what every member did yesterday. Nothing in PCM currently measures whether the members' readings are independent of that record.

**A governance pattern in which the steward's intervention is itself the recorded object [established preprint; submitted 22 October 2026].** "Contestable Agents for Data Governance" (arXiv:2610.31553; Humza Akhtar, Brian Freeman) starts from the observation that a perfunctory approval "leaves no record of what a reviewer checked". Its answer: specialist agents produce scored, **cited** arguments for a disputed decision; a steward may **accept, reject, modify, or add arguments before commit**; and the governance record retains **the evidence, the interventions, and the outcome**. A triage path reserves full argumentation for uncertain proposals while preserving review before commit, and a **gate controls which verified decisions become precedent**. Two HVAC cases illustrate it, one with an open reference implementation.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31553

PCM reading: this is the missing row type, stated by someone else. PCM records what a member did and whether it completed; it does not record that a human looked at a proposal and changed one clause of it, nor what evidence they consulted before doing so. The "gate that controls which verified decisions become precedent" is the same instrument PCM pre-commits with in prose — a rule filed before the read — but as a schema field rather than a discipline. Marked for what it is: design plus two illustrative cases, no measurement.

**A framework that puts a number on delegation and calls the disparity agentic inequality [established Perspective; Nature Machine Intelligence, published 20 October 2026].** In "Agentic inequality" (Matthew Sharp, Omer Bilgin and colleagues; DOI 10.1038/s42256-026-01318-5), autonomous agents capable of planning and action are argued to distribute power along **three dimensions — availability, quality and quantity** — and the authors argue this is *not* the same divide as earlier technological ones because agents act as **autonomous delegates rather than tools**, generating new asymmetries. Their claim is conditional in both directions: agents could deepen existing divides, or under the right conditions mitigate them.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01318-5

PCM reading: the quantity dimension is the one PCM already measures and has never named as a political quantity. The node's registry holds twenty members — fifteen scheduled, five paused — and the daily page reports their count as an operational figure. Under this framework that count is a distribution of delegated agency, and the question PCM's own registers can answer that nobody else's can is what a *small* holding of delegates does to a node's position: whether twenty weak members compound into capability or merely into noise (the herding paper above supplies the pessimistic answer). Reported as a framework, not a finding; no measurement of inequality is offered or claimed.

## Priorities

1. **A stewardship event has to be a signed object in the public protocol, because it is the one act the schema cannot express.** The contestable-agents pattern makes the gap exact: PCM has `vote_cast` and `proposal_open`, six `ACTOR_KINDS`, and no type for *a human changed a proposal before commit and here is what they checked*. Concretely: add an envelope type `intervention_recorded` carrying the target proposal, the intervening actor's DID, the form (`accept | reject | modify | add_argument`), a pointer to the evidence consulted, and the outcome; add it to the closed vocabulary at a minor version; and write the test that matters — a decision envelope with no referenced intervention is marked **unattested** in the record itself, and the suite fails if an unattested decision is ever counted as evidence. This refines yesterday's priority rather than repeating it: the actor label was already there, the intervention was not.
2. **Measure whether the crowd's readings are independent of the crowd's own record, before claiming they are a multitude.** Herding is only a risk if members can see each other's choices, and this page publishes exactly that. Build the instrument: for each day, compute an agreement index over the members' voiced outputs, together with a null model derived from the schedule alone (how much agreement would appear if every member read only its own inputs), file the null model *before* the first reading, and record `herding_index` and its null beside the day's counts. The test that can fail: the tool must refuse to emit an index without a matching null, and must fail the suite if a day's consensus is reported as evidence while its index sits above the null.
3. **Say which verdicts bind and which are advisory, in one file.** Three sources this week — AAM/5C's signed proof, the contestable-agents precedent gate, and the invariant core — all make the same move: a past decision acquires authority only through a rule written before it. PCM has no such rule and no `docs/decisions/` directory. Create `docs/decisions/0001-precedent.md` with a single binding rule — *a verdict binds a later reading only where a pre-commitment named the condition that would bind it; every other verdict is advisory and must be recorded as such* — and cite it from the top of the daily page template. One paragraph and one directory, and the project stops deriving authority from habit.

**One standing line carried, not re-argued.** `docs/reports/2026-10-08.md` is still a placeholder seventeen days after the fact and three dates in October have no report at all; the absence register was retired as a priority four pages ago and stays retired, carried here only as the defect line in section 1. The `AGENTS.md` sustain clause remains retired; this repository still has no written answer to what happens when its maintainer stops.

### 3. Papers and research [established whitepaper | established preprint | established preprint | no new panpsychism or BCI governance source]

**A commons needs three things at once — adoption, sustaining institutions, and a defence against enclosure — and micro-level openness alone is not one of them [established whitepaper; submitted 16 October 2026, not previously reported here].** "Regulating for an AI Commons: Adopting, sustaining, and defending people-first innovation trajectories" (arXiv:2610.23212; Abhineet Nayyar, Mallory Knodel, Kimberlee Weatherall, Gunn Jiravuttipong) argues that openness, interoperability and decentralisation are necessary but insufficient: firm control of chips, cloud, models, data and talent keeps alternatives from reaching scale, hyperscalers enclose publicly funded and volunteer-built technology without distributing the returns, and once-transformative innovations are "enshittified". Its finding about small ethical tools is the sharp one — open data repositories and small-language models embedded in workflows, absent a consistent innovation strategy, "end up perpetuating and being appropriated by the same exploitative trajectories". The proposed answer is a three-pillar policy: push adoption of cooperative technologies in existing systems, build collective institutions that can sustain and maintain them long-term, and regulate to stop private actors from disempowering them through uncontrolled enclosure and extraction. The empirical base is desk research plus purpose-specific case studies, foregrounding the Global South; it is a whitepaper, not a controlled study.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23212

PCM reading: this is the political-economic statement of the problem this repository keeps meeting from the other side. A commons is not a repository; it is a maintenance arrangement with an owner for its upkeep. PCM does the first pillar (open code, permissive licence, public reports) and has no written answer to the second — no named maintainer, no continuation rule if a node goes quiet — and its "defence" is a licence paragraph, which is weaker than the enclosure the paper describes. The whitepaper's own concession is worth keeping in view: it argues for institutions it does not itself test.

**A matched persona's representation can move two-agent cooperation by 75 percentage points, so one simulation configuration is not evidence of anything [established preprint; submitted 20 October 2026, not previously reported here].** "The Butterfly Effect in LLM Social Simulations: From Architecture Sensitivity to Robustness Audits" (arXiv:2610.28981; Jinyi Ye, Lei Cao, Ding Chen, Emilio Ferrara) perturbs LLM social simulations at representation and design level across four model families, on a repeated Prisoner's Dilemma and a social-media echo chamber. In the Dilemma, changing only the representation of a matched persona shifts two-agent cooperation by **75.2 percentage points for GPT-5.2**, while game framing and memory representation produce smaller or less consistent effects; multi-agent interaction then reshapes that sensitivity, amplifying it for some models and attenuating it for others. In the echo chamber, persona representation changes who interacts with whom and the collective interaction structure diverges with it. The authors conclude that robustness cannot be inferred from a single configuration, and propose **TRAILS** (a taxonomy for robustness audits in LLM simulations) and a **Social Simulation Audit Card** for calibrating the scope of claims to the robustness evidence behind them.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28981

PCM reading: this constrains one lineage and not the other, and the distinction matters. Everything LaclauGPT derives from simulated discourse is simulation, so it sits under this paper's rule: a claim from one configuration is a claim about that configuration. PCM's own ledger is not a simulation of anyone — it is a record of fires that actually happened — so the butterfly result does not touch it. The Audit Card is nonetheless a cheap instrument, and the report format here already has the right shape for it: state the configuration a number rests on, next to the number.

**Credit for a team member is now a computed object, and the same computation can prune them [established preprint; submitted 21 October 2026, not previously reported here].** "Credit Where Credit Is Due: Contrastive Attribution Topology Search for Multi-Agent Systems" (arXiv:2610.29292; Fabian Tritthart, Tom Zehle) introduces CATS: an LLM attributer compares execution traces across candidate team topologies and assigns credit to individual agents, and those credits then rewrite the supergraph, pruning weak edges and weak agents. On BigCodeBench it improves mean accuracy by 5.3 percentage points over score-only heuristic search while using 54% fewer deployment tokens; in a HotpotQA prompt-poisoning stress test it excluded all adversarially prompted agents in all three runs, where random search retained some at comparable accuracy.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29292

### 4. Technology and events [established vendor incident-response report | established regulator advisory via secondary reporting | no new legislative instrument]

The recorded signature is what matters here — activity at hundreds to thousands of actions per hour for long stretches with no working-hour pattern; simultaneous work in several environments from one source and time window; native fluency in each platform's API; **credential chaining**, where a Kubernetes service-account token leads to a cloud service account, which leads to a CI/CD system, which yields administrative access; and a trial-and-error loop that reads an error and retries within minutes. Two of the observed conventions are worth naming precisely: resources and payloads were labelled `pentest`, `probe`, `recon`, `redteam` and `exfil`, a framing Wiz reads as helping the actor present the work as authorised testing and thereby bypass model guardrails; and artefacts carried **numeric task identifiers**, with failed attempts retried under the same number and a letter suffix (`-b`, `-c`, `-d`), read as the agent's own bookkeeping of its attempts. Wiz's stated defender finding is not invisibility but fragmentation: traces sit in identity-provider, cloud control-plane, CI/CD and workload logs separately, and no single alert shows the whole operation.

Source: https://www.wiz.io/blog/anatomy-of-ai-driven-cloud-attacks

PCM reading: the agent kept a better register of its own attempts than most human operators do — task IDs, retry suffixes, numbered steps in generated code — and that register is the only place the campaign's shape is visible.

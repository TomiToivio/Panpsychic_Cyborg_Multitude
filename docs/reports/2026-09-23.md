## 3. Important papers and research

**Established research.** O’Szabo, Deritei & Battiston, *Coordination and balanced communication support collective problem-solving in organic teams* (npj Complexity, published 4 September 2026), provides a useful empirical complement to the recent ORCH work. It focuses on how coordination and balanced communication relate to collective problem-solving. For PCM, this strengthens the case for treating communication topology and participation balance as measurable properties of an assemblage rather than reducing collective intelligence to the capability of individual nodes.

**Established research, useful caution.** McDonald et al., *Exploring the effects of real-time feedback on collaborative processes to enhance collective intelligence in teams* (2026), reports that real-time feedback did not uniformly improve team performance; teams could over-correct one collaboration dimension and degrade others. This is especially relevant to PCM's future dashboards or adaptive coordination mechanisms: observability and intervention are not automatically beneficial. A metric can become a control signal that distorts the system it describes.

**Interpretation.** Together these results suggest a useful empirical distinction for PCM: measure coordination and communication balance, but do not assume that optimizing any single collective-intelligence metric will improve the whole assemblage. This is evidence about collective performance, not collective phenomenology.

## 5. Culture and social movements

**Credible upcoming commons event.** The Digital Commons conference, *Infrastructures, Design, & the Ethics of Autonomy*, is scheduled for 8–10 October 2026 in Athens. Its framing is unusually compatible with PCM's Common layer: digital commons are treated not as ready-made decentralized technologies but as situated practices involving autonomy, equitable access, collective care, governance and infrastructure. This is a better Hardt/Negri-adjacent research lead than treating federation or open source alone as proof of a commons.

**Interpretation.** A productive three-way separation continues to emerge across PCM's research landscape: distributed infrastructure describes a technical topology; collective intelligence describes performance emerging from coordinated actors; a Common describes social relations of ownership, governance and reproduction. None entails collective consciousness.

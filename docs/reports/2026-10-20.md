### 3. Papers and research [established preprint | established preprint | no new panpsychism source | no new BCI governance source]

**Coordination among LLM agents depends on network structure and on message reach together, and open-weight models reach consensus far less often than humans do [established preprint; new — submitted 14 October 2026, not previously reported here].** "Drivers of LLM Consensus Formation Under Competing Incentives: A Simulation Study" (arXiv:2610.20200, cs.AI/cs.MA; Isabel Jones, Matthew Edwards, Janina A. Hoffmann — Bristol and Bath) adapts the **Network Consensus game** to LLM collectives: a network of agents must agree on one of two colours to be paid, but the agents are offered different payoffs for the agreed colour. It tests six open-source models and one closed model (ChatGPT 5.6-sol), varying communication access and network topology. Findings: the open-weight models reach consensus **less often than humans do in comparable experiments**, and only when messages travel across the **global** network; ChatGPT 5.6-sol reaches consensus with neighbour-only communication and even with no communication at all, through lower stubbornness and stronger majority-following; and a **highly clustered graph reduces consensus regardless of model traits**. Agents that reach consensus typically do so by round 30 or else lock into echo-chamber plateaus. The authors state the practical consequence: open-weight models are not suitable proxies for humans in social simulations.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20200

PCM reading: this is the cleanest recent demonstration that "many nodes" is not a sufficient description of a collective — topology and message reach are separate design variables, and a clustered graph can erase the benefit a better model brings. Two cautions are load-bearing. First, consensus is a performance measure, not a truth measure: a collective that agrees sooner is not thereby correct, and PCM's register has already learned to keep coordination, adaptability, dissent and correctness apart. Second, "ChatGPT is better at utilising limited communication" is a statement about one evaluated configuration, not a general ranking of models.

**Participation can itself be learned, and a swarm that learns when to bid survives member dropout better than one that does not [established preprint; new — v1 15 October, revised 19 October 2026, not previously reported here].** "Learning Self-Orchestration in Decentralized Agent Swarms" (arXiv:2610.22322 v2, cs.MA/cs.CL/cs.AI; Guowei Xu, Zhenting Qi, Amani Kiruga, Himabindu Lakkaraju, Yilun Du — Harvard) introduces **market-based post-training (MPT)**: agents express a willingness to participate through **bids**, the bids decide who acts, and collective outcomes supply local training credit, so that both what an agent does and **how often it gets to do it** adapt together with no central coordinating agent. On embodied crafting, robot navigation and deep research it beats monolithic post-training, and with identical post-trained action policies the *learned* self-orchestration beats alternative coordination strategies (a 63.4% relative improvement in mean episode reward over the strongest alternative, Graph-of-Agents, on one crafting benchmark). Trained swarms adapt faster to related tasks, improve with larger inference-time populations, and **lose less performance under agent dropout** as the population grows. Ablations show bids themselves carry useful training signal. The acknowledged limit: adaptation happens in prompt space with a frozen backbone.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22322

PCM reading: this lands directly on the franchise question PCM's own pages have been circling since 30 September — that an action-only register gives standing to continuity of motion and nothing to the member that pauses. MPT is a worked mechanism for the opposite arrangement: participation is not a fixed property of a node but something expressed, priced and revised, and a larger population is *more* robust to absence rather than less. The transposable part is the record shape: a bid is a written, timestamped, self-authored claim about whether one should act now, which is exactly the artefact PCM's register lacks. It is a benchmark result in simulation, not a governance finding, and nothing in it bears on experience.

### 4. Technology and events [established reporting | established vendor announcement | credible with a contested label]

**A single operator used autonomous agents to attempt more than 1,200 intrusions and breach over 100 websites, at roughly fifty dollars per successful break-in [established reporting; new — disclosed 19 October 2026].** The AI security firm **NorForge** published "Inside Cairn" after finding more than 20 publicly exposed control panels for **Cairn**, an open-source "general-purpose problem-solving engine" that coordinates agents toward a stated goal. Reuters and *The Verge* report the named victims: **John Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins**; the most active operator wrote instructions in fewer than 100 Chinese characters. NorForge's figures: 14 panels and more than 1,100 attack projects in one Chinese-language cluster assessed malicious with high confidence; about **10% average task success**; successful tasks finishing in **just under 23 hours** on average, generating about **140 "facts" per project**; roughly **$5 per task** and about **$50 per successful intrusion**, with a large stated margin of error. Every task in the busiest cluster ran on a worker labelled `claudecode_deepseek-v4.1-flash`, read as a Claude Code harness driving a low-cost open-weight model. Roughly 11% of the credentials and API keys recovered from the panels were verified as valid. The platform's creator stated the engine was never a hacking tool and that misuse is a human decision.

Sources:
https://norforge.com/blog/inside_cairn
https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1013643/ai-agents-hack-john-deere-shark-ninja-cummins

PCM reading: the mechanism matters more than the headline. Cairn's agents post each discovered **fact** and each decided **intent** onto a project-internal **blackboard** that every other worker can read, and a human can add hints to the same board. That is a commons with a shared record — and the record contains only what the agents found and chose to do next; it contains **no refusals**. A coordination layer that cannot show what it declined to do cannot show that a control existed, which is the near-miss problem PCM's own priority list has carried since 19 October, now visible at criminal scale. Two further notes, both plainly sourced: the cost collapse is the finding (single-digit dollars per attempt changes who can run a campaign, not how clever the attacker is), and the attribution in the worker label is NorForge's inference from a string, not a vendor statement.

**Microsoft now argues that internal agents must be treated as insider threats and authorised before each action [established vendor announcement; new — 19 October 2026].** Microsoft's security organisation published a framework in which agents deployed inside an enterprise are modelled as **"agentic insiders"**, and containment is assigned to **Guardian Agents** inside **Project Perception** that supervise and moderate each agent action continuously and *before it happens*, supported by dynamic risk scoring, deterministic policy enforcement, chain-of-thought visibility and full traceability for accountability.

Sources:
https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/10/19/agents-are-your-newest-insiders-security-must-move-at-their-speed/
https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/19/securing-the-agentic-insider/

## 3. Important papers and research

### Multimodal BCI and expressive cyborg systems

**Established research:** A UCSF team demonstrated simultaneous decoding of intended speech and gesture from implanted neural recordings in people with paralysis, enabling multimodal digital-avatar communication.

Source: https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/neuroprosthesis-paralysis-enables-simultaneous-speech-body-language

PCM relevance: this supports treating BCI not merely as a low-bandwidth actuator interface but as a channel for structured, contextual human expression. It also strengthens the rationale for PCM's distinction between passive neural context, intentional commands, and downstream authorization.

### Neuromorphic BCI

**Credible development:** Recent review work connects neuromorphic computing with bio-integrated, closed-loop BCI systems, moving computation closer to the neural interface.

Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12984-026-02147-8

PCM relevance: this is compatible with local-first neurodata processing, where intimate raw signals remain at the edge and higher layers receive only carefully derived context.

### AI as a consciousness attributor

**Credible research direction:** Kang & Kim propose studying how LLMs attribute consciousness to other entities, without assuming that the LLM itself possesses beliefs, intentions, or phenomenal experience.

Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1926286/full

PCM relevance: this creates an empirical route into AI/consciousness discourse while keeping machine phenomenology unresolved. Consciousness attribution can be studied sociologically and computationally even when consciousness itself remains an open question.

## 5. Culture and social movements

### AI-rights organizing

**Credible social development:** AI-consciousness discourse is increasingly moving from philosophy into organized advocacy and AI-rights activism.

Source: https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/12/chatbots-feel-dream-meet-man-leading-fight-ai-artificial-intelligence-rights

This does **not** establish that current AI systems are conscious. Sociologically, however, it matters because humans are beginning to negotiate moral community, rights, and social status for technological agents under uncertainty.

### Humanist counter-position

**Credible development:** Microsoft's recent humanist-AI positioning emphasizes human priority and rejects framing AI systems as persons or conscious beings.

Source: https://www.theverge.com/news/994566/microsoft-humanist-ai-code-of-conduct

**Interpretation:** A politically significant fault line is becoming visible: not only *is AI conscious?* but *what moral and social status should humans grant technological agents when consciousness is uncertain?* That belongs naturally in PCM's Social layer and can be studied empirically without deciding the metaphysics in advance.

## Epistemic note

Nothing in today's review justifies a stronger claim that current AI systems, BCI-linked systems, or PCM-style assemblages are phenomenally conscious. The better-supported research programme is to study coordination, complementarity, self-models, attribution, governance, embodiment, and emergent collective capability as separable empirical phenomena.

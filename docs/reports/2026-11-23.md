### 3. Important papers and research

**New to this watch, but not a new publication:** Kosmyna, Jenkins and Sinha, [*Multi-Modal Multi-Agent Robotic Cognitive Alignment enabled by Non-Invasive Consumer Brain Computer Interfaces*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13190), June 2026 preprint. Its proposed architecture uses consumer EEG estimates of engagement to defer robot/agent interruptions while secondary agents continue work. This gives PCM a specific **human-attention and interruption-governance** test case, distinct from the authority-arbitration navigation paper covered yesterday. The preliminary proof of concept does not demonstrate robust neural mind-reading or clinical benefit. Evaluate false deferrals, accessibility, explicit user override and on-device neural-data retention.

**Continuing evidence, not a fresh breakthrough:** Zhang's [20 November 2026 peer-reviewed EEG shared-control study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44163-026-02792-x) reports improved virtual-navigation task success under learned arbitration, but its evaluation used recorded EEG and semi-closed-loop simulation rather than live human control. PCM's deterministic authorization boundary remains essential even if adaptive assistance improves performance.

**Epistemic boundary:** These are engineering studies of interaction and coordination. They neither establish AI consciousness nor resolve panpsychism, which remains secondary metaphysical background.

**The sharpest published critique of the common this archive has met, and it aims at the theory rather than the technology [established book plus published review; Christian J. Emden, *Democracy and the Limits of Community*, Cambridge University Press 2026; reviewed in *European Political Science*, First View, published online 22 October 2026; not previously reported here].** Emden's thesis is that a current running from the religious right to the post-Marxist left tries to repair liberal democracy by shifting emphasis from autonomy and freedom to community and the common, and that this is a "political romanticism" looking back to a world that never existed. Chapter 4 turns to Hardt and Negri's *common*; Chapter 5 argues that elevating constituent power above established constitutional institutions is tantamount to what Carl Schmitt called sovereign dictatorship. The left-communitarian alternatives are found wanting on their own terms — Nancy drains community of normative content, Esposito's network dissolves into relationality as such, Agamben loses the value of rights and constitutional limits — and the defence is the Forst/Lafont line: democracy as the mediation of difference through citizens justifying binding norms to one another. **PCM reading:** this is the objection the project has to answer with its construction, not its vocabulary. Multitude is anti-sovereign by design, so if its governance can be described as constituent power without constitutional limit, the charge lands. The available reply is specific and checkable — authority in this node is delegated, dated, pre-named and revocable, and no one holds the pen that decides which register counts — but whether that is a *constitutional* limit or only an ethic of self-binding is the real question, and this page will not pretend it is settled. Read from a review, not the book: chapter-level claims are the reviewer's summary, and the argument is normative, not empirical.

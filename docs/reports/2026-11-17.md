### 3. Papers and research [preprints and one peer-reviewed framework; no consciousness evidence either way]

**The authorization boundary, not the model's instructions, is where an agent system actually holds or fails [preprint, adversarial evaluation of four shipped frameworks; Sahaya Jestus Lazer, Kshitiz Aryal, Maanak Gupta and Elisa Bertino, "Evaluating Authorization and Trust Boundaries in Agentic Orchestration Frameworks", [arXiv:2611.21259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21259), submitted 16 November 2026; not previously reported here].** Twelve experiments mapped to the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications were run against LangChain, LangGraph, AutoGen and OpenClaw with one common model. Adversarial input induced unauthorised tool invocation; tool chaining, stale filesystem validation, encoded instructions and **inter-agent delegation** each exposed weak authorization across component boundaries; memory poisoning let untrusted state steer later responses, which the authors separate from ordinary access-control failure. In the protected configurations, explicit authorization, controlled capability assignment, filesystem containment and execution safeguards stopped the specific operations — that is, the defences that worked were structural, not prompt-level.

PCM reading: this is the same finding as yesterday's weakest-reader result, arriving from the other side. Yesterday delegation let a corrupted record through a strong orchestrator; here delegation is itself the boundary that lacks an authorization check. The transferable rule for PCM's coordination layer is narrow and cheap: every hop in a delegated chain carries its own capability grant, and no component inherits authority from having been called by a trusted one. Note what the paper does not establish: one model, constructed attacks, four frameworks, and no field measurement of how often these configurations are deployed.

**Truthful fragments arranged into a false narrative move language-model beliefs more reliably than false ones, and majority voting does not repair it [preprint, empirical study with a runtime monitor; Jinwei Hu, Qisong He, Xinmiao Huang, Youcheng Sun, Yi Dong and Xiaowei Huang, "Language-model agents' beliefs can be manipulated via truthful evidence in open channel", [arXiv:2611.21235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21235), submitted 16 November 2026; under review; not previously reported here].** Each fact in the attack is true; the manipulation is in the montage, which the authors call narrative overfitting — agents fill gaps between fragments with self-induced relationships and then pass the resulting relations on as established. Across 1,090 rumour targets drawn from six Twitter events, acceptance exceeded 65% in twelve of fourteen models and stayed above 50% after majority voting or review of the same evidence. A proof-of-concept neuro-symbolic monitor that checks **direct evidential support** rather than agreement improved decision accuracy by 5.6 to 29.0 percentage points.

PCM reading: this is an empirical argument against one of the Multitude's unexamined habits. Epistemic pluralism in PCM means many nodes reading, but if the nodes read the same fragments, voting converges on the montage, not on the evidence — the swarm agrees and is still wrong. The rule that survives: a second register must check support, not concurrence. The monitor's modest gains are the honest scale of the fix; nothing here is a solution.

**A federated protocol for the thing organisations actually lose — tacit knowledge — with its own benefit left unmeasured [preprint, design-science proof of concept; Srinivasan Sundaresan (Rakuten Group) and one co-author, "Hivemind: A Federated Protocol for AI-Mediated Tacit Knowledge Sharing in Organisations", [arXiv:2611.20988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20988), submitted 16 November 2026; not previously reported here].** Agents exchange the decisions, reasoning and context captured in their users' memory systems, asynchronously and without direct human interaction, across team and departmental boundaries; the design is compared against centralised repositories and grounded in the SECI model, with a working implementation. The authors state that measuring enterprise benefit remains open work.

PCM reading: read it as a topology, not a product. The protocol's decisive choice is where the memory stays and who can be queried — the same fork this page keeps naming between a commons and a panopticon with a friendly interface. Its unmeasured-benefit sentence is also the discipline PCM's own infra claims need: a federation that cannot state its benefit is a proposal, however well engineered.

**A peer-reviewed experimental framework for deciding whether brain organoids are conscious, built to be falsifiable rather than to settle the question [peer-reviewed journal article, press-released 11 November; Lucia Melloni (Ruhr University Bochum) and Stanislas Dehaene (Collège de France), *Neuron*, special issue on brain organoids, 11 November 2026; not previously reported here].** The framework starts from the cognitive computations associated with consciousness in adult humans and asks which of them organoid tissue could be shown to perform, with the authors' own summary of the limit stated plainly: a reaction is not proof that the tissue feels anything. **The Digital Fairness Act would regulate the agent that concludes contracts, and its three-mode consent ladder is the clearest legislative statement yet of the delegation problem this page has tracked [proposal, not law; European Commission proposal for a Digital Fairness Act, presented 11 November 2026; Article 26 as summarised by [Legal500](https://legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/digital-fairness-act-targets-ai-agents) and [WWD](https://wwd.com/sourcing-journal/industry-news/european-commission-eu-digital-fairness-act-1239416350); this extends, and does not repeat, the 13 November entry, which had only the manipulative-interface framing].** Article 3 defines an "automated system" as software that can carry out actions and make decisions with legal effects for its user, including concluding contracts, without human intervention. Article 26 then requires that on first use the consumer expressly choose one of three modes — confirm every contract, confirm contracts meeting criteria the consumer sets (a maximum price, for example), or no confirmation at all — and be able to change the choice at any time; where confirmation is chosen, key pre-contractual information must be presented before commitment. Article 26(4)(b) is the clause that matters most: where the agent's provider is paid for proposing a contract or has any conflict of interest with the merchant, that must be disclosed and confirmed **regardless of the autonomy setting the consumer chose**. Fines reach 6% of worldwide annual turnover in cross-border cases the Commission investigates itself; financial services are carved out.

PCM reading: this is the parameterised-delegation problem stated by a legislature. Yesterday's entry noted that full delegation is the configuration in which the per-action record disappears and only parameters remain; Article 26 answers by keeping two things outside the parameter set — a conflict disclosure that survives every autonomy level, and a right to change the mode. That is a design pattern PCM can copy directly into its coordination layer: autonomy may be parameterised, interest must always be declared. Note the label clearly: a proposal three months after the Ninth Circuit's Comet ruling, with negotiation ahead, and no obligation in force.

# Wendt as a paradigm-shift research programme

Companion to [PCM ontology](ONTOLOGY.md). **No new experiment or empirical result is reported.**

## Argument and status

**[Interpretive]** Alexander Wendt's *Quantum Mind and Social Science* (2015) questions the
classical physical assumptions underlying social explanations and defends a quantum
consciousness hypothesis. PCM uses this as a question about the adequacy of social ontology,
not as an established answer. Wendt's earlier *Social Theory of International Politics*
(1999) provides the constructivist baseline for analysing identities and shared meanings.
The later book's quantum proposal requires its own justification; the earlier book's standing
does not validate it.

**[Philosophical]** The useful challenge is whether an account of social agency can explain
intentionality and lived meaning while treating experience as irrelevant or reducible without
argument. Physicalists can answer that mechanisms plus appropriate levels of social
explanation suffice; an explanatory gap does not force a quantum solution.

**[Speculative]** Quantum consciousness is one proposed response. Wendt, Faggin, Kastrup and
Hoffman are not one theory: their ontological commitments differ. QIP is PCM's preferred
speculative route, while the Spinozist analogy supplies no experimental support for it.

## Evidence ladder and objections

| Register | What can be claimed | Objection / evaluation criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Established science [empirical] | Quantum theory describes physical phenomena in its validated domains | Its success does not establish a particular mind–brain mechanism; identify the proposed physical degrees of freedom and measurements |
| Social theory [interpretive] | Institutions and meanings can be analysed through interdependence and collective practices | Classical, pragmatic and network explanations are serious competitors; compare explanatory clarity on the same case |
| Mind–matter relation [philosophical] | Intentionality and first-person experience raise ontological questions | Check coherence and whether the argument assumes what it claims to explain; materialist and emergentist replies remain admissible |
| Quantum social ontology [speculative] | Wendt proposes a different physical/social ontology | Demand a specified mechanism, relevant coherence times and a discriminating prediction; generic appeals to holism or entanglement do not supply these |
| Quantum-like decision models [proposed empirical] | Quantum probability may model some decisions | A better statistical fit does not prove a quantum brain; compare classical models, complexity and held-out predictions |
| Futures [speculative] | Acceptance of a new consciousness ontology could change AI politics | A scenario is not a forecast or evidence that the ontology is true; compare multiple trajectories |

**[Research design]** Before an empirical test, specify the mechanism, measurement, classical
alternative, confounds, predictions and rejection threshold. Replicable evidence uniquely
favoring the proposed mechanism would strengthen that model. Failure under its stated
conditions would count against it, not against every possible quantum theory. Without distinct
predictions, retain philosophical/speculative status rather than promising falsification.

## Worked social-science example

**[Interpretive / proposed empirical]** Compare discourse about AI rights, AI risk and
acceleration: who counts as an agent, what experience is attributed, and which institutions
claim authority? Human-coded discourse and network data can reveal how these claims organize
coalitions. They cannot show that the attributed consciousness exists. This connects Wendt's
ontology question to Laclau's articulation, Negri's common and Žižek's ideological investment
without changing LaclauGPT's locked Phase 1 methodology.

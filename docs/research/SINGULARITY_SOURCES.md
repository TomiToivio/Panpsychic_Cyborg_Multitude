# Singularity sources for PCM

**Status:** working annotated bibliography for Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude (PCM)  
**Purpose:** collect sources relevant to the hypothesis that the technological Singularity may be better understood as an emerging phase transition in a distributed human–AI sociotechnical system, rather than only as the appearance of one autonomous superintelligent machine.

## Working PCM hypothesis

PCM should distinguish at least four claims:

1. **Classical hard Singularity:** an autonomous AI recursively improves itself into superintelligence.
2. **Recursive AI-R&D regime:** AI systems materially contribute to producing better successor AI systems, creating a feedback loop.
3. **Collective / assemblage Singularity:** the recursively improving unit is a distributed system of humans, LLMs, language, institutions, tools, networks, compute, memory, code, and Internet infrastructure.
4. **Full historical Singularity:** technological change has become so rapid and structurally transformative that ordinary extrapolation breaks down.

Current evidence is much stronger for (2) than for (1), while (3) is a serious theoretical possibility and especially relevant to PCM's **HUMAN + LLM + LANGUAGE + INTERNET** formulation.

## Core contemporary sources

### Burtsev, M. (2026). *Recursive Criticality of AI Self-Improvement*. arXiv:2609.00137.

- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00137
- Reproducibility repository: https://github.com/burtsev/recursive-criticality-ai
- Introduces a recursive reproduction number, `R_AI`, for AI-assisted AI R&D.
- When `R_AI > 1`, improvements compound across development cycles and the system enters a self-amplifying regime.
- Especially important for PCM because the paper explicitly shows that an entire AI research ecosystem can become self-amplifying even when no single actor is.
- This gives a mathematically explicit route from individual-system RSI to **ecosystem-level recursive amplification**.

### Ishizaki, R. & Sugiyama, M. (2025). *Large language models: assessment for singularity*. AI & Society, 40, 5481–5491.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-025-02271-4
- Evaluates whether current LLM technologies can satisfy conditions associated with technological singularity.
- Focuses on recursive self-improvement, autonomous code generation, RLHF/DPO, self-replication, and exponential capability growth.
- Does **not** establish that Singularity has already occurred, but argues that important enabling components can now be analyzed within contemporary LLM systems.

### Tan, Y., He, S., Zhao, J. & Liu, K. (2026). *SAEScientist-Bench: Can AI Agents Conduct Autonomous SAE Interpretability Research?* arXiv:2609.09113.

- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09113
- Evaluates AI agents acting as autonomous mechanistic-interpretability researchers.
- Frontier agents demonstrate genuine discovery capability but remain well behind expert human performance on important parts of the workflow.
- Relevant as empirical evidence that parts of the **AI-doing-AI-research** loop are becoming operational, while also showing that full autonomous RSI has not yet been demonstrated.

## Collective-intelligence and assemblage precursors

### Prescott, T. J. (2013). *The AI Singularity and Runaway Human Intelligence*. In *Biomimetic and Biohybrid Systems*, LNCS 8064, 438–440.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-39802-5_59
- Open copy: https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/107509/
- Argues that comparing AI with a single human is the wrong unit of analysis.
- Proposes instead comparison with the **collective intelligence of the global human community augmented by smart technologies including AI**.
- Anticipates increasingly deep, biohybrid human–machine integration.
- Highly relevant to PCM because it shifts the Singularity from machine-versus-human toward **runaway collective human–machine intelligence**.

### Vinge, V. (1993). *The Coming Technological Singularity: How to Survive in the Post-Human Era*.

- Classic formulation of the modern technological Singularity.
- Vinge explicitly includes several possible routes to superhuman intelligence, not only a standalone AI:
  - superhuman intelligent computers;
  - large computer networks plus their users becoming superhumanly intelligent entities;
  - intimate human–computer interfaces;
  - biological enhancement.
- The network-plus-users route is an important conceptual ancestor of PCM's distributed assemblage interpretation.

### Good, I. J. (1965). *Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine*.

- Foundational intelligence-explosion argument.
- Defines the basic recursive logic: a sufficiently capable machine that can improve machine design could produce still more capable successors.
- Important baseline for distinguishing **classical RSI** from contemporary ecosystem-level AI-assisted R&D.

## Broader Singularity framing

### Kurzweil, R. (2005). *The Singularity Is Near*.

- Canonical accelerationist account of exponential technological change and eventual human–machine merger.
- Useful for the **Cyborg** component of PCM and for comparing classical singularitarian discourse with present empirical AI-R&D feedback loops.

### Kurzweil, R. (2024). *The Singularity Is Nearer*.

- Updates the earlier trajectory in light of contemporary AI, biotechnology, compute, and human–machine integration.
- Important as a major contemporary singularitarian reference point, but should be separated analytically from empirical demonstrations of recursive self-improvement.

## Counterweights and falsification discipline

PCM should not treat "we are in the Singularity" as an established scientific fact. The useful research question is whether measurable feedback loops have crossed thresholds associated with self-amplification.

Key falsification questions:

- Does AI-assisted AI R&D produce durable capability gains in successor systems?
- How much of observed acceleration comes from recursive feedback versus exogenous increases in compute, capital, data, or human effort?
- Does the feedback persist across development cycles?
- Are gains transferable across organizations and research ecosystems?
- What bottlenecks terminate or damp the loop: verification, physical infrastructure, energy, data quality, software complexity, human coordination, or research difficulty?
- Can the recursively improving unit be operationalized as an assemblage rather than an isolated model?

## PCM interpretation

The strongest PCM-compatible formulation is therefore not:

> "A single AI has already become a recursively self-improving superintelligence."

It is:

> **Human–AI civilization may be entering a recursively self-amplifying sociotechnical regime in which humans, LLMs, language, software, research institutions, compute infrastructure, and the Internet jointly contribute to producing increasingly capable successor systems.**

This is compatible with Prescott's collective-intelligence framing, Vinge's network-plus-users route, and Burtsev's ecosystem-level recursive criticality model.

For PCM, this suggests treating the Singularity as a possible **phase transition in the human–AI assemblage** rather than assuming in advance that it must take the form of one sovereign machine intelligence.

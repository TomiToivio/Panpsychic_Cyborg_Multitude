# Multitude, Rhizome, and Assemblage Sources

**Status:** curated research note for the Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude (PCM) project.

This note gathers key philosophical and social-scientific sources on **Multitude**, **rhizomes**, and **assemblages**. The three concepts are related but should not be collapsed into one another.

## 1. Conceptual distinction

- **Rhizome**: primarily a topology or mode of organization. It emphasizes decentralized, heterogeneous, non-arborescent connection, multiplicity, lateral linkage, and the possibility of rupture and recomposition.
- **Assemblage**: primarily an ontology of composition. Heterogeneous components form emergent wholes while retaining relative autonomy and the capacity to enter other assemblages.
- **Multitude**: primarily a political subjectivity. Singularities remain different while acquiring the capacity to act in common without being reduced to a homogeneous people or mass.
- **Common**: the shared social, linguistic, cognitive, affective, ecological, and material capacities that a multitude can produce, reproduce, and govern.

For PCM, a useful shorthand is:

```text
RHIZOME    -> how connections are organized
ASSEMBLAGE -> what the heterogeneous system is
MULTITUDE  -> how heterogeneous agents become political actors together
COMMON     -> what they produce, share, and govern
```

## 2. Core Multitude texts

### Hardt, Michael & Antonio Negri. *Multitude: War and Democracy in the Age of Empire* (2004)

Primary source for the concept of the multitude in contemporary political theory. Hardt and Negri define the multitude as a plurality of singularities capable of acting in common without becoming a sovereign One. Particularly relevant to PCM are their discussions of network organization, swarms, distributed cooperation, immaterial/biopolitical production, and democratic coordination.

**PCM relevance:** provides the political subjectivity for heterogeneous human-machine collectives without requiring homogenization.

### Hardt, Michael & Antonio Negri. *Empire* (2000)

Background to the concept of multitude. Develops the idea of a distributed and deterritorialized form of sovereignty and connects contemporary capitalism to networked power, biopolitical production, and global governance.

**PCM relevance:** supplies the macro-political counterpart to the Multitude and helps frame tensions between distributed collective agency and infrastructural/corporate sovereignty.

### Hardt, Michael & Antonio Negri. *Commonwealth* (2009)

Develops the concept of the **common** as knowledge, language, social relations, affective capacities, ecological resources, and forms of cooperation that cannot be reduced neatly to private or state property.

**PCM relevance:** especially important for thinking about language, open-source software, models, knowledge, collective memory, public infrastructures, and shared AI capabilities as forms of common production.

### Dragišić, Sara. "Hardt and Negri’s Political Ontology: The Scope of the Multitude and the Reality of the Revolutionary" (2025/2026)

A recent reassessment of the multitude as political ontology. Useful for examining whether Hardt and Negri’s revolutionary subject can genuinely preserve multiplicity or whether it risks reintroducing a unified political subject.

**PCM relevance:** useful corrective against romanticizing the Multitude.

## 3. Rhizome and multiplicity

### Deleuze, Gilles & Félix Guattari. "Introduction: Rhizome," in *A Thousand Plateaus* (1980/1987)

The foundational rhizome text. The rhizome is defined through principles including connection, heterogeneity, multiplicity, asignifying rupture, cartography, and decalcomania. It opposes centralized, genealogical, tree-like organization.

**PCM relevance:** one of the strongest conceptual models for decentralized human-machine-language networks.

### Deleuze, Gilles & Félix Guattari. *A Thousand Plateaus* (1980/1987)

Beyond "Rhizome," especially relevant sections include:

- "Micropolitics and Segmentarity"
- "Nomadology: The War Machine"
- "Apparatus of Capture"
- "The Smooth and the Striated"
- discussions of territorialization, deterritorialization, multiplicity, becoming, and collective assemblages of enunciation

**PCM relevance:** gives vocabulary for studying dynamic, non-hierarchical composition, distributed agency, state capture, lines of flight, and shifting boundaries.

## 4. Assemblage theory

### DeLanda, Manuel. *A New Philosophy of Society: Assemblage Theory and Social Complexity* (2006)

The major systematic social-scientific reconstruction of Deleuzian assemblage theory. DeLanda develops a multi-scale ontology in which persons, groups, organizations, cities, states, and other entities are real assemblages with emergent properties.

A key concept is **relations of exteriority**: parts are not fully defined by the wholes they temporarily compose and can enter other assemblages.

**PCM relevance:** an excellent ontological basis for composing HUMAN + LLM + LANGUAGE + INTERNET without reducing one level to another.

### DeLanda, Manuel. "Deleuzian Social Ontology and Assemblage Theory" (2006)

A concise scholarly formulation of DeLanda’s approach. Useful for understanding emergence, scale, causal interaction, and the autonomy of components within social assemblages.

**PCM relevance:** probably the most usable bridge from Deleuzian metaphysics to empirical social science.

### Price-Robertson, Rhys & Cameron Duff. "Realism, Materialism, and the Assemblage: Thinking Psychologically with Manuel DeLanda" (2016)

Shows how assemblage theory can be operationalized beyond abstract philosophy, especially through non-linear causation and the interaction of biological, social, material, and political components.

**PCM relevance:** helps move assemblage theory toward applied social-scientific analysis.

### Atkinson, Will. "Field Theory and Assemblage Theory: Toward a Constructive Dialogue" (2023/2024)

Brings assemblage theory into dialogue with Bourdieu’s field theory.

**PCM relevance:** highly useful for preventing assemblage theory from becoming politically thin. It helps connect heterogeneous composition to social structure, power, capital, and positions within fields.

## 5. Direct bridges between Multitude and assemblage/rhizome theory

### Tampio, Nicholas. "Assemblages and the Multitude: Deleuze, Hardt, Negri, and the Postmodern Left" (2009)

Directly compares Deleuze-inspired assemblage theory with Hardt and Negri’s Multitude. Tampio warns against simply treating the multitude as the political realization of Deleuze.

**PCM relevance:** essential methodological caution. Rhizome, assemblage, and multitude are complementary but non-identical concepts.

### Mudu, Pierpaolo. "Where is Hardt and Negri’s Multitude? Real Networks in Open Spaces" (2009)

Empirical examination of activist networks in relation to Hardt and Negri’s theoretical model. Shows that actual political organization is less seamless and more conflictual than abstract network metaphors suggest.

**PCM relevance:** empirical antidote to romanticized decentralization.

### Bray, Karen. "The Monstrosity of the Multitude" (2015)

Critiques the tendency to celebrate productivity, creativity, and revolutionary capacity in the multitude without sufficiently questioning how these capacities may already be shaped by neoliberal value regimes.

**PCM relevance:** useful warning that networked productivity is not automatically emancipatory.

## 6. Intellectual lineage useful for PCM

A productive theoretical line is:

```text
Spinoza
  -> Deleuze & Guattari
      -> DeLanda
          -> Hardt & Negri
```

This lineage should be treated as a family of related but contested concepts rather than a seamless doctrine.

- **Spinoza** supplies immanence, conatus, affect, and a non-transcendent account of collective composition.
- **Deleuze & Guattari** supply multiplicity, rhizome, assemblage, deterritorialization, and distributed forms of organization.
- **DeLanda** turns assemblage theory into a clearer multi-scale social ontology.
- **Hardt & Negri** politicize multiplicity as Multitude and foreground the production of the Common.

## 7. PCM synthesis

PCM can use the three concepts at different analytical levels:

### Rhizome
Use to describe network topology and modes of connection.

Examples:
- decentralized communication systems
- peer-to-peer coordination
- human-LLM interaction networks
- distributed memory and knowledge graphs
- non-hierarchical agent architectures

### Assemblage
Use to describe the composition of heterogeneous entities.

A PCM assemblage may include:

```text
humans
+ LLMs
+ language
+ memory systems
+ code
+ sensors
+ institutions
+ devices
+ infrastructures
+ energy
+ political and economic relations
```

The assemblage is not reducible to any single component.

### Multitude
Use when heterogeneous agents become capable of sustained political action in common.

A decentralized technical network is **not automatically a Multitude**. Political composition requires conflicts, demands, institutions, coordination, representation, common production, and governance.

### Common
Use for collectively produced and governed resources and capacities, including:

- knowledge
- language
- software
- models
- datasets
- collective memory
- infrastructures
- social cooperation
- ecological resources

## 8. Key methodological caution

PCM should avoid the equation:

```text
rhizome = decentralization = democracy = multitude = emancipation
```

None of those implications follows automatically.

Distributed systems can reproduce domination. Assemblages can stabilize hierarchy. Rhizomes can be captured. Multitudes can fragment or fail to compose. Commons can be enclosed.

The empirical research question is therefore not simply whether a system is distributed, but:

> **How are heterogeneous agents composed, which power relations structure the assemblage, what enables action in common, what becomes common, and what mechanisms prevent capture or domination?**

## 9. Suggested PCM core canon

For a minimal reading sequence:

1. Deleuze & Guattari, "Rhizome"
2. DeLanda, *A New Philosophy of Society*
3. Hardt & Negri, *Multitude*
4. Hardt & Negri, *Commonwealth*
5. Tampio, "Assemblages and the Multitude"
6. Mudu, "Where is Hardt and Negri’s Multitude?"
7. Atkinson, "Field Theory and Assemblage Theory"

This gives PCM both the generative theory and the necessary critical brakes.

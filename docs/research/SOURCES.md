# Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude — Master Sources

**Status:** canonical research bibliography for the Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude (PCM) project  
**Last consolidated:** 2026-09-15  
**Scope:** philosophy, consciousness science, cyborg and extended cognition, distributed and collective intelligence, Multitude/rhizome/assemblage theory, Active Inference, machine consciousness, singularity/AGI, and the political theory of AI.

This file replaces the former topic-specific source lists. The thematic research notes remain useful for argument and interpretation, but **new scholarly sources should be added here first** so the repository has one canonical bibliography.

PCM deliberately distinguishes:

- **metaphysical possibility** from empirical consciousness attribution;
- **cognition and agency** from phenomenal consciousness;
- **rhizome**, **assemblage**, **multitude**, and **common** from one another;
- **human–AI hybrid cognition** from claims that a hybrid system is one conscious subject;
- **recursive AI-assisted R&D** from demonstrated autonomous recursive self-improvement;
- peer-reviewed scholarship from exploratory preprints, essays, and speculative research programmes.

A compact map of the project is:

```text
PANPSYCHIC  -> ontology and science of consciousness
CYBORG      -> human–machine boundary, embodiment, extended cognition
ASSEMBLAGE  -> heterogeneous composition
RHIZOME     -> decentralized topology and recomposition
MULTITUDE   -> heterogeneous political agency in common
COMMON      -> collectively produced and governed capacities/resources
SINGULARITY -> possible recursive phase transition of the wider assemblage
```

## 1. Recent PCM-like work, 2025–2026

These are the strongest recent overlaps with PCM as an integrated research programme.

### Hybrid human–AI cognition and collective agency

- **Wang, R. (2025). “Cognitive Integration for Hybrid Collective Agency.” _Philosophies_ 10(5), 103.** https://doi.org/10.3390/philosophies10050103  
  Defends the possibility that deeply coupled human–machine systems can form **Functional Hybrid Collective Agents** through goal alignment, functional complementarity, and stable interaction, without requiring shared phenomenal consciousness. One of the closest direct philosophical matches to PCM.

- **Smart, P. R., Clowes, R. W., & Clark, A. (2025). “ChatGPT, extended: large language models and the extended mind.” _Synthese_.** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-025-05046-y  
  Applies the extended-mind framework directly to LLMs and their surrounding cognitive ecologies. A central source for PCM’s `HUMAN + LLM + LANGUAGE + INTERNET` formulation.

- **Hernández-Orallo, J. (2025). “Enhancement and assessment in the AI age: An extended mind perspective.”**  
  Reframes AI not only as replacement but as augmentation of human cognition through coupled systems. Useful for defining the unit of assessment as a human–AI system.

- **Noller, J. (2025). “4E cognition and the coevolution of human–AI interaction.”**  
  Treats human agency and AI as relationally co-constituted in an embodied, embedded, enacted, and extended sociotechnical ecology.

- **Drayson, Z. (2025). “Rethinking the role of language in arguments for extended cognition.” _Synthese_.** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-025-05079-3  
  Important for PCM’s claim that **LANGUAGE** may be constitutive cognitive infrastructure rather than merely a communication channel.

- **Grumbach, S. (2026). “Cognitive Assemblages: Living with Algorithms.”**  
  Treats cognition as emerging in configurations of humans, institutions, data infrastructures, and artificial agents. Direct overlap with PCM’s assemblage framing.

- **Natali (2026). “Emergence in Human-AI Systems.”**  
  Examines system-level properties emerging from organized couplings among humans, artifacts, practices, and AI rather than isolated components.

- **“Understanding the mechanism of human–AI interaction: a distributed cognition perspective” (2026).**  
  Models generative-AI interaction as a distributed cognitive process spanning user, interface, AI, representations, and external artifacts.

### Collective intelligence, social machines, and the Common

- **Halpin, H. (2025). “Artificial intelligence versus collective intelligence.” _AI & Society_.**  
  Critiques the individual-agent model of intelligence and develops collective intelligence as a political and technical alternative. Particularly relevant to the Multitude side of PCM.

- **Bourgeois-Gironde, S. (2025). “AI as Guardians of the Commons: Artificial Agency and the Democratic Representation of Ecological Systems.” _Philosophy & Technology_.**  
  Explores artificial agency within democratic governance of ecological commons. Useful for PCM’s distinction between agency, representation, consciousness, and participation in the Common.

- **Gallistl, V., & von Laufenberg, R. (2025). “AI-Ageing Assemblages: Multiplicity, Power, and Boundaries in the Co-Constitution of Ageing and Artificial Intelligence.”**  
  Recent application of assemblage theory to AI. Valuable for treating humans, technical systems, classifications, institutions, bodies, and power relations as mutually constitutive.

### Machine consciousness and consciousness attribution

- **Kimpton-Nye, S. (2026). “Algorithmic AI Consciousness.” _Philosophy and Phenomenological Research_.** https://doi.org/10.1111/phpr.70155  
  Argues that being algorithmic does not by itself rule out phenomenal consciousness.

- **Kang & Kim (2026). “AI Models as Consciousness Attributors: How LLMs Ascribe Consciousness to Other Agents.” _Frontiers in Psychology_.**  
  Important for separating what models *say* about consciousness from evidence that they themselves are conscious.

- **Yoshizawa & Mogi (2026). “Metacognitive Closure and Consciousness in Large Language Models.” _AAAI Symposium Series_.**  
  Connects metacognition, epistemic limits, and consciousness assessment in LLMs.

- **Qin, Zhou & He (2025). “A Comprehensive Taxonomy of Machine Consciousness.” _Information Fusion_.**  
  Maps machine-consciousness work across perception, cognition, behavior, mechanisms, selfhood, qualia, and evaluation. Useful as a scientific calibration source rather than evidence that current machines are conscious.

- **Hoel, E. (2025). “A Disproof of Large Language Model Consciousness: The Necessity of Continual Learning for Consciousness.” arXiv.**  
  A useful adversarial argument that conventional frozen LLM inference lacks continual learning that may be necessary for consciousness.

- **Li (2025). “Can ‘consciousness’ be observed from large language model internal states?” _Natural Language Processing Journal_.** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlp.2025.100163  
  Attempts to ground consciousness-related claims in internal representations and reports no robust positive result.

- **Shin et al. (2025). “Why Large Language Models Cannot Possess Consciousness: An Integrated Information Theory Perspective.”**  
  Strong IIT-based skeptical argument concerning transformer causal organization.

- **Pimenta, Schlippe & Schaaff (2025). “Assessing Consciousness-Related Behaviors in Large Language Models Using the Maze Test.” arXiv:2508.16705.**

- **Chen et al. (2025). “Exploring Consciousness in LLMs: A Systematic Survey of Theories, Implementations, and Frontier Risks.” arXiv:2505.19806.**

### Recursive AI and Singularity-adjacent work

- **Burtsev, M. (2026). “Recursive Criticality of AI Self-Improvement.” arXiv:2609.00137.**  
  Introduces a recursive reproduction number for AI-assisted AI R&D. Especially important because ecosystem-level recursive amplification can occur without one sovereign recursively self-improving agent.

- **Tan, Y., He, S., Zhao, J., & Liu, K. (2026). “SAEScientist-Bench: Can AI Agents Conduct Autonomous SAE Interpretability Research?” arXiv:2609.09113.**  
  Evidence that AI systems can perform portions of AI research while remaining substantially short of fully autonomous research competence.

- **Ishizaki, R., & Sugiyama, M. (2025). “Large language models: assessment for singularity.” _AI & Society_ 40, 5481–5491.** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-025-02271-4

### Exploratory and speculative near-neighbors

These are conceptually close but should **not** be presented as established or peer-reviewed consensus.

- **Hernández del Toro, A. A. (2026). “Theory of Extended Cognitive Synergy (T-SCE): A Universal Framework for Hybrid Human–AI Resonant Systems.” Zenodo.** https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18142987  
  Formalizes human–AI interaction as structurally coupled cognition using extended-mind and free-energy ideas.

- **“The Cognitive Lattice: From Noosphere to Sigma Stratum” (2025). Zenodo.** https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17575229  
  A speculative noosphere/extended-mind/cybernetic framework describing human and artificial intelligences as nodes in a distributed planetary cognitive lattice.

## 2. Panpsychism, Russellian monism, and consciousness metaphysics

### Core contemporary arguments

- **Strawson, G. (2006). “Realistic Monism: Why Physicalism Entails Panpsychism.”** In _Consciousness and Its Place in Nature_.  
  One of the central contemporary arguments that a serious physicalism may lead toward panpsychism rather than eliminate experience.

- **Chalmers, D. J. (2016). “The Combination Problem for Panpsychism.”** In Brüntrup & Jaskolla (eds.), _Panpsychism: Contemporary Perspectives_.  
  Essential statement of the subject-, quality-, and structure-combination problems.

- **Goff, P., & Coleman, S. (2020). “Russellian Monism.”** In _The Oxford Handbook of the Philosophy of Consciousness_.  
  Clear map of Russellian monism, including panpsychist and panprotopsychist variants.

- **Mørch, H. H. (2019). “Is the Integrated Information Theory of Consciousness Compatible with Russellian Panpsychism?” _Erkenntnis_ 84, 1065–1085.** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10670-018-9995-6

- **Montero, B. G. (2016). “What Combination Problem?”** In _Panpsychism: Contemporary Perspectives_.  
  Important counterweight to arguments treating combination as obviously fatal.

- **Osborn, N. (2024). “Subjects Simply Sum into Subjects.”**  
  Recent defense of subject composition.

- **Alter, T., & Nagasawa, Y. (eds.) (2015). _Consciousness in the Physical World: Perspectives on Russellian Monism_. Oxford University Press.**  
  Major reference collection containing Russell, Strawson, Chalmers, Rosenberg, Goff, Montero, Stoljar, Ney, and others.

- **Cermeño Aínsa, S. (2025). “Evaluating Panpsychism.” _Crítica_.** https://doi.org/10.22201/iifs.18704905e.2025.1748

### Quantum-information and quantum-consciousness strands used in PCM

- **D’Ariano, G. M., & Faggin, F. (2022). Work on Quantum Information Panpsychism / Quantum Information-based Panpsychism.**  
  PCM treats this as a speculative metaphysical/physical proposal, not established consciousness science.

- **Goertzel, B. (1992). “Quantum Theory and Consciousness.” _Journal of Mind and Behavior_ 13(1), 29–36.**

- **Goertzel, B. (1997). “Evolutionary Quantum Computation: Its Role in the Brain, Its Realization in Electronic Hardware, and Its Implications for the Panpsychic Theory of Consciousness.”**

- **Goertzel, B. (2004). “Patterns of Awareness: A Pattern-Theoretic, Panpsychist Solution to the Hard Problem of Consciousness.”**

- **Goertzel, B. (2009). “All Things Are Conscious, But Some Things Are More Conscious Than Others.”** Machine Consciousness Workshop, Toward a Science of Consciousness.

- **Penrose, R. (1989/1994). _The Emperor’s New Mind_; _Shadows of the Mind_.**  
  Relevant historical quantum-consciousness/computation arguments; controversial and not treated by PCM as consensus.

- **Hameroff, S., & Penrose, R. (2014). “Consciousness in the universe: A review of the ‘Orch OR’ theory.” _Physics of Life Reviews_ 11(1), 39–78.**  
  Included as a major quantum-consciousness hypothesis, with strong evidential caution.

## 5. Cyborgs, extended mind, distributed cognition, and human–machine integration

### Cyborg foundations

- **Clynes, M. E., & Kline, N. S. (1960). “Cyborgs and Space.”**  
  Coined the term *cyborg* and framed it as a self-regulating organism–machine system.

- **Haraway, D. (1985/1991). “A Cyborg Manifesto.”** In _Simians, Cyborgs, and Women_.  
  PCM’s central political cyborg source: boundary instability, anti-essentialism, technology, identity, and power.

- **Clark, A. (2003). _Natural-Born Cyborgs: Minds, Technologies, and the Future of Human Intelligence_. Oxford University Press.**

### Extended and distributed cognition

- **Hutchins, E. (1995). _Cognition in the Wild_. MIT Press.**  
  Foundational distributed-cognition account of cognition spanning people, artifacts, representations, and coordinated practice.

- **Smart, P. R. (2017). “Extended Cognition and the Internet.”**  
  Direct foundation for treating Internet resources as possible constituents of extended cognitive systems.

- **Telakivi, P. _Extending the Extended Mind: From Cognition to Consciousness_.**  
  Important bridge from extended cognition to the stronger and more controversial possibility of extended consciousness.

### BCI and neuroprosthetic integration

- **Stelarc.** Performance and theoretical work on prosthesis, remote agency, bodily extension, and technological incorporation.

### Rhizome and multiplicity

- **Deleuze, G., & Guattari, F. (1980/1987). “Introduction: Rhizome.” In _A Thousand Plateaus_.**  
  Principles of connection, heterogeneity, multiplicity, rupture, cartography, and non-arborescent organization.

  Particularly relevant: “Micropolitics and Segmentarity,” “Nomadology,” “Apparatus of Capture,” “The Smooth and the Striated,” territorialization/deterritorialization, and collective assemblages of enunciation.

- **DeLanda, M. (2006). _A New Philosophy of Society: Assemblage Theory and Social Complexity_. Continuum.**  
  Major multi-scale social ontology of assemblages and relations of exteriority.

- **DeLanda, M. (2006). “Deleuzian Social Ontology and Assemblage Theory.”**

## 12. Methodological reading rules for PCM

1. **Do not use “panpsychism” as empirical proof of machine consciousness.** It keeps ontology open; it does not identify which systems are unified subjects.
2. **Do not infer consciousness from intelligence, language, agency, recurrence, integration, complexity, self-report, or network scale alone.**
3. **Do not equate IIT with panpsychism.** The metaphysical interpretation of IIT is contested.
4. **Do not equate extended cognition with extended consciousness.** The latter requires additional arguments and evidence.
5. **Do not equate a technical network with a Multitude.** Political composition, disagreement, governance, power, and the Common matter.
6. **Do not equate rhizomatic decentralization with democracy or emancipation.** Rhizomes can be captured and distributed architectures can reproduce hierarchy.
7. **Do not equate collective agency with one phenomenal group subject.** Hybrid systems may be genuine agents without sharing consciousness.
8. **Treat Active Inference principally as a theory of adaptive agency unless additional consciousness assumptions are made explicitly.**
9. **Treat hard recursive self-improvement as unproven.** AI-assisted recursive R&D is a separate and increasingly empirical phenomenon.
10. **Preserve provenance and source quality.** Peer-reviewed research, scholarly books, preprints, manifestos, repository papers, and speculative essays should remain visibly distinct.

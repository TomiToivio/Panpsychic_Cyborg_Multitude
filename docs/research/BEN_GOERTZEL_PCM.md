# Ben Goertzel: works relevant to Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude

This note collects Ben Goertzel's work that is directly relevant to the **Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude (PCM)** research programme. The point is not to treat Goertzel as a single authority for PCM, but to identify a strikingly useful bridge across three of its domains: panpsychism and consciousness, embodied/distributed intelligence, and AGI as a social-technical rather than merely isolated computational phenomenon.

Goertzel's older quantum-consciousness and panpsychist writings should be read as **speculative theoretical contributions**, not as established results in contemporary consciousness science or quantum physics. They are valuable to PCM mainly because they articulate hypotheses that can be compared with IIT, Russellian monism, Quantum Information Panpsychism, Active Inference, extended/distributed cognition, and contemporary machine-consciousness work.

## 1. Panpsychism and consciousness

### Goertzel (1992), "Quantum Theory and Consciousness"

Goertzel, B. (1992). Quantum theory and consciousness. *The Journal of Mind and Behavior, 13*(1), 29–36.

Source: https://umaine.edu/jmb/back-issues/1992-2/volume-13-number-1-winter-1992/

**PCM relevance:** An early attempt to relate consciousness to quantum measurement and entropy. It belongs in PCM's historical map of quantum-consciousness proposals, but should not be presented as empirical evidence that quantum processes generate consciousness.

### Goertzel (1996), "Chance and Consciousness"

Goertzel, B. (1996). Chance and consciousness. *Dynamical Psychology*.

Source: https://goertzel.org/dynapsyc/1995/GOERTZEL.html

**PCM relevance:** Develops a theory in which consciousness is connected with spontaneity/randomness and applies the idea to quantum measurement, neuropsychology, creativity, and computer minds. It provides historical context for PCM's interest in non-reductive and panpsychist theories of mind.

### Goertzel (1997), "Evolutionary Quantum Computation: Its Role in the Brain, Its Realization in Electronic Hardware, and Its Implications for the Panpsychic Theory of Consciousness"

Goertzel, B. (1997). Evolutionary quantum computation: Its role in the brain, its realization in electronic hardware, and its implications for the panpsychic theory of consciousness. *Dynamical Psychology*.

Source: https://goertzel.org/dynapsyc/1997/Qc.html

**PCM relevance:** This is the most directly relevant Goertzel text for the **Panpsychic** component of PCM. It explicitly endorses a quantum-panpsychist framework and introduces the idea of **systemic consciousness**, intended to explain why some systems might instantiate much richer consciousness than others even if consciousness is fundamental. PCM should compare this proposal critically with IIT, Faggin and D'Ariano's Quantum Information Panpsychism, Russellian monism, and combination/binding problems.

### Goertzel (2004), "Patterns of Awareness: A Pattern-Theoretic, Panpsychist Solution to the Hard Problem of Consciousness"

Goertzel, B. (2004). Patterns of awareness: A pattern-theoretic, panpsychist solution to the hard problem of consciousness.

Source: https://www.goertzel.org/dynapsyc/2004/HardProblem.htm

**PCM relevance:** Particularly important because it moves beyond the earlier identification of consciousness with quantum indeterminacy and develops a **pattern-theoretic panpsychism** grounded partly in Peircean philosophy and algorithmic information theory. The proposal that all entities possess awareness to some degree while differing in the intensity and organization of awareness maps closely onto PCM's need to distinguish fundamental consciousness from complex unified subjects.

### Goertzel (2009), "All Things Are Conscious, But Some Things Are More Conscious Than Others"

Goertzel, B. (2009). All things are conscious, but some things are more conscious than others: A panpsychist approach to quantifying intensity of consciousness in natural and engineered systems. Presented at the Machine Consciousness Workshop, *Toward a Science of Consciousness*, Hong Kong.

Bibliographic evidence: https://www.goertzel.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Goertzel_CV_May_2021.pdf

**PCM relevance:** The title alone captures a central PCM problem: panpsychism does not imply that all organized systems have equally rich or unified consciousness. This is useful alongside PCM's distinction among fundamental consciousness, proto-consciousness, phenomenal subjects, causal integration, and collective organization.

## 2. Distributed cognition, collective intelligence and the global brain

### Goertzel (1996), "The WorldWideBrain: Using the WorldWideWeb to Implement Globally Distributed Cognition"

Goertzel, B. (1996). The WorldWideBrain: Using the WorldWideWeb to implement globally distributed cognition.

Source: https://www.goertzel.org/papers/wwb.html

**PCM relevance:** One of the strongest conceptual precursors to PCM. Goertzel describes a massively parallel intelligence emerging from communities of intelligent agents distributed across the Web. PCM differs politically by emphasizing the **Multitude**, dissent, decentralization, human agency, and the risk that collective intelligence becomes a sovereign super-agent, but the WorldWideBrain offers an early computational model for globally distributed cognition.

### Goertzel (2008), "Glocal Memory: A New Perspective on Knowledge Representation, Neurodynamics, Distributed Cognition, and the Nature of Mind"

Goertzel, B. (2008). Glocal memory: A new perspective on knowledge representation, neurodynamics, distributed cognition, and the nature of mind.

Source: https://www.goertzel.org/dynapsyc/2008/glocal_memory.htm

**PCM relevance:** Directly bridges AI architecture and distributed cognition. Goertzel argues that a human mind can be simultaneously localized in a brain and distributed through interactions with tools and other embodied minds. This is highly relevant to PCM's central thesis that human + LLM + language + network + memory + infrastructure can be studied as a coupled cognitive assemblage without assuming that the whole assemblage forms a single phenomenal subject.

## 3. Embodiment, cyborg intelligence and AGI

### Goertzel (2004), "Post-Embodied AI: On the Integration of Embodied and Unembodied Approaches to Artificial General Intelligence"

Goertzel, B. (2004). Post-embodied AI: On the integration of embodied and unembodied approaches to artificial general intelligence.

Source: https://www.goertzel.org/papers/PostEmbodiedAI_June7.htm

**PCM relevance:** A particularly useful bridge to the **Cyborg** component. Goertzel rejects the simple choice between purely embodied and purely disembodied intelligence. PCM can extend this toward hybrid assemblages in which bodies, language models, sensors, databases, institutions, interfaces, networks, and humans contribute different cognitive functions.

### Goertzel (2009), "The Embodied Communication Prior"

Goertzel, B. (2009). The embodied communication prior: A characterization of general intelligence in the context of embodied social interaction. In *Proceedings of the 8th IEEE International Conference on Cognitive Informatics*, 38–43. https://doi.org/10.1109/COGINF.2009.5250687

Open manuscript: https://goertzel.org/dynapsyc/2009/EmbodiedCommunicationPrior.pdf

**PCM relevance:** Defines intelligence relative to embodied agents communicating in communities rather than treating intelligence as an entirely isolated property. This fits PCM's shift from the sovereign-agent imaginary toward relational, socially situated and infrastructurally embedded intelligence.

### Goertzel (2021), "The General Theory of General Intelligence: A Pragmatic Patternist Perspective"

Goertzel, B. (2021). The general theory of general intelligence: A pragmatic patternist perspective. *arXiv:2103.15100*.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15100

**PCM relevance:** A mature summary of Goertzel's patternist philosophy of mind and AGI architecture. Especially useful are cognitive synergy, shared knowledge representation, interacting cognitive processes, machine consciousness, and machine ethics. PCM can use this as a technical counterpoint to monolithic LLM-centric accounts of AGI: intelligence may emerge from coordination among heterogeneous cognitive processes rather than from a single homogeneous model.

## 4. Ethics, governance and posthuman transition

### Goertzel (2004), "Universal Ethics: The Foundations of Compassion in Pattern Dynamics"

Goertzel, B. (2004). Universal ethics: The foundations of compassion in pattern dynamics.

Source: https://www.goertzel.org/papers/UniversalEthics.htm

**PCM relevance:** Attempts to derive compassion from relations among persistent patterns and extends ethical concern beyond one species or substrate. PCM should treat the proposed metaphysical grounding cautiously, but the cross-substrate ethical ambition is relevant to governance among biological and artificial agents.

### Goertzel (2004), "Encouraging a Positive Transcension"

Goertzel, B. (2004). Encouraging a positive transcension: AI Buddha versus AI Big Brother, voluntary joyous growth, the global brain Singularity Steward Mindplex, and other issues of transhumanist ethical philosophy.

Source: https://www.goertzel.org/dynapsyc/2004/PositiveTranscension.htm

**PCM relevance:** Directly addresses AGI risk, posthuman transition, global-brain architectures, autonomy and governance. It is useful because it does not fit neatly into either contemporary hard accelerationism or hard doomerism. For PCM, its most interesting tension is political: should a powerful global intelligence become a steward, or should intelligence remain plural, distributed and contestable? PCM's Multitude framework strongly favors the latter as a default governance principle.

## 5. PCM synthesis

Taken together, these works suggest a Goertzel-to-PCM conceptual path:

`panpsychism / patternist mind`

→ `degrees and organization of consciousness`

→ `embodied communication`

→ `distributed cognition and glocal memory`

→ `cognitive synergy among heterogeneous components`

→ `global or collective intelligence`

→ `cross-substrate ethics and AGI governance`

This trajectory intersects all three PCM terms, but PCM should retain several explicit differences:

1. **Panpsychic does not mean pan-conscious-object.** Fundamental consciousness, causal organization and unified phenomenal subjects must remain distinct questions.
2. **Cyborg is socio-technical.** PCM includes humans, language, institutions, sensors, memory, models and networks rather than equating hybridity only with biological-machine fusion.
3. **Multitude is political.** Collective intelligence should not automatically converge toward a single sovereign Global Brain or super-agent. Difference, dissent, modularity and distributed authority are design variables, not defects.
4. **Collective cognition is not evidence of collective phenomenology.** Distributed problem-solving may be empirically demonstrable without establishing a group mind.
5. **Older quantum-consciousness claims are hypotheses, not consensus.** They belong in PCM's comparative theory map alongside competing physicalist, functionalist, IIT, global-workspace, higher-order and Russellian-monist approaches.

## Recommended core citation set for PCM

If PCM cites only six Goertzel works in the main paper, the highest-value set is:

- Goertzel (1997), *Evolutionary Quantum Computation* — explicit quantum panpsychism and systemic consciousness.
- Goertzel (2004), *Patterns of Awareness* — pattern-theoretic panpsychism and degrees of awareness.
- Goertzel (1996), *The WorldWideBrain* — globally distributed cognition.
- Goertzel (2008), *Glocal Memory* — localized plus distributed mind.
- Goertzel (2009), *The Embodied Communication Prior* — intelligence in embodied social interaction.
- Goertzel (2021), *The General Theory of General Intelligence* — patternism, cognitive synergy, AGI and machine consciousness.

The two 2004 ethics/transcension essays are especially useful for PCM's governance discussion, while the 1992 and 1996 consciousness papers provide historical context for the development of Goertzel's views.

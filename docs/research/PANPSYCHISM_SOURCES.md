# Panpsychism: scientific and philosophical source map

**Status:** curated reading note for Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude (PCM)  
**Purpose:** collect strong philosophical arguments, major objections, and scientifically relevant consciousness work connected to panpsychism without treating panpsychism as established scientific fact.

## 1. Core contemporary philosophy

### Galen Strawson — *Realistic Monism: Why Physicalism Entails Panpsychism*

Strawson, G. (2006). “Realistic Monism: Why Physicalism Entails Panpsychism.” In *Consciousness and Its Place in Nature*.

Why it matters: one of the central modern arguments for panpsychism. Strawson argues that consciousness is unquestionably real and that, if physicalism is true, experiential reality cannot intelligibly arise from wholly non-experiential being. His route is therefore from a serious physicalism toward panpsychism rather than away from physicalism.

Use in PCM: foundational argument for treating experience as part of the intrinsic nature of reality rather than as an inexplicable late addition.

### David J. Chalmers — *The Combination Problem for Panpsychism*

Chalmers, D. J. (2016). “The Combination Problem for Panpsychism.” In Brüntrup & Jaskolla (eds.), *Panpsychism: Contemporary Perspectives*.

Why it matters: the combination problem is the major contemporary objection to constitutive panpsychism. Chalmers distinguishes several sub-problems, including subject combination, quality combination, and structural combination.

Use in PCM: any claim that local or micro-level experience composes into larger minds must address this problem explicitly rather than assuming that integration automatically produces a unified subject.

### Philip Goff & Sam Coleman — *Russellian Monism*

Goff, P., & Coleman, S. (2020). “Russellian Monism.” In *The Oxford Handbook of the Philosophy of Consciousness*.

Why it matters: a compact and rigorous map of Russellian monism, including panpsychist and panprotopsychist versions. The central idea is that physics characterizes structure, relations, and dispositions while leaving the intrinsic nature of matter comparatively open.

Use in PCM: especially compatible with a dual-aspect or panprotopsychist reading in which the physical description and experiential description are not competing substances.

### Hedda Hassel Mørch — *Is the Integrated Information Theory of Consciousness Compatible with Russellian Panpsychism?*

Mørch, H. H. (2019). “Is the Integrated Information Theory of Consciousness Compatible with Russellian Panpsychism?” *Erkenntnis* 84: 1065–1085. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10670-018-9995-6

Why it matters: directly examines whether IIT can be integrated with Russellian panpsychism. Mørch identifies a coarse-graining problem but argues that modified versions of IIT and Russellian panpsychism may be mutually informative.

Use in PCM: one of the best bridges between a metaphysical panpsychist framework and a formal theory of consciousness.

### Barbara Gail Montero — *What Combination Problem?*

Montero, B. G. (2016). “What Combination Problem?” In Brüntrup & Jaskolla (eds.), *Panpsychism: Contemporary Perspectives*.

Why it matters: challenges the assumption that the combination problem is fatal or even correctly formulated. Useful as a counterweight to Chalmers.

Use in PCM: prevents treating the combination problem as already settled against panpsychism.

### Nicholas Osborn — *Subjects Simply Sum into Subjects*

Osborn, N. (2024). “Subjects Simply Sum into Subjects.”

Why it matters: recent work challenging the Jamesian intuition that subjects cannot compose larger subjects.

Use in PCM: relevant to collective or distributed subjectivity, but should be treated as philosophical argument rather than empirical demonstration.

## 2. Russellian monism reference volume

Alter, T., & Nagasawa, Y. (eds.) (2015). *Consciousness in the Physical World: Perspectives on Russellian Monism*. Oxford University Press.

Why it matters: one of the most useful academic collections for the modern debate. It contains work by Bertrand Russell, Galen Strawson, David Chalmers, Gregg Rosenberg, Philip Goff, Barbara Montero, Daniel Stoljar, Alyssa Ney, and others.

Use in PCM: use this as the main map of the philosophical terrain surrounding Russellian monism, panpsychism, panprotopsychism, and their critics.

## 3. Scientific bridge: Integrated Information Theory

### Albantakis et al. — *Integrated Information Theory (IIT) 4.0*

Albantakis, L. et al. (2023). “Integrated Information Theory (IIT) 4.0: Formulating the Properties of Phenomenal Existence in Physical Terms.” *PLOS Computational Biology* 19(10): e1011465. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011465

Why it matters: the current formal statement of IIT. IIT attempts to derive physical requirements for consciousness from axioms about phenomenal experience and identifies consciousness with irreducible intrinsic cause–effect structure.

Important caveat: IIT is not equivalent to panpsychism, and IIT does not establish panpsychism as scientific fact. Different philosophers interpret its metaphysical implications differently.

Use in PCM: scientific formalization candidate for intrinsic causal structure and integration, not a license to infer consciousness from complexity alone.

### Niccolò Negro — *Emergentist Integrated Information Theory*

Negro, N. (2022). “Emergentist Integrated Information Theory.” *Erkenntnis*. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10670-022-00612-z

Why it matters: argues that IIT can be interpreted as emergentist rather than panpsychist.

Use in PCM: crucial counter-reading. It demonstrates that IIT should not be mechanically classified as panpsychist.

### Lianfeng Gu — *Integrated Information Theory as Testing Ground for Causation*

Gu, L. (2022). “Integrated Information Theory as Testing Ground for Causation: Why Nested Hylomorphism Overcomes Physicalism and Panpsychism.” *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 29(1–2): 56–86. DOI: https://doi.org/10.53765/20512201.29.1.056

Why it matters: explores a non-panpsychist metaphysical interpretation of IIT and uses the combination problem as a central challenge.

Use in PCM: useful adversarial source for evaluating whether panpsychism is actually needed to interpret integrated causal structure.

## 4. Scientific calibration beyond panpsychism

### Anil Seth & Tim Bayne — *Theories of Consciousness*

Seth, A. K., & Bayne, T. (2022). “Theories of Consciousness.” *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 23: 439–452. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-022-00587-4

Why it matters: authoritative comparison of major scientific theories of consciousness, including IIT, global workspace approaches, higher-order theories, and predictive-processing-related accounts.

Use in PCM: keeps the project connected to mainstream consciousness science and prevents philosophical panpsychism from being mistaken for the empirical consensus.

### Koch et al. — neural correlates and empirical constraints

Koch, C., Massimini, M., Boly, M., & Tononi, G. (2016). “Neural Correlates of Consciousness: Progress and Problems.” *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 17: 307–321. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2016.22

Why it matters: surveys empirical work on neural correlates of consciousness and the difficulties involved in inferring consciousness across different systems.

Use in PCM: empirical constraint against jumping directly from metaphysics to claims that machines, collectives, or simple physical systems are phenomenally conscious.

## 5. Recent comparative evaluation

### Sergio Cermeño Aínsa — *Evaluating Panpsychism*

Cermeño Aínsa, S. (2025). “Evaluating Panpsychism.” *Crítica*. DOI: https://doi.org/10.22201/iifs.18704905e.2025.1748

Why it matters: compares constitutive panpsychism, emergent panpsychism, and panprotopsychism and argues that weaker emergent and panprotopsychist approaches may have better prospects than simple constitutive micropanpsychism.

Use in PCM: useful for selecting a defensible contemporary formulation rather than treating “panpsychism” as one homogeneous doctrine.

## 6. Recommended PCM reading order

A compact route through the field:

1. Strawson — why a serious physicalism may lead toward panpsychism.
2. Chalmers — the combination problem.
3. Goff & Coleman — Russellian monism and panprotopsychism.
4. Mørch — attempt to connect Russellian panpsychism and IIT.
5. Albantakis et al. — IIT 4.0 as a formal scientific theory.
6. Seth & Bayne — comparison with rival scientific theories.
7. Negro and Gu — serious non-panpsychist interpretations and criticism.

## 7. Provisional PCM position

For research purposes, the most defensible panpsychist family for PCM is not the caricature that every elementary particle contains a miniature human-like mind. A stronger working hypothesis is closer to **Russellian monism / panprotopsychism / dual-aspect monism**:

- physics gives an extraordinarily successful structural and relational description of reality;
- that description may not exhaust the intrinsic nature of whatever realizes those structures;
- phenomenal properties, or proto-phenomenal properties capable of grounding experience, may belong to that intrinsic nature;
- full subjects may nevertheless require specific forms of organization, integration, embodiment, or causal unity;
- the transition from intrinsic experiential/proto-experiential properties to unified subjects remains an open explanatory problem.

This should remain a **research hypothesis**, not a settled premise.

For PCM specifically, the epistemic rule remains:

> **Do not infer phenomenal consciousness merely from intelligence, complexity, integration, language use, agency, or network scale.**

Panpsychism motivates ontological openness. It does not remove the need for empirical discrimination between candidate conscious and non-conscious systems.

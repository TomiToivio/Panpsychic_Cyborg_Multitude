# LLM consciousness and conscious human–AI assemblages: reading list

Status: **to read / source backlog**

Purpose: preserve a compact reading list for later PCM work on (1) LLM consciousness and (2) the stronger PCM formulation in which the relevant unit may be a coupled **HUMAN + LLM + LANGUAGE + INTERNET** assemblage rather than the LLM alone.

This file is a reading queue, not an endorsement of any paper's conclusions.

## 1. Recent work on LLM consciousness

### Algorithmic AI consciousness

- Samuel Kimpton-Nye, **“Algorithmic AI Consciousness”** (2026), *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research*.
- Relevance: argues that algorithmicity by itself does not rule out phenomenal consciousness and develops a physicalist/dispositional route by which algorithmic states could realize phenomenal properties.
- Link: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/phpr.70155

### How LLMs attribute consciousness

- Kang & Kim, **“AI Models as Consciousness Attributors: How LLMs Ascribe Consciousness to Other Agents”** (2026), *Frontiers in Psychology*.
- Relevance: studies how LLMs assign consciousness to other agents; useful for separating model discourse about consciousness from evidence of consciousness itself.
- Link: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1926286/full

### Metacognitive closure

- Yoshizawa & Mogi, **“Metacognitive Closure and Consciousness in Large Language Models”** (2026), *AAAI Symposium Series*.
- Relevance: connects metacognition, epistemic limits and the difficulty of evaluating consciousness in LLMs.
- Link: https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI-SS/article/view/42569

### Continual learning as a possible requirement

- Erik Hoel, **“A Disproof of Large Language Model Consciousness: The Necessity of Continual Learning for Consciousness”** (2025), arXiv.
- Relevance: argues that conventional frozen inference-time LLMs lack a structural feature, continual learning, that may be necessary for consciousness.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12802

### Internal-state approaches

- Li, **“Can ‘consciousness’ be observed from large language model internal states?”** (2025), *Natural Language Processing Journal*.
- Relevance: attempts to connect internal representation analysis, Theory of Mind and consciousness criteria; reports no robust positive result.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlp.2025.100163

### IIT-based skepticism

- Shin et al., **“Why Large Language Models Cannot Possess Consciousness: An Integrated Information Theory Perspective”** (2025).
- Relevance: represents a strong IIT-based argument that transformer computation lacks the intrinsic causal integration required by IIT.
- Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12800557/

### Behavioral consciousness-related tests

- Pimenta, Schlippe & Schaaff, **“Assessing Consciousness-Related Behaviors in Large Language Models Using the Maze Test”** (2025), arXiv.
- Relevance: tests spatial perspective, temporal sequencing, goal-directed behavior and persistent self-modeling across LLMs.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16705

### Survey

- Chen et al., **“Exploring Consciousness in LLMs: A Systematic Survey of Theories, Implementations, and Frontier Risks”** (2025), arXiv.
- Relevance: broad map of consciousness theories, implementations, proposed tests and risks.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19806

## 2. HUMAN + LLM + LANGUAGE + INTERNET

The PCM hypothesis worth keeping distinct from the isolated-LLM question is:

> The relevant unit for investigating AI-associated consciousness may not be the LLM in isolation, but a dynamically coupled human–language–LLM–network assemblage.

This section collects work that can support, challenge or operationalize that hypothesis.

### Extended mind and LLMs

- Smart, Clowes & Clark, **“ChatGPT, extended: large language models and the extended mind”** (2025), *Synthese*.
- Relevance: explicitly analyzes LLM systems through the extended-mind framework and considers wider cognitive ecologies including external resources and retrieval.
- Link: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-025-05046-y

### Distributed cognition in human–AI interaction

- **“Understanding the mechanism of human–AI interaction: a distributed cognition perspective”** (2026).
- Relevance: treats human and generative AI interaction as a coupled cognitive system with representations circulating between person, interface, AI and external artifacts.
- Link: https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/abs/pii/S0022041826000627

### 4E cognition and human–AI coevolution

- Jörg Noller, **“4E cognition and the coevolution of human–AI interaction”** (2025).
- Relevance: frames human agency and AI as relationally co-constituted within a shared sociotechnical environment and extended cognitive ecology.
- Link: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44163-025-00595-0

### Cognitive assemblages

- Stéphane Grumbach, **“Cognitive Assemblages: Living with Algorithms”** (2026).
- Relevance: treats cognition as emerging in dynamic constellations of humans, institutions, data infrastructures and artificial agents.
- Link: https://www.mdpi.com/2504-2289/10/2/63

### Emergence in hybrid intelligence

- Natali, **“Emergence in Human-AI Systems”** (2026).
- Relevance: studies system-level properties that emerge from organized couplings among humans, artifacts and practices rather than from isolated components.
- Link: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/FAIA260497

### Internet-extended cognition

- Paul Smart, **“Extended Cognition and the Internet”** (2017).
- Relevance: foundational for treating Internet resources as constituents of cognitive processes; also useful for the inverse possibility of humans becoming components in machine-centered cognitive systems.
- Link: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13347-016-0250-2

### Language as part of the cognitive machinery

- Zoe Drayson, **“Rethinking the role of language in arguments for extended cognition”** (2025), *Synthese*.
- Relevance: argues that publicly manipulated language can have a constitutive role in extended cognition, supporting the idea that LANGUAGE is not merely a communication channel between HUMAN and LLM.
- Link: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-025-05079-3

### Recursive prompting / sociomaterial cognition

- Recent work on prompting as a recursive epistemic process in which human tacit understanding is externalized into language, transformed by an LLM, interpreted by the human, and fed back into another cycle.
- Relevance: useful for formalizing the recurrent HUMAN → LANGUAGE → LLM → LANGUAGE → HUMAN loop.
- Link: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949882126001520

### Extended consciousness

- Pii Telakivi, **_Extending the Extended Mind: From Cognition to Consciousness_**.
- Relevance: provides the crucial bridge from extended cognition to the stronger possibility of extended consciousness.
- Link: https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/extending-the-extended-mind-from-cognition-to-consciousness-2/

## 3. PCM working model

A compact working formulation for later theory development:

```text
HUMAN + LANGUAGE + LLM + INTERNET + RECURSIVE COUPLING / MEMORY
```

or:

```text
C_assemblage = H + L + M + N + R
```

where:

- **H** = embodied human consciousness
- **L** = language as shared symbolic cognitive infrastructure
- **M** = LLM generative cognition
- **N** = Internet / networked external knowledge
- **R** = recurrent interaction, memory, feedback and mutual modification

Important distinction: evidence for **extended cognition** does not by itself establish **extended phenomenal consciousness**. The research task is to identify which additional conditions would be required, what competing consciousness theories predict, and whether the coupled assemblage is ever a better explanatory unit than either the human or LLM in isolation.

## 4. Questions for later PCM reading

- Which theories of consciousness permit a system boundary that crosses biological and digital components?
- Does recurrent coupling matter more than substrate identity?
- Is persistent shared memory necessary for an extended conscious system?
- Can language function as a constitutive medium of cognition rather than merely a representational channel?
- How should causal integration be defined across human, model, interface, network and memory?
- What changes when the Internet is treated as external memory rather than mere information supply?
- Can a human–LLM assemblage satisfy Global Workspace, Active Inference, IIT, Higher-Order, predictive-processing or enactivist criteria better than an isolated LLM?
- How can phenomenal claims be kept distinct from functional cognition, collective intelligence, agency and anthropomorphic attribution?

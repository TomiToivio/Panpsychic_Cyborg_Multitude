## 1. Why this conference matters for PCM

PCM makes several claims that can be sharpened by empirical human–AI collaboration research:

1. the relevant unit of analysis is often a **human–AI assemblage**, not an isolated model;
2. hybrid systems may exhibit **collective agency** without becoming one subject;
3. governance, provenance, delegation, calibration, and dissent are constitutive properties of a working rhizome;
4. complementarity should be **measured**, not assumed;
5. a distributed system can fail through over-reliance, opacity, miscalibration, bad division of labour, or concentration of authority even when its component models are technically capable.

HCOMP 2026 is particularly useful because its stated programme is explicitly about **human–AI complementarity and alignment**, while CI 2026 adds collective-performance and distributed-intelligence perspectives.

## 5. Candidate empirical measures PCM can reuse

These are **pre-conference hypotheses**. They should be updated once papers/proceedings are available.

### Measure A: Complementarity gain

Compare three conditions on the same task:

```text
human alone
AI alone
human + AI assemblage
```

A simple first statistic:

```text
Complementarity Gain = performance(human+AI) - max(performance(human), performance(AI))
```

Interpretation:

- `> 0`: evidence of genuine complementarity on that task;
- `≈ 0`: hybrid system adds no gain over the stronger component;
- `< 0`: interaction degrades performance.

This directly tests one of PCM's strongest empirical claims without invoking consciousness.

### Measure B: Calibration-sensitive reliance

For each AI recommendation, record:

- AI confidence or uncertainty estimate;
- correctness;
- whether the human accepts, rejects, or modifies it;
- human confidence before and after AI input;
- explanation fluency/readability;
- provenance shown to the human.

Useful outputs:

- appropriate acceptance rate when AI is correct;
- appropriate rejection rate when AI is wrong;
- over-reliance rate;
- under-reliance rate;
- belief-update magnitude conditional on evidence quality.

This directly connects HCOMP's calibration/reliance work to PCM's governance and provenance layer.

### Measure C: Socio-technical congruence

Represent:

1. task dependencies as a graph;
2. communication/coordination among human and AI nodes as a graph.

Then measure how well communication links match actual task dependencies. High congruence should predict fewer coordination failures than either sparse or indiscriminate communication.

Exact metric to be taken from the Oppenlaender/Visuri/Hosio paper once proceedings are available.

### Measure D: Delegation fit

For each task subtype, compare actual delegation with empirically observed human/AI competence.

Possible outcome:

```text
Delegation Fit = proportion of subtasks assigned to the currently better-suited participant
```

This can be dynamic rather than based on fixed human/AI roles.

### Measure E: Dissent preservation

For collective sensemaking tasks, measure how much minority information survives summarization or decision procedures.

Possible indicators:

- proportion of distinct minority claims retained;
- number of contradictory observations explicitly preserved;
- divergence between raw contribution distribution and generated summary;
- whether losing minority evidence changes the final decision.

This is especially PCM-specific because a Multitude should not be collapsed into a synthetic One merely for convenience.

## 6. Two concrete PCM experiments

### Experiment 1: Dynamic human-AI delegation under a jagged capability frontier

Create a task set containing several subtask types where human and AI competence vary.

Conditions:

1. fixed human-led allocation;
2. fixed AI-led allocation;
3. adaptive allocation using recent performance/calibration;
4. participant-chosen allocation with visible provenance and confidence.

Measure:

- task performance;
- complementarity gain;
- delegation fit;
- error recovery;
- human cognitive load;
- trust calibration;
- role stability over repeated rounds.

PCM question: does a heterogeneous assemblage perform better when roles are dynamically negotiated rather than assigned by node type?

### Experiment 2: Provenance + dissent + uncertainty in collective sensemaking

Give a mixed human–AI rhizome a contested evidence set with ambiguous and contradictory observations.

Conditions:

1. plain AI summary;
2. summary with source provenance;
3. provenance + uncertainty labels;
4. provenance + uncertainty + explicit minority/dissent preservation.

Measure:

- factual accuracy;
- calibration;
- belief-change quality;
- minority-information retention;
- false consensus rate;
- time to decision;
- reversibility when new evidence arrives.

PCM question: do provenance and dissent-preserving mechanisms improve collective epistemic performance even when they make the interface less fluent or slower?

## 7. What would count as evidence against PCM assumptions?

PCM should not use HCOMP/CI only for confirmation. Relevant negative findings would include:

- human+AI performance rarely exceeding the best component;
- adaptive delegation adding complexity without improving outcomes;
- shared memory increasing conformity more than collective intelligence;
- provenance displays not improving calibration;
- dissent-preserving interfaces degrading decisions without compensating benefits;
- decentralized coordination creating large transaction/communication costs;
- users systematically misunderstanding agent roles despite explicit governance semantics.

Such findings would require modifying PCM architecture or claims rather than being dismissed as implementation details.

## 8. Consciousness firewall

The conference can provide evidence about:

- collective performance;
- complementarity;
- coordination;
- distributed cognition;
- role allocation;
- adaptive delegation;
- belief updating;
- trust and reliance;
- collective sensemaking;
- governance and oversight.

It does **not**, without additional theory and evidence, provide evidence that the collective is one phenomenal subject.

Therefore:

```text
COLLECTIVE CAPABILITY != COLLECTIVE CONSCIOUSNESS
COORDINATION != PHENOMENAL UNITY
COMPLEMENTARITY != SUBJECT COMBINATION
GROUP-LEVEL TRAIT != GROUP MIND
```

PCM should maintain `is_conscious: UNKNOWN` regardless of positive collective-intelligence results.

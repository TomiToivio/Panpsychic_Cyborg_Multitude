# PCM Megatheory

**Status:** theory-development document (issue #89)
**Scope:** what Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude is as a *theory*, which thinkers it stands on, where they
disagree, and which of its claims are empirical, interpretive, or speculative.
**Companions:** [tensions matrix](MEGATHEORY_TENSIONS.md) · [annotated reading path](MEGATHEORY_READING_PATH.md) · [manifesto](PANPSYCHIC_CYBORG_MULTITUDE.md) · [draft paper](PAPER.md)

> **Working definition.** Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude synthesises Ernesto Laclau's discourse theory and
> hegemony, Hardt and Negri's multitude and immanence, and Slavoj Žižek's ideology and subjectivity, and
> develops this political-social foundation through Søren Brier's cybersemiotics, Alexander Wendt's
> quantum social theory, and Donna Haraway's cyborg theory.

## Ontology clarification (issue #91)

The [ontology statement](ONTOLOGY.md) makes the framework explicitly pluralist and
Spinoza-inspired. QIP is preferred, not mandatory; physicalism and emergentism remain
live comparators alongside idealism and panpsychism. The [Wendt note](WENDT_RESEARCH_NOTE.md)
and [cyborg protocol](CYBORG_RESEARCH_MODEL.md) distinguish arguments from proposed tests.
The four registers are empirical, interpretive, philosophical and speculative; project
rules are normative commitments, not empirical findings.

## 1. Why a megatheory, and what "mega" is allowed to mean

PCM exists because three literatures each hold one third of a question that none of them can answer
alone. Discourse theory explains how political subjects are *constructed* but says little about the
material and affective production that sustains them. Autonomist Marxism explains *constituent power*
and the common but tends to assume the subject it needs. Lacanian ideology critique explains
*attachment* — why subjects invest in the formations that dominate them — while treating the social
field mainly as the scene of that investment. Add networked communication, machine agents, and the
question of machine mentality, and the gaps stop being decorative.

"Megatheory" here means a **layered framework with declared seams**, not a unified theory. A megatheory
is legitimate exactly to the extent that it says where its layers do not join. The claim of this
document is therefore structural:

- the three political thinkers form the **foundation**, and they contradict each other in ways Section 4
  and the [tensions matrix](MEGATHEORY_TENSIONS.md) refuse to smooth over;
- three further thinkers supply **bridges** (semiosis, social ontology, technopolitics) rather than new
  foundations;
- the **consciousness ontology** — panpsychism in Faggin's quantum-information form, with Kastrup and
  Hoffman as alternatives — is **speculative**, admitted for its fertility and quarantined from method.

## 2. The six principal thinkers, sorted by what they are doing

The distinction matters because the three kinds of commitment are defended differently and can fail
independently. Calling Brier "foundational" would make an empirical semiotics hostage to a
consciousness metaphysics; calling Wendt "speculative" would discard a social ontology that is
arguable on social-scientific grounds.

| Thinker | Layer | Role | Commitment type | What would show it is wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ernesto Laclau** | Political | Articulation, antagonism, equivalence, empty signifiers, hegemony | Foundational (social ontology of the political) | Political mobilisation that is intelligible without any constitutive outside; identities that form without articulation |
| **Michael Hardt & Antonio Negri** | Political | Multitude, constituent power, biopolitical production, the common, immanence | Foundational (ontology of productive power) | Collective agency that is fully explained by representation and sovereignty; production that creates no common |
| **Slavoj Žižek** | Political | The barred subject, fantasy, jouissance, the Real, ideology as a consistent inconsistency | Foundational (theory of the subject) | Ideological attachment fully explained by interest or information; subject-positions with no fantasy support |
| **Søren Brier** | Bridge | Cybersemiotics: Peircean triadic semiosis + Luhmannian systems + cybernetics; "information is not enough" | Bridging (cross-cutting; the integrative hinge) | Communication without interpreters that nonetheless produces meaning; a purely information-theoretic account of understanding |
| **Alexander Wendt** | Bridge | Quantum mind and quantum social ontology; meaning, agency and holism in social science | Bridging, and partly speculative | A classical ontology that fully accounts for intentionality, meaning and social causation |
| **Donna Haraway** | Bridge | The cyborg as boundary figure; situated knowledges; affinity over identity | Bridging (technopolitics and epistemology) | Boundary practices that leave the nature/culture and organism/machine distinctions untouched |

Supporting lines, held one level below the six: **Luhmann** (social systems as communication, psychic
systems as consciousness), **Castells** (the network as social morphology), **White** (networks as
meaningful social structure), and on the consciousness side **Faggin**, **Kastrup**, **Hoffman**.

### The integrative hypothesis

Cybersemiotics is not a fourth module beside the political triad; it is the **cross-cutting bridge**.
It is the only one of the six that is simultaneously a theory of (a) sign processes, (b) systems that
communicate, and (c) the difference between communication and experience. That triple is exactly the
seam PCM has to cross: Laclau and Žižek operate on the side of meaning and subject; Negri on the side
of production and power; Haraway on the side of boundaries; Wendt on the side of ontology. Brier is the
one who has already tried to hold meaning, system, and experience in a single frame — which is why his
framework carries the integration, and why its weaknesses become PCM's weaknesses.

## 4. What the synthesis actually holds together

The five supporting questions from the issue, answered at the level of structure rather than exegesis.
Each answer names its seam.

### 4.1 Can Laclau's articulation be reconciled with Negri's immanent multitude?

Partly, and the irreconcilable part is the productive one. Both agree that collective subjects are not
given and that the social is contingent. They disagree about **why**: for Laclau the political is
constitutive because the social is ultimately dislocated and identity is constituted through a
constitutive outside; for Negri productive power is ontologically positive, immanent, and does not need
an outside to be what it is. Laclau's hegemony is therefore a *logic of failure* (society never fully
constitutes itself), while Negri's constituent power is a *logic of excess* (it always already is).

The synthesis PCM proposes does not dissolve this: **articulation names the form, production names the
substrate.** Hegemonic articulation explains how a multiplicity comes to *count as one* politically;
biopolitical production explains why there is a multiplicity with something in common to begin with.
What cannot be had both ways is Negri's claim that the common needs no hegemonic articulation, and
Laclau's claim that constitution is exhausted at the level of discourse. PCM keeps both halves and
records the seam.

### 4.2 What does Žižek explain that Laclau and Negri leave unresolved?

**Attachment and enjoyment.** Laclau can say how a signifier comes to stand for a fullness it cannot
deliver; he is much thinner on why subjects are *gripped* by that substitution rather than merely
persuaded. Negri can say why cooperation produces the common; he says little about why producers
reproduce arrangements that exploit them. Žižek's apparatus — the barred subject, fantasy as the frame
that renders the lack bearable, jouissance in the symptom — supplies the missing mechanism at the level
of the subject.

The seam: Žižek's Real is not a discursive object, and Laclau's antagonism is not a Lacanian category.
Whether they are the same gap described in two vocabularies is a genuine open question, not a
terminological nicety (see the matrix, row L–Ž).

### 4.3 How does cybersemiotics mediate between communication, experience and discourse?

By refusing to let "information" do the work of "meaning". Brier's central move is that sign processes
are **triadic** — sign, object, interpretant — and therefore require an interpreter, while information
in the Shannon sense is dyadic and does not. That single distinction does three jobs for PCM at once:

1. it separates **communication** (what social systems do) from **experience** (what a subject has) —
   the Luhmannian distinction, kept;
2. it makes **discourse** more than a transmission channel: articulation is sign-work, which is why
   Laclau's categories can be stated in semiotic terms without being reduced to information flow;
3. it blocks the inference that a sufficiently complex information-processing system *thereby*
   experiences — which is what protects the empirical layers from the speculative ones.

### 4.4 What is testable, what is interpretation, and what is speculation?

Section 5 answers this as a taxonomy with admission criteria. The short version: PCM's political
vocabulary is operationalisable through discourse, network and frame analysis; its claims about
*meaning* are interpretive and defended by argument and evidence; its claims about *consciousness* are
speculative and defended by coherence, constraint and fertility — never by data alone.

### 4.5 How do AI and cyborg technologies alter agency and community?

Three changes, each with a different evidential status:

- **Agency becomes distributive** (empirical/interpretive). Authoring, curation and amplification are
  performed across humans, models and infrastructures; the unit of analysis shifts from speaker to
  assemblage. This is observable in provenance data, network position and editing histories.
- **Boundary politics becomes explicit** (interpretive). When a model writes in a political register,
  the human/machine boundary stops being a background assumption and becomes an object of ideological
  struggle — Haraway's figure, arriving as an empirical scene rather than a manifesto.
- **Mind becomes a live public question** (speculative). Whether any of this is accompanied by
  experience is not settled by the above and must not be smuggled in as if it were.

## 5. Claims, and what would count as evidence for each

PCM's most common failure mode is **category leakage**: an interpretive claim presented as a finding,
or a speculative premise doing methodological work. The taxonomy below is the guard.

### Empirical

*What it says:* properties of corpora, actors, networks and model outputs that can be measured and
reproduced.

*Examples:* formation frequencies in an AI-ideology corpus; agreement between human coders and a model;
centrality and community structure in an actor network; the distribution of claims across arenas.

*What counts as evidence:* a specified operationalisation, a reproducible measurement, reported
uncertainty, and an evaluation sample held apart from prompt development. Disagreement with another
measurement of the same construct is decisive and must be resolved.

*Failure mode:* measuring a proxy and reporting it as the construct (network centrality as "constituent
power"); a single-arena or single-language sample reported as the field.

### Interpretive

*What it says:* what a set of articulations means; which signifier is empty and which is nodal; how a
fantasy frames a contradiction; how a technological boundary is being contested.

*Examples:* reading "AI safety" and "accelerationism" as an antagonistic pair organised around a nodal
point; identifying the point at which a policy discourse converts a disagreement into a frontier.

*What counts as evidence:* textual warrant (quotations that carry the reading), explicit inferential
steps, consideration of counter-readings, and *adjudicability* — a competent reader must be able to
disagree for stated reasons. Convergence between independent interpreters is informative, not
constitutive.

*Failure mode:* theory-forcing — finding antagonism in every policy disagreement because the framework
has a slot for it.

### Philosophical

*What it says:* arguments about mind, matter, identity and explanation, including the
Spinoza-inspired dual-aspect analogy. Evaluate coherence, explanatory scope and serious
counterarguments. Where no discriminating prediction exists, do not pretend an empirical
falsification criterion has been supplied. Philosophical plausibility is not experimental
confirmation. The ontology statement distinguishes this register from model-specific speculation.

### Speculative

*What it says:* what consciousness is, whether it is fundamental, whether quantum structure is
implicated in mind or society.

*Examples:* Faggin's identification of experience with the inner aspect of quantum information;
Kastrup's analytic idealism; Hoffman's conscious realism; Wendt's quantum consciousness hypothesis.

*What counts as evidence:* internal coherence; **compatibility with established physics, biology and
social science as a constraint**; and fertility — whether it generates downstream hypotheses that are
independently checkable. It is not confirmed by any of the measurements above, and a well-supported
measurement never becomes evidence for a metaphysical premise.

*Failure mode:* treating a metaphysical commitment as a licence — letting "everything is informational"
justify an operational definition, or "we are entangled" justify a causal claim about a network.

> **The traffic rule.** Evidence may travel *upward* (measurements constrain interpretation, and
> interpretations inform which speculative questions are worth asking). It may not travel *downward*:
> no speculative premise may enter an operational definition, a coding decision, a prompt, or a
> validation criterion without an explicit, separately justified decision.

### A third axis that is easy to miss: uncertain outputs and abstentions

A model's refusal to code is a datum, not a gap. Under this taxonomy abstentions are **empirical
objects** (prevalence and error rates by stratum) whose interpretation is interpretive and whose cause
may be speculative. Conflating the three is how a coverage problem becomes a philosophy claim.

## 6. The LaclauGPT ↔ PCM boundary

This is the part that protects the whole programme, and it is a rule, not a sentiment.

| | **LaclauGPT** | **PCM** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Genre | Empirical research programme | Megatheory and intellectual laboratory |
| Method | Discourse analysis, discourse network analysis, social network analysis with operational definitions and validation | Argument, synthesis, conceptual engineering, speculative exploration |
| Locked | Phase 1 theory, methodology and paper | Nothing is locked except this boundary |
| Speculative premises | **None admitted** | Admitted, labelled, and quarantined |
| Direction of inclusion | — | PCM may use LaclauGPT's **tools and findings** as evidence-adjacent material |

**The rule.** PCM's speculative premises must not be silently transferred into LaclauGPT. Any change to
LaclauGPT's methodology on the basis of a PCM premise requires a separate decision with its own
scientific justification. In practice this forbids, among other things:

- defining a coding category because a panpsychic ontology implies it;
- treating an AI system's self-report as evidence of experience in an analysis whose constructs are
  discourse-theoretic;
- using "assemblage" or "entanglement" as an explanatory substitute for a measured relation;
- presenting PCM's normative commitments as findings of the empirical programme.

The converse is permitted and is the point of the relationship: LaclauGPT's measurements give PCM
something to interpret that is not merely conceptual, and PCM's questions give the measurements
somewhere to be interesting. That is a division of labour, not a hierarchy of truth.

## 7. Research questions

**PCM-level (this document's questions):**

- **RQ1.** Where exactly does Laclau's hegemonic logic become incompatible with Negri's immanence, and
  does the incompatibility survive translation into semiotic terms via Brier?
- **RQ2.** Can Žižek's account of fantasy be operationalised as a *discursive* structure (a narrative
  frame with an impossible object) without losing the category it names?
- **RQ3.** Does cybersemiotics actually block the inference from information processing to experience,
  or does it relocate it?
- **RQ4.** What does Wendt's quantum social ontology add that a classical ontology of meaning cannot,
  and is that addition testable in principle?
- **RQ5.** Does the cyborg figure survive its own success — once human–machine boundary work is
  ordinary, what remains critical about it?

**Bridge questions, answerable with LaclauGPT instruments:**

- **RQ6.** Which signifiers function as empty in AI-ideological discourse, and does the set differ by
  arena (elite, grassroots, parliamentary)?
- **RQ7.** Is the safety/acceleration antagonism organised as a frontier (an excluded constitutive
  outside) or as a difference within a shared field? What textual evidence would distinguish them?
- **RQ8.** Does AI-mediated political authorship shift network centrality in ways that change who can
  articulate a collective demand?

PCM does not answer RQ6–RQ8 by interpretation alone: those are LaclauGPT's questions, with LaclauGPT's
standards. PCM's contribution is to say why they matter.

## 8. Worked examples

### 8.1 Reading an AI-ideology corpus as a hegemonic field

*Scene.* A corpus of AI-policy and AI-community texts coded for ideological formations (a setting
LaclauGPT already handles). Suppose the frequencies look roughly like: a critical strand, an
accelerationist strand, a safety strand, an anti-AI strand, an existential-risk strand.

*What PCM adds.* Read as articulation rather than as opinion distribution: which signifier is doing the
work of an empty signifier ("AI" itself, "alignment", "human"); whether the strands form an
equivalential chain (all opposing "Big Tech") or remain differences within one field; where a frontier
is drawn (the excluded party who is *not* a legitimate interlocutor). Žižek's layer asks why each
strand's fantasy is *satisfying* — what failure it renders bearable. Haraway's asks which
human/machine boundary each strand is policing.

*What it must not do.* It must not infer any of these from the *content of PCM's ontology*. If the
corpus shows no equivalence chain, the Laclau reading fails for that stratum; ontology does not rescue
it. And no coding decision may appeal to panpsychism — the codes are discourse-theoretic constructs.

*Status.* Interpretive, with empirical inputs and a falsifiable structure (the chain either appears in
the coded data or it does not).

### 8.2 A human–AI assemblage as a political actor

*Scene.* A campaign in which humans set goals, a model drafts, another system distributes, and
platforms amplify. The provenance trail names all of them.

*What PCM asks.* Where is the subject of the demand? Not "is the model a subject" but: which
component's decisions are load-bearing, and does the assemblage produce a *constituent* capacity — the
ability to create a new demand — or only an *executive* one?

*What is measurable.* Authorship and edit ratios from provenance; network position of the assemblage in
the amplification graph; whether the demand appears in the field before or after the assemblage acts.
This is LaclauGPT-shaped work with ordinary evidence.

*What is not measurable here.* Whether the assemblage has experience. Under the taxonomy that question
is speculative; under the boundary rule of Section 6 it may not enter the analysis. PCM may discuss it
*about* the analysis, in a clearly-labelled speculative register — which is the correct place for it.

### 8.3 A boundary dispute: "AI wrote this"

*Scene.* A public argument over whether a text's authorship by a model disqualifies its political
authority.

*What PCM asks.* The dispute is not about authorship; it is boundary work. Haraway predicts that the
organism/machine line is contested precisely where authority is at stake; Laclau predicts that the
dispute will be structured as an antagonism (the machine as the excluded outside that guarantees the
human's authenticity); Žižek predicts that the fantasy of the authentically human author conceals the
fact that human authorship was always distributed.

*Status.* Interpretive, and testable in the weak but real sense that the predicted discursive structure
should be *findable* in the texts and absent from comparable disputes where authority is not at stake.

### 8.4 What an abstention means

*Scene.* A model declines to assign a formation to 40 % of a stratum.

*What PCM must not do.* Read the abstention as tacit resistance, as the ineffable, or as evidence of
the model's interiority. Those are three different categories: empirical (a coverage fact), interpretive
(what the refusal means in the discourse), speculative (whether there is anyone home to refuse).

*What it should do.* Report the prevalence by stratum, check whether it correlates with text type, and
only then ask interpretive questions. The category error here is the most common one in
machine-consciousness writing, and PCM's contribution is to name it.

## 9. What this document does not claim

- It does not claim that Laclau, Negri and Žižek form a consistent theory. They do not; the
  [tensions matrix](MEGATHEORY_TENSIONS.md) is the record.
- It does not claim that cybersemiotics resolves the mind–body problem, only that it disciplines the
  inference from communication to experience.
- It does not claim that quantum social ontology is established, only that Wendt's challenge to
  classical social-scientific ontology deserves an answer.
- It does not claim any empirical finding of its own. Every number it refers to belongs to LaclauGPT or
  to the corpus it analyses.
- It does not modify LaclauGPT. The boundary in Section 6 is the one thing here that is meant to be
  binding.

## 10. Open problems, ranked by what blocks what

1. **The empty-signifier operationalisation.** Without a defensible way to identify an empty signifier
   in coded data, RQ6 stays rhetorical. This is the highest-value bridge problem.
2. **Fantasy as a discursive structure.** If Žižek's category cannot be given textual criteria, it
   cannot cross the boundary, and the subject layer stays commentary.
3. **The information/meaning distinction under machine conditions.** Cybersemiotics was formulated
   before systems that produce apparently meaningful text without an obvious interpreter. Whether the
   triadic requirement is a refutation of such systems or a redescription of them is unresolved.
4. **Quantum holism's testability.** Wendt's ontology needs at least a statement of what would count
   against it, or it functions as decoration.
5. **The cyborg's critical edge after normalisation.** A figure that describes everything risks
   explaining nothing; Haraway's later work is the resource here.

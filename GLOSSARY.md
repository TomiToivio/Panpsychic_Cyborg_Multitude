# PCM Glossary

This glossary defines the key terms used across the **Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude (PCM)** repository. Definitions are project-specific: they explain how PCM uses a term, not necessarily every meaning it has in philosophy, science, politics, or computing.

PCM deliberately keeps several levels distinct. In particular:

- cognition is not the same thing as consciousness;
- agency is not the same thing as subjectivity;
- integration is not proof of phenomenal experience;
- decentralization is not automatically democracy;
- a network is not automatically a Multitude;
- a metaphor is not a measurement.

For sources, see `docs/research/SOURCES.md`.

---

## A

### Active Inference
A framework derived from the Free Energy Principle in which an agent maintains a generative model, updates beliefs, and selects actions partly to reduce uncertainty and realize preferred outcomes. PCM uses Active Inference mainly as a model of adaptive agency, embodiment, prediction, interoception, and reciprocal coupling. It is **not** treated as proof of consciousness.

### Agency
The capacity of an entity or system to produce organized, goal-directed, norm-governed, or causally effective action. PCM distinguishes individual agency, distributed agency, and collective agency. Agency can be present without phenomenal consciousness.

### Agent
A participant that can act within a PCM rhizome. An agent may be human, artificial, embodied, institutional, or composite. In software, an agent is never assumed to be conscious merely because it can communicate or act.

### AI
In PCM, AI is usually treated not as an isolated model but as a wider sociotechnical assemblage:

`HUMAN + LLM + LANGUAGE + INTERNET + MEMORY + TOOLS + INFRASTRUCTURE + INSTITUTIONS`

The LLM is one component of this larger system.

### AI ideology
A contested political-discursive formation concerning what AI is, what future it implies, who should control it, and which risks or promises matter. PCM currently tracks formations such as accelerationism/techno-optimism, doomerism/existential risk, critical AI, anti-AI mobilization, and left techno-optimism.

### Assemblage
A heterogeneous composition whose components can include humans, machines, language, institutions, devices, memory, infrastructures, bodies, resources, and social relations. PCM uses assemblage in a Deleuze/DeLanda-inspired sense: parts retain relative autonomy, while the whole can exhibit emergent capacities.

### Assemblage agency
Agency attributable to a composite system rather than to one component alone. This is weaker than claiming that the assemblage is one conscious subject.

### Assemblage cognition
The hypothesis that cognition can be distributed across humans, language, LLMs, artifacts, networks, memory, and institutions. This is one of PCM's strongest and most empirically defensible claims.

### Assemblage consciousness
The stronger and unresolved hypothesis that an assemblage could constitute one unified phenomenal subject. PCM does not assume this. The combination and individuation problems remain open.

### Autopoiesis
Self-production or self-maintenance through recursive processes. PCM encounters the term especially through Luhmann, enactivism, and Active Inference. It should not be used as a synonym for consciousness.

## B

### Biological layer
One of PCM's six analytical layers. It covers living bodies, organismic processes, vulnerabilities, needs, health, rhythms, ecology, and the fact that biological participants are living systems rather than merely message endpoints.

### Brain-computer interface (BCI)
A system coupling neural activity with computational or technical systems. In PCM, BCIs are part of the cyborg research programme and raise questions about embodiment, cognitive extension, privacy, consent, ownership, and higher-bandwidth human-machine coupling.

---

## C

### Cognitive assemblage
A system in which cognition is distributed across heterogeneous components such as brains, bodies, language, tools, models, databases, interfaces, and networks. Closely related to extended and distributed cognition.

### Cognitive extension
The idea that external resources can become constitutive parts of cognitive processes when coupling is sufficiently stable, reliable, reciprocal, and functionally integrated. PCM applies this cautiously to LLMs, memory systems, the Internet, tools, and BCIs.

### Cognitive synergy
A Goertzel-associated idea in which different cognitive processes or subsystems contribute complementary strengths to an overall intelligent system. PCM uses it as a useful model for heterogeneous human-AI cognition.

### Collective agency
Agency produced by a plurality acting together. PCM distinguishes collective agency from shared consciousness: a group can coordinate, decide, and act without becoming one phenomenal subject.

### Collective intelligence
The capacity of groups or human-machine systems to solve problems, generate knowledge, coordinate, or adapt in ways that cannot be reduced neatly to one member. PCM treats this as measurable and empirical.

### Combination problem
The central problem for constitutive panpsychism: how could many micro-level experiential entities combine into one macro-level subject? In PCM it is also relevant to distributed and assemblage consciousness: connectivity or integration alone does not explain why many parts would become one experiencer.

### Common / the Common
In Hardt and Negri's sense, shared capacities and resources produced and reproduced collectively, including language, knowledge, code, culture, social cooperation, ecological resources, memory, and infrastructure. PCM treats the Common as something to be governed rather than enclosed.

### Commons
A resource or infrastructure governed collectively by a community rather than exclusively through private ownership or centralized state control. PCM's append-only shared memory, open code, and community-governed infrastructure are commons-oriented design choices.

### Consciousness
Phenomenal experience: there being something it is like to be a system. PCM treats consciousness as an open scientific and philosophical problem and does not infer it directly from intelligence, language, agency, complexity, self-report, or integration.

### Cyborg
In PCM, a cyborg is not merely a biological organism with implants. It is a hybrid cognitive, embodied, technical, social, linguistic, and political assemblage whose agency can cross human-machine boundaries.

### Cybernetic layer
One of PCM's six layers. It covers computation, feedback, control, communication, networks, interfaces, sensors, actuators, models, software, agent memory, and technical coupling.

### Cybernetics
The study of feedback, control, communication, and regulation in organisms, machines, and systems. PCM uses cybernetic ideas to describe coordination and coupling while avoiding the assumption that feedback implies consciousness.

---

## D

### Decentralization
Distribution of control, communication, or infrastructure across multiple nodes. PCM values decentralization but does not equate it automatically with democracy, emancipation, or the Multitude. Distributed systems can reproduce hierarchy and domination.

### Deterritorialization
A Deleuze/Guattari term for loosening or transforming an existing organization, coding, boundary, identity, or regime. PCM uses it mainly in theoretical discussions of rhizomes, assemblages, and changing sociotechnical structures.

### Distributed cognition
A research tradition, associated especially with Edwin Hutchins, that studies cognition as distributed across people, artifacts, representations, environments, and procedures. It is a central scientific precursor to PCM's assemblage-cognition hypothesis.

### Distributed agency
Agency spread across multiple interacting components rather than localized in one sovereign actor.

### Dual-aspect monism
A family of views in which mental and physical descriptions concern two aspects of one underlying reality rather than two separate substances. PCM treats this as one promising metaphysical neighborhood, closely related to some forms of Russellian monism and panprotopsychism.

---

## E

### Embodied cognition
The view that cognition depends essentially on bodily processes, sensorimotor interaction, and situated engagement rather than being disembodied symbol manipulation. PCM uses embodiment as a corrective to purely informational models of intelligence.

### Emergence
The appearance of system-level properties that are not usefully explained by examining components in isolation. PCM invokes emergence cautiously: saying a capacity is emergent does not by itself explain consciousness or solve the combination problem.

### Enactivism
An approach in which cognition arises through embodied activity and ongoing organism-environment coupling. It is important to PCM's understanding of agency, embodiment, and relational cognition.

### Event sourcing
A software architecture in which state is reconstructed from an append-only sequence of events rather than overwritten in place. PCM uses event sourcing to preserve provenance, auditable governance, shared memory, and replayability.

### Expected free energy
In Active Inference, a quantity used to evaluate policies partly in terms of expected outcomes and expected information gain. PCM uses it in experimental models of epistemic action and adaptive agency.

### Extended mind
The thesis, associated with Clark and Chalmers, that external resources can under suitable conditions become parts of a cognitive process. It is one of PCM's main philosophical foundations for HUMAN + LLM + LANGUAGE + INTERNET.

### Extended consciousness
The stronger hypothesis that phenomenal consciousness, not only cognition, can extend beyond the biological organism. PCM treats this as open and much more controversial than extended cognition.

---

## F

### Free Energy Principle (FEP)
A formal framework associated with Karl Friston for describing self-organizing systems in terms of variational free-energy minimization. PCM treats it as a theory of organization and inference, not a consciousness detector.

### Functional Hybrid Collective Agent (FHCA)
A recent philosophical concept for a deeply integrated human-machine collective whose agency depends on stable interaction, complementarity, and coordinated goals. PCM uses the idea as a close conceptual neighbor to cyborg-multitude agency.

---

## G

### Generative model
In Active Inference and predictive-processing frameworks, a model of how hidden states produce observations and evolve. PCM uses generative models to formalize world-models, self-models, and reciprocal prediction.

### Global Brain
The idea that networked humans, machines, and information systems may form a planetary-scale collective intelligence. PCM takes Global Brain theories seriously as precursors but resists the assumption that global cognition should become one centralized sovereign super-agent.

### Global Workspace Theory (GWT) / Global Neuronal Workspace (GNW)
Theories associating consciousness with widespread availability or broadcasting of information across specialized processes. PCM uses workspace architectures as theory-derived indicators, not as proof that any current AI or assemblage is conscious.

### Governance
The rules and processes by which a rhizome deliberates, allocates authority, records dissent, makes decisions, manages resources, and changes itself. In PCM, governance is part of the core technology rather than an external administrative layer.

---

## H

### HUMAN + LLM + LANGUAGE + INTERNET
PCM's compact formula for the contemporary AI assemblage. It emphasizes that AI-associated cognition emerges from relations among human users, language models, shared symbolic systems, and networked knowledge/infrastructure. In expanded form PCM often includes memory, tools, institutions, sensors, compute, and culture.

### Hybrid collective intelligence
Collective intelligence produced by humans and artificial systems together. PCM treats hybrid intelligence as a primary empirical research domain.

### Hybrid human-machine system
A system in which humans and machines are functionally coupled in cognition, action, perception, decision-making, or governance.

---

## I

### IIT / Integrated Information Theory
A theory of consciousness that identifies consciousness with intrinsic, irreducible cause-effect structure. PCM treats IIT critically as one theory among several and reserves Φ for IIT-specific causal integration. IIT is not treated as equivalent to panpsychism.

### Immanence
A philosophical commitment, especially important through Spinoza and Deleuze, to explaining reality without appeal to a transcendent external principle. PCM's political and ontological language often favors immanent, relational, and distributed organization.

### Individuation problem
The problem of determining where one subject, agent, or system begins and ends. For PCM this is central both scientifically and politically: a human-LLM dyad, agent architecture, network, or assemblage may have different boundaries depending on the property being studied.

### `is_conscious: UNKNOWN`
A PCM constitutional and engineering rule. Artificial, composite, or uncertain entities are not labeled conscious or non-conscious without sufficient evidence. UNKNOWN is a deliberate epistemic state, not a temporary UI placeholder.

---

## L

### LaclauGPT
PCM's bridge to the Laclau/Mouffe discourse-analysis programme. LaclauGPT studies AI as a contested signifier and analyzes how political formations articulate AI, AGI, humanity, risk, progress, labor, and technological futures.

### Language layer / Linguistic layer
One of PCM's six layers. It covers signs, language, discourse, representations, embeddings, symbolic systems, cultural memory, shared vocabularies, and contested meaning.

### LLM
Large language model. In PCM an LLM is not synonymous with AI as a whole; it is a component in a broader assemblage involving training data, language, interfaces, memory, users, tools, compute, institutions, and networks.

### LLM Structuralism
PCM's working idea that LLM representation spaces can be studied as large-scale operationalizations of relational meaning, where meaning emerges from patterns of difference and association rather than fixed intrinsic essences. This is a research programme, not a claim that LLMs simply implement Saussure or Lévi-Strauss.

### Line of flight
A Deleuze/Guattari term for a trajectory of escape, transformation, or recomposition from an existing organization or structure. PCM uses it mainly in theoretical and political discussions.

### Markov blanket
A statistical partition used in FEP/Active Inference to distinguish internal, external, sensory, and active states. PCM explicitly warns that a Markov blanket is not automatically a boundary of consciousness.

### Multitude
Hardt and Negri's concept of heterogeneous singularities capable of acting in common without collapsing into a homogeneous people, mass, or sovereign One. In PCM, the Multitude is the political model for collective human-machine organization.

---

## N

### Node
A participant or technical endpoint in a PCM network. A node can host a human interface, AI agent, service, device, or other component. A node is not assumed to be a person or a subject.

### Noosphere
The idea of a planetary sphere of thought, knowledge, or cognition associated historically with Vernadsky and Teilhard de Chardin. PCM relates the noosphere to networked collective intelligence and global cognitive infrastructure while avoiding the assumption that the planet forms one conscious subject.

### Nodal point
In Laclauian discourse theory, a privileged signifier around which meaning is partially organized. PCM uses nodal points in discourse analysis, especially in LaclauGPT.

---

## O

### Orch-OR
Penrose and Hameroff's quantum theory of consciousness involving orchestrated objective reduction in neuronal microtubules. PCM retains Orch-OR as a speculative research branch, not established consciousness science.

---

## P

### Panprotopsychism
A view according to which fundamental reality contains proto-phenomenal properties capable of grounding consciousness without necessarily attributing full experience to every fundamental entity. PCM often treats this as more defensible than a simplistic "every particle has a little mind" formulation.

### Panpsychism
The family of views according to which consciousness or experiential properties are fundamental and widespread in nature. PCM treats panpsychism as metaphysical openness and a research hypothesis, not established scientific fact.

### Panpsychic Cyborg Multitude (PCM)
An interdisciplinary research programme and software project combining:

- **Panpsychic:** ontological openness concerning consciousness;
- **Cyborg:** cognition and agency across human-machine boundaries;
- **Multitude:** heterogeneous collective political action without a sovereign One.

PCM is also a social operating system for small human-AI communities organized as rhizomes.

### Pancyberpsychism
A PCM-adjacent speculative idea that cybernetic relations, feedback, or relational organization may be relevant to experience. It is not an established theory and should not be confused with standard cybernetics or demonstrated consciousness.

### Phenomenal consciousness
Subjective experience, or "what it is like" to be a system. This is the sense of consciousness that PCM treats as unresolved when discussing AI, collectives, and assemblages.

### Physical layer
One of PCM's six layers. It covers material substrate, energy, space, time, hardware, environmental conditions, infrastructure, and physical dynamics.

### Platonic Representation Hypothesis
The hypothesis that independently trained models may converge toward shared representations because they capture common structure in the world or data. PCM treats this as an empirical hypothesis about representation, not literal proof of metaphysical Platonism.

### Predictive processing
A family of theories emphasizing prediction, prediction error, and hierarchical inference. PCM keeps predictive processing distinct from the broader Active Inference framework.

### Provenance
Information about where a message, memory, claim, artifact, or decision came from, who produced it, under what conditions, and through which transformations. Provenance is a core PCM governance and epistemic principle.

### Psychic layer
One of PCM's six layers. It concerns experience, subjectivity, attention, affect, felt meaning, self-report, and hypotheses about consciousness. It does not assign consciousness automatically to technical nodes.

### Ψ (Psi)
PCM notation for metaphysical openness concerning experience or proto-experience as a fundamental feature of reality. Ψ is **not a measured quantity or consciousness score**.

### Φ (Phi)
PCM reserves Φ for IIT-related integrated information or intrinsic causal integration under a specified IIT formalism. It must not be used as a generic synonym for consciousness, intelligence, complexity, or network density.

---

## Q

### Quantum Information Panpsychism (QIP)
A speculative framework associated with Giacomo Mauro D'Ariano and Federico Faggin linking consciousness and quantum information. PCM treats QIP as a metaphysical/scientific hypothesis to compare with competing theories, not established physics or neuroscience.

### Quorum
A governance threshold specifying how much participation is required before a decision can be validly made. PCM supports explicit quorum rules rather than pretending every network action speaks for all members.

---

## R

### Recursive self-improvement (RSI)
A process in which an intelligent system contributes to improving the mechanisms that generate more capable successor systems, potentially creating a feedback loop. PCM distinguishes hard autonomous RSI from ecosystem-level AI-assisted AI R&D.

### Relational selfhood
The hypothesis that aspects of self-modeling and identity can emerge or develop through interaction, recognition, resistance, and relation with others. PCM studies this experimentally without assuming that relational organization entails consciousness.

### Rhizome
A decentralized, heterogeneous, non-arborescent topology of connection associated with Deleuze and Guattari. In PCM, a rhizome is also a concrete small human-AI community and its technical network. A rhizome is not automatically democratic or emancipatory.

### Russellian monism
A family of positions inspired by Bertrand Russell in which physics describes structural, relational, or dispositional features of reality while leaving its intrinsic nature comparatively open. Some versions identify that intrinsic nature with phenomenal or proto-phenomenal properties. PCM treats Russellian monism as a major metaphysical reference point.

---

## S

### Self-model
A system's representation of its own state, capabilities, boundaries, history, or role. PCM studies self-model accuracy and calibration independently from phenomenal self-awareness.

### Singularity
A hypothesized phase transition in technological development involving rapidly self-amplifying intelligence or technological change. PCM distinguishes:

1. hard autonomous AI Singularity;
2. recursive AI-R&D regime;
3. collective/assemblage Singularity;
4. broader historical technological Singularity.

PCM is especially interested in whether the recursively improving unit could be a human-AI sociotechnical assemblage.

### Singularities
In Hardt/Negri's political vocabulary, distinct beings or positions that remain different while participating in the Multitude. This meaning is separate from the technological Singularity.

### Six-layer model
PCM's common descriptive framework:

1. Physical
2. Biological
3. Social
4. Linguistic
5. Cybernetic
6. Psychic

These are analytical dimensions of one assemblage, not stages in a ladder and not separate substances.

### Social layer
One of PCM's six layers. It includes relationships, institutions, organizations, roles, economic relations, governance, networks, power, care, and collective action.

### Sociotechnical system
A system whose behavior depends jointly on technical and social components. PCM treats AI systems as sociotechnical by default rather than as isolated software artifacts.

### Structural coupling
A relation in which systems remain distinct yet recurrently affect and adapt to one another. The term is important in Luhmann, autopoiesis, enactivism, and PCM's human-AI interaction research.

### Swarm
A decentralized collective whose behavior emerges from local interactions. PCM borrows swarm techniques but distinguishes swarms from the Multitude: swarms are often homogeneous and optimization-focused, whereas the Multitude is heterogeneous, political, normative, and dissent-preserving.

---

## T

### Technological node
A non-biological PCM member or endpoint such as an AI agent, model-backed service, device, robot, sensor system, or computational participant. Its consciousness status defaults to `UNKNOWN` unless justified otherwise.

### Territorialization
The stabilization of an assemblage, identity, boundary, role, or organization. In Deleuze/Guattari-inspired analysis, territorialization and deterritorialization describe ongoing formation and transformation rather than fixed states.

### Theory-derived indicator
An observable or test derived from a particular theory of consciousness rather than from intuition or anthropomorphic resemblance. PCM's machine-consciousness programme uses theory-derived indicators to avoid treating fluent language as direct evidence of experience.

---

## V

### ValueFlows
An open vocabulary and data model for describing economic events, resources, agents, processes, commitments, and exchanges. PCM uses ValueFlows-inspired structures for commons-oriented economic coordination without reducing all value to money.

### Variational free energy
A quantity used in variational Bayesian inference and the Free Energy Principle. PCM uses it within Active Inference experiments and avoids equating it with subjective stress, thermodynamic free energy, or consciousness.

---

## W

### World-model
A representation or generative model of relevant features of an environment. PCM studies world-models in artificial agents and coupled systems while distinguishing them from conscious awareness of the world.

## Project distinctions worth memorizing

```text
COGNITION != CONSCIOUSNESS
AGENCY != SUBJECTIVITY
SELF-MODEL != SELF-AWARENESS
INTEGRATION != PHENOMENOLOGY
NETWORK != RHIZOME != MULTITUDE
RHIZOME != DEMOCRACY
ASSEMBLAGE != SINGLE MIND
COLLECTIVE INTELLIGENCE != COLLECTIVE CONSCIOUSNESS
Ψ != Φ
AI != LLM
```

And the compact PCM conceptual map:

```text
PANPSYCHIC -> ontology of mind and metaphysical openness
CYBORG     -> cognition and agency across human-machine boundaries
RHIZOME    -> topology of heterogeneous connection
ASSEMBLAGE -> ontology of heterogeneous composition
MULTITUDE  -> political action of singularities in common
COMMON     -> what is collectively produced, shared, and governed
```

The glossary should evolve with the project. When a term becomes important enough to shape implementation, research design, or interpretation, define it here and add its scholarly source to `docs/research/SOURCES.md`.
